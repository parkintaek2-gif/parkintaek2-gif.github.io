Korean esports players in our Southeast Asian panel all peak in the same month. The obvious next question is whether that is a fact about esports or a fact about Korean players, and there is only one way to find out: measure someone else's players the same way.

We did. It did not work, and why it did not work turned out to be the more interesting number.

| Country | Esports players on Wikidata | With an article in these four editions | Read enough to measure | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| South Korea | 1,266 | 14 | 10 | 71.4% |
| United States | 470 | 14 | 1 | 7.1% |
| Sweden | 105 | 12 | 1 | 8.3% |
| China | 161 | 7 | 1 | 14.3% |
| Vietnam | 18 | 4 | 4 | 100% |

Taken together, the American, Swedish and Chinese players come to **9.1%** read enough to measure, against **71.4%** of the Korean ones.

Read the third and fourth columns against each other. American players have exactly as many articles in the Indonesian, Vietnamese, Thai and Malay Wikipedias as Korean players do — fourteen each. Swedish players have twelve. The encyclopaedias have written them up.

Then almost nobody opens them. One American player out of fourteen drew 300 reads across those four editions in a year. One Swede out of twelve. One Chinese player out of seven. Ten of the fourteen Koreans did.

## What that costs the original question

To say the November pattern belongs to esports rather than to Korea, we would need a second national group large enough to have a peak month at all. Setting the bar at three measurable players — low enough to be generous, high enough that one person cannot decide the answer — exactly two groups clear it. One is Korea. The other is Vietnam, whose four players are read by their own countrymen, which is a different phenomenon rather than a control.

So the question stays open. This piece does not answer it, and the honest reason is that the comparison group is not on the page. That absence is measurable, and it is the finding here.

## The share is not the same as the earlier one

Our earlier piece found every esports player in its panel peaking in the same month. This one puts the figure at 5 of 7. Both are true of the group each describes.

The earlier panel was the twenty most-read Korean athletes in these editions — the top of the distribution. This one starts from Wikidata and reaches further down. The pattern is strongest among the players who are read most, and it weakens as you go down. Neither number corrects the other; they describe different depths of the same list.

## What this cannot say

This is what Southeast Asian Wikipedia readers looked up. It says nothing about how these players are read at home, and a Swedish player unread in Indonesian may be very well read in Swedish. The claim here is narrow: on these four editions, in this year, there was no second group to compare Korea against.

It also cannot say why. An encyclopaedia article being written and an encyclopaedia article being opened are different acts by different people, and the gap between them is what this table measures.
