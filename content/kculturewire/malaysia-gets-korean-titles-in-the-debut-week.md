A Korean title turns up on some country's Netflix top 10 for the first time. Call that week its
debut. How long does every other market take to carry the same title?

**Malaysia is in the debut week 71.9% of the time. Britain manages 32.6%.**

## The obvious measure cannot see this

Our first cut used the median lag, which is what you would reach for. It was almost useless: nearly
every market's median is **zero or one week**, because a Korean title usually enters a lot of charts
in the same week. A median cannot separate 92 markets that mostly sit at zero.

What separates them is **how often** a market is in that first week at all. That is the column
below, and it spreads from 72% to 25%.

## Fastest markets, excluding Korea

| Market | In the debut week | Within four weeks | Titles |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Malaysia | **71.9%** | 89.2% | 249 |
| Indonesia | 71.6% | 88.3% | 264 |
| Thailand | 70.8% | 84.6% | 267 |
| Vietnam | 68.8% | 88.0% | 250 |
| Singapore | 67.3% | 87.8% | 245 |
| Taiwan | 65.9% | 88.0% | 249 |
| Philippines | 64.5% | 87.5% | 248 |
| Hong Kong | 60.9% | 83.4% | 235 |
| Slovakia | 60.3% | 82.8% | 58 |
| Czech Republic | 57.8% | 84.4% | 64 |

The first eight are the Asian bloc, and they are the same eight markets that turn up at the top of
every concentration measure we run. Slovakia and the Czech Republic are next, on a quarter as many
titles.

**Korea itself is at 87%**, on 575 titles, and we left it out of that table on purpose: for most of
these titles Korea *is* the debut week, so ranking it would be scoring a market against a line it
drew itself.

## Slowest

| Market | In the debut week | Median lag | Titles |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| United Kingdom | 32.6% | 3.5 weeks | 46 |
| Egypt | 25.2% | 1 week | 107 |
| Iceland | 26.2% | 1 week | 42 |
| Lithuania | 29.3% | 1 week | 41 |
| Slovenia | 30.4% | 1 week | 46 |
| Malta | 32.1% | 1 week | 53 |

Britain is the only market in the set with a median lag above two weeks, and it is also one of the
thinnest: **46 Korean titles in five years**, against Malaysia's 249.

That combination is the explanation, and it is not about British viewers being slow. In a market
where few Korean titles chart at all, the ones that do get there tend to be the ones that built up
over weeks — a title that arrives everywhere at once and charts in Britain immediately is exactly
the case Britain rarely has.

## What this number contains that we cannot remove

**Netflix decides when a title is available in each market.** If a series goes live in Southeast
Asia on the same day as in Korea and in Britain a fortnight later, the lag we measured is the
release calendar and nothing else. We cannot separate the two from these files, and we are not going
to pretend the number is about audiences.

What it does establish is narrower and still worth having: **the first week of a Korean title's life
on the Netflix charts happens in the same eight markets, over and over.** Whatever produces that —
licensing, scheduling, or people — it is stable across 250 titles and five years, and nobody else is
counting it.
