Yesterday we counted how Southeast Asia looks up a Korean trip, month by month. Going back to the same numbers with a longer ruler — twelve months against the twelve before them — produced a figure we had not seen: reads of Korean travel articles in the Indonesian, Vietnamese, Thai and Malay Wikipedias were down about 30%.

That is a headline. **Interest in Korea is cooling.** We nearly wrote it.

It would have been wrong, and the thing that stopped us was not caution. It was a second set of numbers we did not have yet.

## The control group

A number falling tells you nothing on its own. It has to fall against something. So we fetched a matched set: the same kinds of articles about Japan and Taiwan, in the same four editions, over the same months. Airport against airport, high-speed rail against high-speed rail, transit card against transit card. Countries foreign to all four editions, and comparable to Korea as places a Southeast Asian traveller might go.

Then we added a second axis. Not travelling to Korea — Korea itself: the country's own article, its pop music, its dramas, its cinema, its language, its alphabet.

| Articles about | Korea | Japan and Taiwan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Travelling there | −30.4% | −16.9% |
| The place and its culture | −11.8% | **+6.8%** |

Reads are per million reads of that edition, so an encyclopaedia shrinking overall is already divided out of every cell.

The row that matters is the second one. Culture articles about Japan and Taiwan did not fall. They rose. Whatever emptied the travel articles did not touch them.

## The comparison we threw away

Korea fell further than the controls in both rows, and that is the comparison a reader will reach for. We are not making it, because our own check took it away.

Korea's articles also start higher, and an article that starts higher has further to fall. So we kept only the pairs where the two sides began within a factor of two of each other and measured again. On the travel axis the gap went from 13.4 points to 8.1. On the culture axis it went from 18.6 points to 5.7 — and the count of pairs where Korea still fell further landed near even.

A gap that survives only until both sides start from the same place *was* the starting place. So nothing below rests on it.

## What is left standing is inside Korea's own pages

Both of these rows are Korean articles, in the same four editions, over the same months. No other country is involved, so nothing hangs on how Korea compares to Japan.

| Korean articles about | Where they started | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Travelling there | 57.7 per million | −31.0% |
| The place and its culture | 589.3 per million | −14.1% |

These two percentages differ slightly from the ones in the first table, and the reason is worth a sentence. In the first table each Korean article is counted once per control it was paired against, because that table is about the pairings. Here each Korean article is counted once per edition, full stop. Same reads, different denominator.

The culture pages sit about ten times higher and fell about seventeen points less. Height makes a steep fall easier, not harder — so for once the confound pushes the wrong way, and the gap survives it.

## Meanwhile the aeroplanes

Over exactly the same twenty-four months, passengers on the Asia routes into and out of Korea moved −0.7%. Japan routes were up 13.5%, China up 21.2%.

Nobody stopped going. They stopped opening an encyclopaedia article about the visa rule first.

That is the shape of it. Visa policy, airport transfers, what a transit card is — questions with one settled answer, the kind that have been draining out of encyclopaedias everywhere as the answer arrives some other way. The Korean pages that held up are the ones you open because you want to, not because you need a fact before Tuesday.

## The same table, two rulers

One line in this story exists only because of how we measured, and we would rather show it than bury it.

| Route region | One month | Twelve months |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Oceania | −25.1% | +2.1% |
| Middle East | −31.1% | −13.6% |
| Asia (excl. Japan, China) | −9.6% | −0.7% |

Oceania is the clean case. Read June against last June, it is down a quarter. Read the year against the year before it, it is up. Same table, same source, opposite direction — and the shorter ruler is the one that would have made the better headline. That is exactly why we do not use it.

## What this cannot tell you

The KOSIS air table has no Southeast Asia row. Its Asia line puts Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia together with India and Central Asia. It is the closest ruler we have to the four editions we read, and it is not the same ruler.

These reads are not travellers. Some are homework, some are curiosity, and some never become a trip. A read can date when the looking happened, not whether anyone went.

The pairings are article-kind against edition, so the same article appears in several of them. They are not independent observations and we do not treat them as a sample.

And we cannot tell you why the culture pages held. We can tell you that the fall was not about Korea, was not about the encyclopaedia shrinking, and was not about where the articles started — because we checked all three, and each check took something away from us.
