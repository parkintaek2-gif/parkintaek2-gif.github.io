**Choi Baek-ho** was the 13th most-read article on Korean Wikipedia on 30 August 2026 — **3,454 opens in a day**.

He has no English Wikipedia article.

Neither does **Lee Yong-joo**, the actor, who was 10th with 4,044. Neither does the comedian who shares his name, who was 12th with 3,930. Nor **Park Wi**, the YouTuber, 22nd with 2,523.

## One in three

We took Korean Wikipedia's 300 most-read articles for that day, resolved 294 of them to Wikidata items, and kept the ones that are people with South Korean citizenship: **113 people**. Of those, **75 have an entertainment occupation recorded** — actor, singer, comedian, director, presenter, YouTuber.

**26 of the 75 have no English Wikipedia article. That is 34.7%.**

Two names in that list are the same two words — **Lee Yong-joo** the actor at rank 10 and **Lee Yong-joo** the comedian at rank 12, two different people reading at nearly the same volume on the same day. Korean Wikipedia distinguishes them with a parenthesis. English Wikipedia has neither.

## What the singers have in common

Read down the occupations and the pattern is hard to miss: **19 of the 26 are singers**, and several of the names — Choi Baek-ho, Jo Myung-seob, Park Seo-jin, Kim Yong-bin — belong to a genre with a large domestic audience and almost no English-language encyclopedia footprint.

By contrast, every Korean name that reaches an English-language audience through Netflix or a global chart has an English article. **Song Kang** was 14th on the Korean list that day, with 3,369 reads — 85 fewer than Choi Baek-ho — and he has one.

Two people, 85 reads apart in Korea, on opposite sides of a line in English.

## Why this is the measurement, not the opinion

It would be easy to write this up as a story about what the English-speaking world overlooks. **We are not going to, because we did not measure that.** What we measured is which articles exist. An encyclopedia article is written by whoever turns up to write it, and its absence records who has been writing, not who deserves attention.

What the count does give you is a **denominator**: on one ordinary Sunday, a third of the entertainers Korea was reading about could not be read about in English at all. That number is checkable, it moves daily, and as far as we can tell nobody else is publishing it.

We will keep taking it.
