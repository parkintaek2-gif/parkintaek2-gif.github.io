We publish two counts that describe the same industry: how much Korean content the world watches, and how many people look up the performers in it. They do not have the same shape.

## Viewing is a spike

Across 265 weeks of Netflix's global chart, 294 Korean titles have drawn 27.7 billion hours. *Squid Game* alone accounts for **18.2%** of that. The ten biggest titles account for 36.4%.

## Attention is a slope

Now the people. Over 30 days we measured English Wikipedia pageviews for **1,008 Korean performers** who appear in charting titles. They drew 7,463,168 views between them.

The most looked-up performer, So Ji-sub, drew 233,542 — **3.1%** of the total.

| | Share of all measured attention |
| --- | ---: |
| Top 1 | **3.1%** |
| Top 3 | 5.9% |
| Top 5 | 8.5% |
| Top 10 | 13.8% |
| Top 20 | 21.7% |
| Top 50 | 38.8% |

One title is a fifth of all viewing. One person is a thirty-second of all attention. The fiftieth-ranked performer still draws a seventh of what the first does — where, among titles, the top one is larger than the next several combined.

## And appearing in more of the hits does not help

The obvious hypothesis is that attention follows exposure: be in more of the shows the world is watching and more people will look you up.

Within the fifty most-viewed performers, the correlation between the number of charting titles they appear in and their pageviews is **−0.16**. That is no relationship at all, with a faint negative lean too small for a sample of fifty to mean anything by.

So the ensemble regular of four charting series and the lead of one are not separable on this measure. Whatever drives someone to type a name into Wikipedia, it is not simply how often that name has been on screen.

## Why the difference is worth noticing

A title is a single object that everyone either watched or did not. Attention on a person is a thousand separate small decisions, spread across a month, and it does not collapse onto one winner the way a chart does.

That is a practical point for anyone reading Korean entertainment coverage. Rankings of titles and rankings of people look like the same kind of list and are not. A title chart is dominated by its top row and the rest is a long tail. A person chart is a gentle slope where the difference between first and fiftieth is a factor of seven.

**One caution that applies to every figure above.** 336 of the 1,344 performers on our roster have no English Wikipedia article, so they cannot be measured at all — they are not in the 1,008 and are not counted as zero. And a pageview count records that someone looked, never why. A month with one large piece of news about one person would redraw the top of this table entirely.
