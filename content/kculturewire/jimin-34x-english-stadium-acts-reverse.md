If you write about Korean music in English, you are working from an English-language measure whether
you meant to or not — search interest, Wikipedia traffic, English-language coverage. It is worth
knowing what that measure sees clearly and what it barely registers.

Wikipedia lets us check, because it counts each language edition separately.

## The same six people, two languages

| Singer | Kind | Korean | English | Which language wins |
|---|---|---:|---:|---|
| Jang Minho | Trot | 59 | 17 | Korean, by 3.5× |
| Young Tak | Trot | 64 | 28 | Korean, by 2.3× |
| Lim Young-woong | Trot | 149 | 91 | Korean, by 1.6× |
| IU | Solo | 195 | 1,844 | English, by 9.5× |
| Rosé | Idol | 105 | 2,461 | English, by 23.4× |
| Jimin | Idol | 61 | 2,091 | English, by 34.3× |

Median daily reads over the last 30 days. [The full table and method are here](/which-language-reads-you).

**Jimin is opened 2,091 times a day in English and 61 times in Korean.** Same encyclopedia, same
person, thirty-four times the difference depending on which edition you open.

## The reverse case, and why it is worth a paragraph

Lim Young-woong played Goyang Stadium three nights running this weekend, the run reported at around
95,000 seats across the three days. In our measure he draws **91 English readers on an ordinary
day** — fewer than a twentieth of Jimin.

That is not a claim that he is small. It is a claim about the instrument. An artist whose audience
reads Korean produces almost no signal in an English-language measure, however many seats they sell.
All three trot singers we checked sit on that side of the line, and all three of the acts English
outlets cover heavily sit on the other.

## What this means if you read English coverage of K-pop

The English-language picture of Korean music is not a shrunken copy of the Korean one. It is a
**differently shaped** picture. The acts that dominate it are the acts whose audience already reads
English, and the gap is not small — it runs from 9.5× one way to 3.5× the other across these six
names alone.

We publish this because every reading figure on this site is counted in English, including all of
ours. A reader is entitled to know which parts of Korean music our own numbers systematically
under-count. This is one of them, [along with the acts that have no English article at
all](/cannot-see).

## What we could not measure

Popularity, sales, streams or attendance — none of those are in this. The concert figure above is
someone else's count, quoted as the occasion for looking, and it is not used in any calculation here.

Korean Wikipedia is also a smaller encyclopedia than English Wikipedia overall, so some of this gap
is about the encyclopedias rather than the artists. We cannot separate the two with this data, so we
do not claim to.

---

*This counts which language an encyclopedia page is opened in. It is not a measure of anyone's
career, and six people are six people.*
