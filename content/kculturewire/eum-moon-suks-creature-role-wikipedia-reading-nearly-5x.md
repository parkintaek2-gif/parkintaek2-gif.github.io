Eum Moon-suk played "Yang-bae," a village hunter in Na Hong-jin's creature film "Hope" -- a role
Korean entertainment coverage repeatedly singled out for its physical transformation, months before
the film reached North American theaters. His English Wikipedia reading averaged 186 views a day in
the first week of September, 2026. By 13 September, four days after the film's confirmed North
American release date, that number reached 905 -- 4.9 times his baseline.

## What happened

"Hope" premiered in the main competition at the 2026 Cannes Film Festival in May and opened in South
Korean theaters on 15 July. Nate News reported in June that its North American theatrical release
had been confirmed for 9 September. Eum's reading was already at baseline through the film's Korean
run over the summer; the climb we can see starts at the North American release window in September,
consistent with an English-speaking audience encountering the film, and one of its more visually
striking supporting performances, for the first time.

## What we're not claiming

We have a confirmed release date and a reading climb that starts around it, but not a single dated
article that explains why the peak specifically landed on 13 September, four days after release,
rather than on release day itself. Reviews, word of mouth, and social clips about a striking makeup
effect can all build over days rather than landing in one dated story we can point to. We are
reporting the release window as the trigger and the exact shape of the four-day climb as something
we could not pin down further.
