Rock in Rio has run since 1985 without a K-pop headliner. That changed on 12 September 2026, when
Stray Kids topped the bill in front of what Korea JoongAng Daily reported as a 100,000-strong
crowd — a booking Billboard called a "historic Rock in Rio debut." Mamamoo's Hwasa also performed
at the festival; a Reddit post citing G1, one of Brazil's largest news outlets, said G1 named her
the best K-pop performance on the bill and the second-best performance overall, across every act.

We don't referee stage performances. What we can check is how many people looked each act up on
English Wikipedia in the days around the festival — and there, the group that got the "historic
headliner" coverage rose less than the solo act on the undercard, whose own group's page barely
moved at all.

## What we measured

We pulled daily English Wikipedia pageviews for Stray Kids, Hwasa and Mamamoo (the group page, not
counting Hwasa's individual page) over the 14 days ending 14 September 2026, the freshest complete
day the Wikimedia API had at the time of writing. Using the nine days before the festival buildup
(1-9 September) as a baseline, Stray Kids averaged **2,143 views a day**, Hwasa **538**, and
Mamamoo's group page **456**.

| | 9-day avg | 12 Sep | vs. avg | 13 Sep | vs. avg | 14 Sep | vs. avg |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stray Kids | 2,143 | 3,019 | 1.41x | 2,648 | 1.24x | 2,700 | 1.26x |
| Hwasa | 538 | 1,301 | **2.42x** | 895 | 1.67x | 785 | 1.46x |
| Mamamoo (group) | 456 | 566 | 1.24x | 435 | 0.95x | 430 | 0.94x |

Stray Kids' rise is real and sustained — three straight days above baseline, which is the shape
we'd expect from a headline booking that made international entertainment news for days. But it
tops out at 1.41x. Hwasa's page rose to nearly two and a half times its own baseline on the same
day, and unlike Stray Kids' curve, it hadn't fully decayed two days later.

## The group page did not follow its own member

The sharpest contrast isn't between the two acts — it's between Hwasa and her own group. Mamamoo's
group page moved the least of the three on 12 September (1.24x, the same multiple as Stray Kids'
two-days-later reading) and then fell **below** its own baseline on both following days. A member's
individual page can carry a spike that the group page it belongs to does not share. We've seen this
shape before in K-pop reading data — attention aimed at one name doesn't reliably transfer to the
page for the act that name is part of — and this is another instance of it, not a new finding.

## What we're not saying

G1's ranking of Hwasa's performance and the Wikipedia numbers here are not the same measurement,
and we're not stacking them into a single "Hwasa won" conclusion. One is a critic's judgment made
on the night; the other is a count of lookups over the following two days, which could reflect the
award mention, pre-existing interest in her name, festival press coverage generally, or a
combination we can't separate with pageview data alone. We're reporting the gap, not explaining it.
