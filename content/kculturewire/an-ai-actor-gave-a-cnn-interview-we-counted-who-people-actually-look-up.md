Three Korean newspaper headlines landed on the same morning, 10 September 2026. An AI actor
had given an interview to CNN and said it was not humanity's enemy. In a second piece the
same AI actor was asked to improvise a scene and, in the reporter's word, creaked. And a
studio announced it was starting co-production with the Korean film industry using
generated performers.

The question underneath all three is whether synthetic performers are taking the place of
human ones. That question gets argued. It rarely gets counted.

One thing about it can be counted, cheaply and repeatedly: how many people go and look
something up. English Wikipedia publishes per-article pageview counts, the API filters out
bots before it hands the number over, and the data is public domain. So we took nine
virtual performers and six real K-pop groups and counted twelve months of lookups —
September 2025 through August 2026.

## The headline number, and why we do not trust it on its own

| Twelve months, English Wikipedia | Lookups |
|---|---|
| KPop Demon Hunters (film, virtual) | 8,303,346 |
| BTS, BLACKPINK, TWICE, Stray Kids, NewJeans and aespa **combined** | 8,267,254 |

The virtual side wins. It wins by 36,092 lookups out of 8.3 million — **0.4%**.

That is not a lead. That is a tie with a rounding error attached, and anyone who published
it as "virtual K-pop has overtaken real K-pop" would be telling a lie with a true number.

Here is the real side broken out, so the total above can be added up rather than taken on
trust:

| Real group | Twelve-month lookups |
|---|---|
| BTS | 3,025,276 |
| BLACKPINK | 1,818,838 |
| TWICE | 974,635 |
| Stray Kids | 956,386 |
| NewJeans | 888,004 |
| aespa | 604,115 |

We know exactly how fragile it is, because we measured the same comparison twice on
purpose. For our [fictional-vs-real](/fictional-vs-real) page we used the window August 2025
to July 2026 and got **1.2 times**. This piece uses September 2025 to August 2026 and gets
**1.00 times**. Nothing about the world changed between those two measurements. We moved
the window by one month.

## Last month the real groups won by 3.2 times

| Most recent single month | Lookups |
|---|---|
| KPop Demon Hunters | 199,338 |
| The six real groups combined | 639,708 |
| BTS alone | 260,085 |

The film's page fell **87.7%** from its first month in the window to its last. BTS's page
**rose 42%** over the same period. One of these is a release; the other is a following.

## Strip the film out and the picture changes completely

The film is one title with one opening. Virtual performers as a *category* have been
operating for far longer — Gorillaz launched in 1998, Hatsune Miku in 2007, K/DA in 2018,
Eternity and Isegye Idol in 2021, MAVE: and PLAVE in 2023. That is 27 years of shipped
product, not a summer.

So we removed the film and its in-film group and counted the seven that have been running
for years:

| Virtual, film excluded | Twelve-month lookups |
|---|---|
| Gorillaz (British, 1998) | 2,682,745 |
| Hatsune Miku (Japanese, 2007) | 1,138,576 |
| K/DA (from a video game, 2018) | 168,128 |
| PLAVE (Korean, 2023) | 69,094 |
| MAVE: (Korean, 2023) | 21,475 |
| Isegye Idol (Korean, 2021) | 6,347 |
| Eternity (Korean, AI-generated, 2021) | 4,827 |

The biggest virtual performer that is not a 2025 film is a **British band from 1998**. It
draws 0.32 times the six real Korean groups combined, and less than BTS on its own
(3,025,276).

And the biggest *Korean* virtual idol group, PLAVE, drew 69,094 lookups in a year —
**2.3% of BTS's 3,025,276.** Not a third. Not a tenth. Forty-four times smaller.

## Two of the seven grew, five shrank

Direction is worth separating from size, because a small thing that is growing and a small
thing that is fading are different stories.

- **Isegye Idol** rose 4.9% from its first month in the window to its last — the only
  virtual page here that ended higher than it started.
- Eternity fell 5.2%, Hatsune Miku 0.3%, MAVE: 22.2%, K/DA 41.5%, PLAVE 52.5%, Gorillaz
  58.5%.
- On the real side BTS rose 42% while the other five fell.

## What this does not tell you

Pageviews are lookups. They are not streams, tickets, ad revenue or a contract with a
burger chain. A page can be read by someone who loves a group and by someone who has just
heard the name in a headline and wants to know what it is, and this count cannot tell those
two people apart.

The film comparison has a further problem we will not paper over: people open a film page to
find the plot, the cast or the song they heard, and they open a group page because they
follow that group. Putting the two side by side is useful for asking "what is being looked
up" and useless for asking "who has fans."

The AI actor from the CNN interview is not in this table at all. It has no English Wikipedia
article inside our window, so there is nothing to count. We could have substituted something
adjacent and called it a proxy. We would rather leave the row empty and say why.

## Why we published the weaker version of our own finding

We had a cleaner number available. The August-to-July window gives 1.2 times, which reads
as a real lead, and it is a number we measured ourselves and already published on an axis
page. Choosing that window and staying quiet about the other one would have produced a
better headline out of the same honest data.

The reason we did not is that a reader who repeats "virtual beat real by 1.2 times" and is
then shown the September-to-August window has been handed a fact that dissolves. We would
rather hand over a tie, two windows that disagree, and the arithmetic to check both.
