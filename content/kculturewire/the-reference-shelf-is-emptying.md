Southeast Asia is reading less about Korea than it did a year ago. We have measured that on
several pages now. The question we had not answered is whether it is true of everything Korea
makes, or whether it depends on what.

So we split the articles into three kinds — music, screen, and a third we called language and
craft: the Korean language, hangul, hanbok, taekwondo. Then, beside each Korean article, we put
a Japanese one of the same kind. Not to rank them. To find out whether a fall is something
happening to Korea or something happening to that shelf of the encyclopaedia.

Only one of the three genres survived the check we run before publishing a median. That one is
language and craft, and it says something we did not expect.

## Both sides fell, by almost the same amount

| Article | First 12 months | Last 12 months | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Korean language | 168.3 | 95.4 | −43.3% |
| Taekwondo | 165.9 | 121.8 | −26.6% |
| Hanbok | 40.7 | 30.8 | −24.4% |
| Hangul | 67.1 | 62.7 | −6.5% |
| **Korean median** | | | **−25.5%** |
| Japanese language | 296.0 | 236.5 | −20.1% |
| Karate | 87.3 | 56.1 | −35.7% |
| Kana | 19.9 | 13.0 | −34.8% |
| Kimono | 49.7 | 35.7 | −28.2% |
| Manga | 113.9 | 112.7 | −1.1% |
| **Japanese median** | | | **−28.2%** |

Figures are reads per million reads of each Wikipedia, summed across the Indonesian, Vietnamese,
Thai and Malay editions, first twelve months of the window against the last twelve.

The Japanese articles are a control, not a competitor. Had they held steady while the Korean ones
dropped, the fall would be about Korea, and that would be the story. They did not hold steady.
They fell slightly further. Whatever moved, moved both.

## What kind of article this is

The articles in this genre have something in common that the music and screen ones do not. Nobody
opens *Hangul* because they are a fan of hangul. They open it to find out what it is — how the
letters work, who made them, why the blocks are shaped that way. The same goes for *Kana*,
*Hanbok*, *Kimono*, *Karate*, *Taekwondo*.

This is the reference shelf: the articles you go to when you have encountered something and want
to know what it was. And across four countries, over two years, it is being opened less — for
Korea and for Japan alike, by roughly a quarter.

We can rule out one explanation with the control we have: this is not Southeast Asia turning away
from Korea specifically. We cannot tell you what it is instead. A reader who once opened an
encyclopaedia article may now be asking somewhere else entirely, and we have no way to see that
from inside Wikipedia's own numbers.

## The two genres we could not use

We measured three. Two of them produced numbers that look like findings and are not.

| Genre | Korea | Japan (control) |
|---|---|---|
| Music | −10.8% (4 articles) | +14.5% (3 articles) |
| Screen | −4.3% (4 articles) | +22.9% (4 articles) |

Korean music down while Japanese music is up is the kind of gap that writes its own headline. We
are not going to print it, because it does not survive the check.

Each of those medians rests on three or four articles. Before publishing any median we remove one
observation, recompute, and repeat for each — a jackknife, a method from 1949. If removing a
single article moves the answer by more than half its own size, the sample is too small for that
median to be a finding. For Korean music the swing is 1.13 times the figure itself. For Japanese
screen it is 4.12. Take one article out of either and the comparison can reverse.

We are showing those rows rather than dropping them, because dropping them would turn this page
into a claim about Korean music that we cannot support. The honest version is: we looked, and our
sample is not good enough to say.

## What this does not tell you

The genres are ours. We decided that taekwondo belongs with hangul and that *Squid Game* belongs
with cinema, and a different grouping would produce different medians. Wikipedia does not sort
itself this way.

An encyclopaedia article is not the thing itself. Reads of *Korean language* are not people
learning Korean, and a fall in reads of *Taekwondo* is not a fall in people practising it. What
we can see is how often someone opened a page to find out what something was.

And a control removes an explanation without supplying one. We know this is not a fall that
singles Korea out. We do not know what it is.
