Twelve Korean titles, a mix of films and shows whose English Wikipedia pages are known to have
spiked at some point, give a clean pattern when you ask one question of each: on the single day
each title's own page peaked, how much of that peak's traffic went to its best-known cast member,
measured the same way?

## The bigger the peak, the smaller the actor's share of it

| Title | Title's peak-day views | Best-drawing cast member | Their views, same day | Share |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Parasite | 2,696,922 | Song Kang-ho | 105,177 | 3.9% |
| Squid Game | 889,821 | Lee Jung-jae | 92,964 | 10.4% |
| All of Us Are Dead | 225,923 | Cho Yi-hyun | 39,862 | 17.6% |
| Crash Landing on You | 131,083 | Hyun Bin | 44,880 | 34.2% |
| Hellbound | 108,058 | Kim Hyun-joo | 12,977 | 12.0% |
| Sweet Home | 106,217 | Song Kang | 19,439 | 18.3% |
| The Glory | 102,540 | Song Hye-kyo | 27,338 | 26.7% |
| Itaewon Class | 74,543 | Park Seo-joon | 23,054 | 30.9% |
| Extraordinary Attorney Woo | 69,017 | Park Eun-bin | 20,136 | 29.2% |
| Kingdom | 63,783 | Ju Ji-hoon | 11,142 | 17.5% |
| The Silent Sea | 59,760 | Gong Yoo | 13,678 | 22.9% |
| Mousetrap | 23,512 | Ryu Jun-yeol | 5,427 | 23.1% |

Sorted by the title's own peak, the pattern runs almost straight down: Parasite's 2.7 million-view
peak day sent only 3.9% of that traffic to its most-looked-up actor, Song Kang-ho. Crash Landing
on You's much smaller 131,083-view peak sent more than a third of it — 34.2% — to Hyun Bin. Across
all 12, the correlation between a title's own peak size and its lead actor's share is -0.68
(t = -2.92 on 10 degrees of freedom).

## The lead actor was not our pick

We did not choose who counts as each title's "lead" before measuring. We pulled every credited
cast member's page and read all of them on the title's own peak day, then used whichever one
actually drew the most views. Guessing in advance would have been wrong twice here — Hellbound's
biggest draw is Kim Hyun-joo, not the ensemble's top-billed name, and the same is true for The
Silent Sea, where Gong Yoo leads.

## What could be doing this instead of what it looks like

This sits beside our running [fictional-vs-real count](/fictional-vs-real), which asks a related
question — whether a title's own page draws more English-language attention than the people in
it. Two things push against reading this new pattern as "megahits are about the story, not the
star." First, the
biggest peaks in this set — Parasite, Squid Game — are also the titles with the most credited cast
members we could measure (9 and 14), which mechanically spreads the same pool of attention across
more pages and shrinks any one person's slice. Second, cast coverage is uneven: Crash Landing on
You is missing 10 of a possible 25 cast pages from this count, and a title missing rows could show
a share that is higher or lower than its true one for that reason alone, not because of anything
about audience attention. We're publishing the correlation with both of those caveats attached,
not as a settled account of why it holds.

Every value here is same-day: the actor's page is read on the exact date the title itself peaked.
Actor pages commonly peak a day or two after a title's own peak, once a cast is being searched by
name rather than the title's headline. That makes every share in this table a floor on how much of
a title's attention eventually reaches its lead — not the final word on it.
