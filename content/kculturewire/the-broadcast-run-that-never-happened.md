There are two ways a Korean series ends up in a Netflix top 10. It aired on a Korean television
network — SBS, tvN, KBS, MBC — and was licensed on afterwards. Or it never had a broadcast run at
all, and streaming was the only place it ever played.

We had the credits to split the charting series on that line, so we did. The two halves do not behave
like the same business.

## Four markets, or thirty-nine

| | Aired on a Korean network | No broadcaster on record |
|---|---|---|
| Series | 238 | 49 |
| Markets reached, median | 4 | **39** |
| Chart places held, median | 12 | **83** |
| Charted in exactly one market | 79 | 1 |
| Reached 20 markets or more | 45 | 37 |

The median series that aired in Korea first reached **four** countries. The median series that did
not reached **thirty-nine**. Seventy-nine of the network series charted in one country and never
travelled anywhere else; one of the forty-nine did.

That is not a small difference at the edges. It is a different distribution.

## The network route is not the losing one

The obvious misreading is that broadcast is finished and streaming won. The numbers say something
narrower and more useful.

The 238 network series hold **14,847 chart places** — 60.6% of everything Korean series took across
93 markets. The 49 hold 9,668. There are nearly five times as many network series, each travelling
much less far, and between them they still account for the larger share of the chart.

**Two businesses, both working, with different shapes.** One puts a great many titles into a few
markets each. The other puts a few titles into most of the world.

## What we are actually measuring

We are reading "Wikidata records no first broadcaster" as "it never aired on a Korean network". That
is a proxy and we want to be plain about it.

Two things support it. Every title in that group carries **other** company credits — production
companies, distributors — so the record is not blank; only the broadcaster field is. And the group
is recognisable on sight. These are the ones that reached 60 markets or more, alphabetically:

*All of Us Are Dead*, *Bloodhounds*, *Gyeongseong Creature*, *Hellbound*, *If Wishes Could Kill*, *Mask Girl*, *Mercy For None*, *Money Heist: Korea - Joint Economic Area*, *Oasis*, *Squid Game*, *The 8 Show*, *The Art of Sarah*, *The Glory*, *The Trauma Code: Heroes on Call*, *The WONDERfools*.

That is the made-for-streaming slate, and it is why we are willing to use the proxy. It is also why
we are printing the list rather than describing it: anyone who thinks the proxy is wrong can see
exactly what it caught.

## Films go the other way

Run the same split on films and the direction reverses — 3 markets for those with a broadcaster
credit against 1 for those without, on much smaller medians either way.

So this is a fact about Korean **series**, not a fact about streaming, and we are not going to write
it as one. A film's broadcast history is a weaker signal in any case: films reach television after
cinemas, if they reach it at all, and a missing broadcaster says less.

## What this cannot tell you

It cannot tell you **why**. A streamer that promotes its own commissions in every market on the same
day would produce exactly this shape. So would a genuine difference in what the two pipelines choose
to make. Netflix publishes neither its promotion budgets nor its commissioning decisions, so we can
describe two footprints and not explain them, and anyone who tells you which mechanism is at work is
telling you something this data does not hold.

It also rests on the 77.2% of charting Korean series that carry any company credit at all — 287 of
372. A series whose credits Wikidata does not hold is absent from this comparison rather than
counted as either kind.

## Why it matters to anyone buying

Because "a Korean series" is not one product. If you are looking at a title with a Korean broadcast
run behind it, the base case from five years of charts is a handful of markets, most likely nearby
ones. If you are looking at one made for streaming, the base case is most of the world. Those two
cases justify very different prices, and a single average across both would misprice both.
