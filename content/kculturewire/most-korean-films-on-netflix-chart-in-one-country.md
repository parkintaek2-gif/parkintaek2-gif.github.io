Reviews out of Venice this week put Lee Chang-dong's *Possible Love* — his first feature in
years, running close to three hours, made for Netflix — in front of an international
audience before most people have had a chance to watch it. It is a good week to ask what
usually happens to a Korean film on that service.

The answer is narrower than the headlines about Korean cinema suggest.

## The median Korean film on Netflix charts in exactly one country

Between July 2021 and August 2026, across 93 national Top 10 charts:

| | Korean films | Korean series |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Titles that charted anywhere | **577** | 396 |
| Median countries reached | **1** | 5 |
| Median weeks on a chart | 3 | 6 |
| Median chart places held | 3 | 15 |
| Charted in exactly one country | **328** | 119 |
| Charted for exactly one week | 118 | 37 |
| Reached number one somewhere | 155 | 149 |

**328 of the 577 films — 57% — never appeared outside a single country's chart.** For
series the same figure is 30%.

More Korean films chart than Korean series, and they travel far less far. That is not a
contradiction: a film is a cheap thing to add to a catalogue in one market, and a great
many Korean films are added that way.

## Why these numbers are lower than the ones we published in August

We wrote in August that the median Korean series charted in 35 countries and the median
film in 11. Those figures came from a deliberately narrow set: the 232 titles that had
appeared in a Netflix *global* Top 10 at least once, because the global list carries a
language label and the label is what proves a title is the Korean work rather than a
foreign one with the same name.

That door only admits titles that already travelled. This piece uses the wider set —
titles identified by Wikidata item number, whether or not they ever reached a global chart —
and the medians fall accordingly. Both numbers are correct about their own set. The August
piece answers *how far do the Korean titles that travel get?* This one answers *what
happens to a Korean film on Netflix, taken as a whole?*

We are keeping both, with the doors named, rather than quietly replacing one with the other.

## The ceiling, when a film hits it, is the whole map

| Film | Countries | At once | Weeks | Chart places | Run |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | --- |
| The Great Flood | 93 | **93** | 11 | 351 | 2025-12-21 → 2026-03-15 |
| Carter | 92 | 92 | 5 | 201 | 2022-08-07 → 2022-09-04 |
| Desire | 90 | 90 | 5 | 247 | 2026-07-19 → 2026-08-16 |

*The Great Flood* charted in all 93 markets **in the same week**, and held 351 country-weeks
in total. That is what the top of this distribution looks like — not a slow build across
territories but everything, everywhere, immediately, and then eleven weeks.

Length is a separate thing from width. *Tarot* charted for 34 weeks, the longest run of any
Korean film here, but reached only 54 countries and never more than 10 at once. It stayed
on shelves rather than sweeping them.

## What this says about a prestige release

A festival premiere buys the one thing the median Korean film on Netflix does not have:
attention in several countries at the same time. Whether it converts into a wide chart run
is a different question, and one we can only answer afterwards — so we will not guess it
now.

What we can say is that attention of this kind is short. We measured [774 Korean musical acts](/group-afterlife)
and found the median one loses half of its peak Wikipedia readership within **two months**;
for [26 Korean titles measured the same way](/half-life), the answer was also two months. Comics are the
exception — a Korean webtoon is [read **14 times more**](/webtoon-adaptations) once a drama exists — because an
adaptation builds an audience rather than spending one.

## Method

Every chart place here was read from Netflix's published weekly Top 10 tables for individual
countries, 93 markets, from the week of 2021-07-04 to the week of 2026-08-16. Korean titles
were identified through Wikidata by item number — the companies credited on a title and
cast members recorded as Korean citizens — never by matching names. A place is one
appearance in one country's weekly chart; reach is the count of distinct countries, and we
never add places across countries and call the total a reach. Whether a film opened in
cinemas first is not in these rows, so films of both kinds sit in the same table.
