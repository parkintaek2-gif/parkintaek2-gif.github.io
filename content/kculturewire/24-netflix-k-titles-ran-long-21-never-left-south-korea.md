Ask what a "quiet hit" is and most lists answer with taste: a handful of titles someone decided
were underrated. We wanted a version of that question with no taste in it, so we took every
Korean-credited title we hold in Netflix's own weekly top 10 data — 546 of them, across 93
countries and five years — and let the data set both thresholds itself: a long run is one at or
above the median (6 weeks), and a narrow one is one at or below the bottom quarter for how many
countries it ever charted in (1 country). Twenty-four titles clear both bars at once.

## The Promise ran 36 weeks — in one country that is not South Korea

| Title | Type | Weeks on chart | Peak position | Only country |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| The Promise | TV | 36 | 2 | Philippines |
| Mr. Sunshine | TV | 23 | 7 | South Korea |
| Exhuma | Film | 13 | 1 | South Korea |
| It's Okay to Not Be Okay | TV | 13 | 1 | Philippines |
| 12.12: The Day | Film | 12 | 1 | South Korea |
| Good Partner | TV | 12 | 1 | South Korea |
| The Killing Vote | TV | 11 | 2 | South Korea |
| Bad Guys | TV | 7 | 1 | Thailand |
| Decision to Leave | Film | 6 | 2 | South Korea |

The full list runs to 24 titles between 6 and 36 weeks, every one confined to a single country's
chart for its entire run. (The remaining 15 — Citizen of a Kind, My Mister, The Dream Life of Mr.
Kim, Yadang: The Snitch, Miracle: Letters to the President, The Ugly, A Good Day to Be a Dog,
Amazon Bullseye, Behind Every Star, In Our Prime, My Happy Ending, No Other Choice, Pale Moon,
Parole Examiner Lee and The Witch — all ran 6 to 9 weeks, all in South Korea alone.)

## Only three actually travelled

Of the 24, only three charted in a country other than South Korea, and none of them charted at
home *and* abroad — each ran its whole streak in exactly one foreign market: The Promise and It's
Okay to Not Be Okay in the Philippines, Bad Guys in Thailand. The other 21 are titles that stayed
on South Korea's own weekly top 10 for one and a half to five and a half months and, by this
chart, nowhere else at all.

## The surprise is not the obscure ones

The list includes Decision to Leave — a Park Chan-wook film that won Best Director at Cannes the
year it was released. By Netflix's country-level weekly chart, it spent six weeks on South
Korea's top 10 and never appeared on any other country's list. That is not a knock on the film;
Netflix's top 10 counts hours viewed within a country, and a title can be watched heavily abroad
through a cinema release, a different platform, or word of mouth, none of which this chart sees.
It is a reminder of what "quiet" means here: not obscure, just absent from every chart but one.

That absence runs in both directions. The same chart also holds titles that never once appeared
on [South Korea's own weekly top 10](/article/a-third-of-what-travels-never-charts-at-home) —
150 of them, travelling abroad while going unwatched, by this measure, at home.
