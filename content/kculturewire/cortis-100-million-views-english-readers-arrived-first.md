Cortis's music video for "FaSHioN" passed 100 million views on YouTube at about 4am Korean time this
morning, roughly a year after its release. That number is YouTube's, not ours, and we have no way to
audit it.

We hold a different number, and it says something the view count does not.

## What we count

Every reading figure we publish comes from one place: how many people opened an English Wikipedia
page on a given day. It is a narrow measure and we say so on every page that uses it. But it has one
property that matters here — it is recorded daily, from the day an article exists, for every group
alike. So we can ask a question the milestone cannot answer:

**Of the groups that debuted alongside Cortis, how many are read more in English now than they were
at the start?**

## One of five

| Group | Debuted | First 90 days | Last 30 days | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Cortis | 2025-08-18 | 231 | 2,196 | 9.5x |
| Hearts2Hearts | 2025-02-24 | 840 | 912 | 1.1x |
| KickFlip | 2025-01-20 | 280 | 189 | 0.7x |
| Idid | 2025-09-15 | 196 | 81 | 0.4x |
| AHOF | 2025-07-01 | 700 | 140 | 0.2x |

Readers a day, median. [The full axis, with the established groups as a ruler, is here](/rookie-reading).

Cortis started smaller than three of the four. In its first three months it drew 231 English readers
on an ordinary day — fewer than Hearts2Hearts, fewer than AHOF, fewer than KickFlip. It is now read
about ten times as often as it was then, and more than any of them.

None of the other four did that. Hearts2Hearts holds roughly the audience it opened with.
KickFlip, Idid and AHOF are each read less on an ordinary day now than in their first months —
AHOF at a fifth of where it began.

That is worth stating plainly, because it inverts the usual assumption: **for most groups the debut
months are the peak of English-language attention, not the beginning of a climb.** The debut is when
the coverage happens. What follows is usually a decline.

## Why the milestone is the wrong place to look

A view count is cumulative. It can only go up, and it goes up fastest for whoever is already largest.
It cannot tell you whether the audience is still arriving or arrived a year ago and stopped.

Daily reading can fall, and for four of these five groups it did. That is what makes it useful.
Cortis's 100 million is the visible result of an audience that had already been building for months —
by our count the climb is well over a year old, and today's number is a receipt for it rather than a
cause of it.

## What we cannot say

We cannot say the milestone moved anything, because **we cannot measure today yet**. Wikimedia fills
its counts in about a day late, so 5 September is not in our data — absent, not zero. We have marked
the date and will be able to look at it later this week.

We also cannot say why any group's biggest day was its biggest day. Three of the five — Cortis,
KickFlip and Idid — each peaked on the same date, 29 November 2025. A shared date suggests a shared
event. Naming that event would be a guess, and we do not publish guesses as findings.

And we cannot see any group that has no English Wikipedia article at all. [Three in four Korean
singers do not have one](/cannot-see), and none of them can appear in a table like the one above.

---

*This is a statistic, not a verdict on anyone's career. It counts people who opened an encyclopedia
page in English, which is one narrow slice of an audience, and it says nothing about how any of these
groups are doing in Korea.*
