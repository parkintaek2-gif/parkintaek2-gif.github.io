Count casting slots — one person in one title — and this panel leans television. **1,804 series slots
against 1,609 film slots**, across 635 titles.

Now look at the thirty people who appear in ten or more of these titles. Their slots split the other
way, and not narrowly.

**263 film. 109 series.**

| | Titles | Film | Series |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Hwang Jung-min | 21 | 20 | 1 |
| Ma Dong-seok | 18 | 17 | 1 |
| Sul Kyung-gu | 17 | 17 | 0 |
| Lee Byung-hun | 17 | 12 | 5 |
| Ha Jung-woo | 16 | 15 | 1 |
| Kim Eui-sung | 15 | 9 | 6 |
| Kim Hae-suk | 14 | 6 | 8 |
| Jin Seon-kyu | 14 | 11 | 3 |

The most prolific person in the panel has one series credit in twenty-one titles. The second has one
in eighteen. Sul Kyung-gu has none at all.

## Why this is not a contradiction

A series occupies an actor for months and yields **one** credit. A film occupies weeks and yields
one credit too. Anyone accumulating a large number of credits is therefore far more likely to be
doing it through film, whatever the medium's relative size — and the top of this list is what that
arithmetic looks like.

So the finding is not that Korean television employs fewer people. It employs more, and the slot
count says so. The finding is that **"appears in the most titles" is close to a definition of film
actor**, and any ranking built on title counts will quietly select for film without saying so.

That matters if you were about to use a count like this as a proxy for prominence. It is a proxy for
throughput, and throughput is a property of format before it is a property of a person.

## The exceptions are the interesting rows

Kim Hae-suk is the only name in the top eight with more series than film. Kim Eui-sung is close to
even at 9 and 6. Both are character actors who work constantly in both, which is exactly the profile
the arithmetic above says should be rare.

## What could be producing this instead

**Series casts are long and recorded thinly.** A sixteen-episode drama may credit forty people;
Wikidata records the median series here with five. If the missing names are disproportionately
series supporting cast, the true series slot count is higher and this gap narrows. We cannot measure
how much, and we are not going to model it.

**And this is only what charted.** A television actor with thirty credits, none of which reached a
Netflix Top 10, does not appear at all.
