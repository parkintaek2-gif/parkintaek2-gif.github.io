Across the 248 listed Korean content companies that disclose it, **38.8% of staff are women** —
against **28.0%** across all 2,779 listed companies that report the same field.

That is the headline, and it is the less interesting of the two facts here.

| | Staff | Women |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Film, video and audio production | 10,540 | **56.9%** |
| Broadcasting | 6,393 | 47.5% |
| Publishing (includes games) | 51,876 | **34.1%** |
| All 248 content companies | 68,809 | 38.8% |
| Whole listed market | 1,865,198 | 28.0% |

**The spread inside the sector is 22.8 points. The gap to the rest of the market is 11.** Whatever
"content industry" means for this question, it means less than which part of it you are looking at.

## Why the sector total lands where it does

Film is the most female workforce in the table and one of the smallest — 10,540 people. Publishing
and games is the least female and by far the largest at 51,876, three-quarters of the sector's staff.

So the sector figure of 38.8% is much closer to games than to film, because games is where most of
the people are. **A reader who takes 38.8% as a description of working in Korean content will be
wrong about both ends of it** — too low for film, too high for games.

This is the ordinary hazard of a weighted average over unlike parts, and it is why we print the three
rows rather than the one.

## What we cannot tell you

**The disclosure is a headcount split and nothing else.** It does not say who holds which roles, who
is senior, or what anyone is paid. A workforce that is 56.9% women may or may not have women in the
jobs that decide what gets made, and this filing is silent on it.

**We have no explanation for the 22.8-point gap.** We looked for one in the same filings — company age,
company size, tenure, pay — and none of them line up with it. Broadcasting is the oldest and
best-paid group and sits in the middle. Film is the lowest-paid and the most female. Publishing has
the shortest tenure and the fewest women. There is no ordering of the three by any other column that
reproduces the gender ordering.

Stating that plainly is the honest end of this piece. The number is large, it is measured the same
way in all three groups, and we do not know why it is what it is.

**And it is listed companies only.** Much of Korean film and drama production is private and files
none of this. The 10,540 people in the film row are the ones inside listed companies, which is not
the same as the people who make Korean films.
