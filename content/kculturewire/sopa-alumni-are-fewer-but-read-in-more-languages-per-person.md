School of Performing Arts Seoul (SOPA) has a third as many credited alumni on Wikidata as Seoul Institute of the Arts. Its alumni's Wikipedia articles exist in more than double the number of language editions per person.

## What the numbers say

We linked every Korean public figure with a Wikidata "educated at" entry to their school, then counted how many language editions of Wikipedia carry each person's own article — a rough proxy for how widely known someone has become outside Korea.

| School | Alumni (measured) | Median language editions | Share in 10+ editions | Top name |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| School of Performing Arts Seoul | 125 | 13 | 58.4% | Jungkook (68) |
| Seoul Institute of the Arts | 352 | 6 | 31.0% | Cho Jung-seok (57) |
| Chung-Ang University | 310 | 6 | 34.8% | Kim Soo-hyun (52) |
| Dongguk University | 200 | 6 | 41.5% | Jun Ji-hyun (49) |

Seoul Institute of the Arts has nearly three times SOPA's alumni count, and Chung-Ang University is close behind it — but neither school's median comes close to SOPA's. More than half of SOPA's alumni sit in 10 or more language editions; less than a third of Seoul Institute of the Arts' do.

## Who is on each list

SOPA's top five are Jungkook (68 editions), Jisoo (68), Kang Seul-gi (59), Bae Suzy (51), and Sulli (47) — five names whose careers are built on K-pop group membership with existing international fan bases. Seoul Institute of the Arts' top five — Cho Jung-seok, Bae Suzy, Park Seo-joon, Son Ye-jin, Lee Joon-gi — are mostly actors whose international reach follows a drama's own export path rather than a group's.

## What this can and cannot say

This cannot separate a school's own effect from who chooses to attend it. SOPA is known for training idol trainees before their labels debut them internationally; a roster tilted toward K-pop idols with pre-existing global fandoms would show exactly this pattern regardless of anything the school itself does. What the count does establish cleanly is that alumni size and per-person reach are not the same measurement — the school with fewer credited alumni has the wider footprint per person, not the other way around.
