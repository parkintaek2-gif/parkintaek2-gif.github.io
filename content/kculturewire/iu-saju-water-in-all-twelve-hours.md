IU was born on 16 May 1993, and no source anywhere publishes the hour. Those two facts are the only things in this article that are not a reading.

## Three pillars, not four

Saju sets a birth moment out as four pillars of two characters each — year, month, day, hour — so a complete chart is eight characters. The hour pillar needs a clock time. KLifeMap, which sells saju readings in Korean, therefore stopped at three for IU: year 癸酉, month 丁巳, day 丁酉. It did not fill the missing hour with noon, and it did not estimate one.

That leaves a question the reading has to answer honestly, and it is the interesting part: of the things a saju engine outputs, which ones survive not knowing the hour, and which ones move?

## What holds: water, in all twelve hours

The day stem is 丁, and the birth month 巳 is a summer month. KLifeMap's engine puts seasonal balance — jo-hu (調候), the principle set out in the Qiongtong Baojian (窮通寶鑑), also known as the Nangangmang (欄江網) — ahead of both strength and structure for anyone born in summer or winter. On that rule it returns water as IU's governing element.

Then it substituted all twelve possible hour branches, one at a time, holding the calendar date fixed, and re-ran the reading. Water came back twelve times out of twelve. The rule that decides the answer does not depend on the hour, so the answer does not either. That is one judgement the engine will make about IU without knowing something nobody knows.

## What moves: five hours strong, seven weak

The strength reading does not hold. Across the same twelve hours the chart comes out sin-gang (身强), strong, in five of them and sin-yak (身弱), weak, in the other seven. Strength is what an engine leans on to weigh the ten gods and to read the luck cycles, so knowing IU's birth hour would change that part of the reading — and the reading itself says so rather than picking the larger side and calling it settled.

Seven out of twelve is not a finding about IU. It is a measurement of how much of a saju an unknown hour takes away.

## The day pillar was wrong once, and the cause was a clock

The first edition of this reading gave IU's day pillar as 丙申, one place back in the sixty-cycle from 丁酉 — a difference of exactly one day. The engine shifts a Korean clock time back by 32 minutes to approximate true solar time at the longitude of Seoul, and a chart cast at midnight minus 32 minutes lands on the day before. The midnight branch was being represented by 00:30, which crossed that line. We found it while checking the numbers before setting any of them in type; KLifeMap fixed the boundary and republished. The full account, with the twenty-four-hour table for all three charts, is in [our earlier piece on the missing birth hour](/article/iu-jungkook-karina-missing-birth-hour).

## What none of this can tell you

This is a three-pillar reading, not a four-pillar one. Knowing the hour can change it. Public profiles carry dates and almost never hours, which is why no complete chart for any Korean public figure can be built from the public record — ours included.

And a count we published ourselves, on our own data: across 1,047 Korean actors who reached a Netflix chart, the spread across the twelve zodiac years is indistinguishable from chance, chi-square 7.77 against a threshold of 19.68. A birth year does not pick out who reaches a chart, and this article does not say otherwise.
