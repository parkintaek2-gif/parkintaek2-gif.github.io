Two days in a row, we ran the same test on a Korean brand-reputation ranking: does the order the index reports match the order English Wikipedia readers actually looked people up in? Yesterday it didn't. Today it does.

The Korea Corporate Reputation Institute's ranking for September 2026, covering individual members of boy groups, put BTS's Jimin first with a brand reputation index of 3,905,514. BIGBANG's G-Dragon was second at 3,517,368, up 56.04% from the previous month. ATEEZ's San was third at 3,296,639, according to STARNEWS.

## What we measured

We pulled daily English Wikipedia pageviews for all three over the 29 days ending 19 September 2026 — the same window our K-pop reading panel already covers.

| | 29-day average | Highest day | Last 7 days |
|---|---|---|---|
| Jimin (1st in the index) | 2,029 a day | 2,738 on 7 Sept | 12,930 |
| G-Dragon (2nd) | 1,592 a day | 2,496 on 12 Sept | 10,585 |
| San (3rd) | 1,371 a day | 1,908 on 2 Sept | 8,624 |

The order is the same as the reputation index: Jimin ahead of G-Dragon, G-Dragon ahead of San. The gap sizes are not identical between the two measures — the reputation index has Jimin only 11.0% ahead of G-Dragon, while our reading count has him 27.5% ahead — but the ranking itself holds in both.

## Why we're not calling this a pattern

We ran the same comparison the day before this one, on a group-level ranking that put girl group Re:Scene first among K-pop groups, ahead of BTS and BLACKPINK. On English Wikipedia, Re:Scene averaged 11 views a day over a comparable window; BTS averaged 6,860 — about 624 times as many. The reputation order and the reading order were not close.

That comparison and this one used the same method and a similar time window, and they came out differently. We don't have a theory for why a reputation index will sometimes track English-language reading and sometimes not, and two data points is not enough to build one. What we can do is keep running the same test each time one of these rankings comes out, and report what we find rather than what would make a tidier story.

## What we could not measure

- The reputation index's underlying data (Korean-language media mentions, community engagement scores, link and keyword analysis) — we did not verify it and do not redistribute the score itself.
- Any non-English readership. Naver, Korean Wikipedia, and Korean-language social platforms are not part of this measurement.
- Whether either idol's reading rise on their peak day was caused by a specific news event. We report the peak date; we did not trace a cause for it in this piece.
