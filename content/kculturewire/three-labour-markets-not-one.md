Korea's listed content companies are usually discussed as one sector. Their own filings say
otherwise. Split the 248 of them by the industry code each company reports for itself, and three
workforces appear that have almost nothing in common.

| | Companies | Staff | Median tenure | Pay | Women | Company age |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Broadcasting | 12 | 6,393 | **10.3 yrs** | ₩87.0m | 47.5% | 31.5 yrs |
| Film, video and audio | 40 | 10,540 | 7.3 | **₩63.1m** | **56.9%** | 25.7 |
| Publishing (includes games) | 196 | 51,876 | **5.4** | ₩77.4m | 34.1% | 22.5 |
| All 248 together | 248 | 68,809 | 6.2 | ₩76.1m | 38.8% | 23.9 |
| Whole listed market | 2,779 | 1,865,198 | 11.3 | ₩95.1m | 28.0% | 43.5 |

**Broadcasting keeps people almost twice as long as publishing and games**, and it is the only one of
the three that comes near the wider market's 11.3 years.

## Pay does not follow tenure here

The obvious story would be that the industry with the longest tenure pays best and the shortest pays
worst. It is not what the filings show.

Film has the **second-longest tenure and the lowest pay** — ₩63.1m against publishing's ₩77.4m,
while keeping staff two years longer. Whatever holds people in film production, it is not the salary
line, and whatever moves people out of games, it is not a pay gap in the other direction.

All three sit below the listed-market average of ₩95.1m. The content sector does not pay a premium
in either direction; it pays less than the market across all three of its parts.

## The confound we are not hiding

Look at the last column. **These companies are young.** Publishing averages 22.5 years of corporate
existence against the market's 43.5, and a company that has existed for 22 years cannot produce a
30-year employee.

So part of the tenure gap is arithmetic, not behaviour. We tested how much across the fourteen
largest entertainment and game firms in an earlier piece and found tenure and company age correlate
at only 0.30 — enough to matter, not enough to explain the gap. We report age beside tenure rather
than adjusting for it, because an adjustment would hide exactly how much of the difference is
structural.

## The women's column is the sharpest split

Film is **56.9% women**. Publishing and games is 34.1%. That is a 22.8-point gap inside one sector,
and both ends sit above the listed market's 28.0%.

It is also the one figure here that cannot be explained by company age or firm size, and we do not
have anything in these filings that explains it. It is stated because it is large, not because we
can account for it.

## What this is not

These are 248 listed companies. **Most Korean content production is private** — the studios behind
much of what reaches a Netflix Top 10 file nothing here at all. Tenure is what the filing says, not a
measure of whether people want to stay. And "publishing" is a filing category: Korean game companies
report under it, which is why the largest group by staff is named for a form and not for a medium.
