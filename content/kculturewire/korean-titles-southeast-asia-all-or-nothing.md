Southeast Asia is the largest audience for Korean film and television outside Korea itself, and Netflix publishes a weekly Top 10 for six of its markets. Over 265 consecutive weeks — from July 2021 to July 2026 — **405 Korean titles** have appeared on at least one of those six lists.

Sort them by how many of the six countries they reached and the distribution is not a curve. It is two piles.

| Charted in | Titles | Share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| All 6 countries | **180** | **44.4%** |
| 5 | 19 | 4.7% |
| 4 | 22 | 5.4% |
| 3 | 22 | 5.4% |
| 2 | 33 | 8.1% |
| Exactly 1 | **129** | **31.9%** |

Nearly three-quarters of everything sits at one end or the other. The four middle buckets — titles that travelled to some of the region but not most of it — hold **23.7%** between them.

## What the two piles look like

The titles that reach all six do not just reach further, they last longer once there: **8.4 weeks** on average against **2.8** for the single-country titles. Whatever carries a title across the region also keeps it on the chart when it arrives.

At the top of the six-country pile the runs are long by any standard — *Alchemy of Souls* 45 weeks, *Hometown Cha-Cha-Cha* 34, *Squid Game* and *Extraordinary Attorney Woo* 29 each.

## Series travel. Films mostly do not.

Split the 405 by type and the two piles are largely two formats.

| | Titles | Average countries | Average weeks | Reached #1 somewhere |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Series | 238 | **4.60** | 7.7 | 101 |
| Films | 167 | **2.57** | 2.8 | 49 |

A Korean series that charts in Southeast Asia reaches, on average, nearly five of the six markets. A Korean film reaches barely more than two and a half.

Part of that is mechanical and we should say so: a film is one sitting and a series is sixteen episodes, so a series holds a chart slot for longer almost by construction. But the country count is not a runtime artefact. Reaching Indonesia and not Thailand is a separate event from staying on a chart for six weeks, and on that measure the gap is nearly two markets.

## The useful version of this

"Korean content is popular in Southeast Asia" is true and tells you nothing about any particular title. What the chart data says is narrower and more usable: a Korean title released into this region has roughly a **44% chance of behaving as a regional release** and a **32% chance of being a single-market event**, with relatively little chance of the middle outcome that intuition expects.

And 150 of the 405 — **37.0%** — reached number one somewhere in the region at least once. Charting at all in Southeast Asia is hard; charting at the very top, for the titles that get there, is common.
