Ask which Korean cultural export is bigger, music or television, and most people will guess television. The drama series are what the world talks about, and *Squid Game* alone has been watched five billion hours.

The answer is music, and it has been for most of the last decade — but never by much, and not without interruption.

## They started the same size

In 2012 Korea exported **$235.1m** of music and **$233.8m** of broadcast programming. That is a difference of half a percent: for practical purposes, two identical businesses.

| Year | Music | Broadcast | Music ÷ broadcast |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2012 | 235,097 | 233,821 | 1.01 |
| 2014 | 335,650 | 336,019 | 1.00 |
| 2016 | 442,566 | 411,212 | 1.08 |
| 2018 | 564,236 | 478,447 | 1.18 |
| 2020 | 679,633 | 692,790 | **0.98** |
| 2022 | 927,613 | 948,045 | **0.98** |
| 2023 | 1,222,535 | 1,047,219 | 1.17 |
| 2024 | **1,801,449** | **1,257,183** | **1.43** |

*Thousands of US dollars.*

Broadcast was ahead in 2013, again in 2020, and again as recently as **2022**. The lead has changed hands three times in thirteen years. Anyone who declared a winner in 2020 would have been wrong by 2023.

## 2024 is the widest the gap has been

Music finished 2024 at **$1.80bn** against broadcast's **$1.26bn** — a ratio of 1.43, the largest in the series. Music grew 47.4% in that single year; broadcast grew 20.0%.

Over the whole thirteen years music multiplied **7.7 times** and broadcast **5.4 times**. Both are extraordinary numbers for any export category. Music is simply further along the same curve.

Together the two came to **$3.06bn** in 2024, from $468.9m in 2012.

## Why the framing matters

Cultural exports are usually discussed one title at a time — this drama charted in ninety countries, that group sold out a stadium. At that resolution television looks dominant, because a drama is a single object that a great many people watch at once and a song is not.

At the level of what the industries actually sell abroad, the two are close enough that the ranking has flipped three times in thirteen years, and the current gap opened up in the last two of them.

**One caution.** This is not K-pop against K-drama. The music series covers the entire music industry's exports and the broadcast series covers programme sales; each contains activity most readers would not picture under those names, and neither survey separates a genre. What the comparison supports is the size of two trades against each other — not a verdict on which art form travels better.
