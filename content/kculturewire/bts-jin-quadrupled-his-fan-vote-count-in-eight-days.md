STARPOLL is a fan-voting app run by STARNEWS, official partner of the AAA 2026 awards. Its
public "Star Ranking" leaderboard updates continuously, and we archive a snapshot of it roughly
once a week. On 16 September 2026, the Male Idol category read: CORTIS's Sunghyun first, CORTIS's
Kunho second, 2PM's Junho third, CORTIS's Juhoon fourth, and BTS's Jin fifth with 4,574 votes.

We took the next snapshot eight days later, on 24 September. Jin's vote count had become 19,663 —
**4.3 times** what it was — and he had moved past Junho into third place.

## What changed in eight days

| | 16 September | Rank | 24 September | Rank | Change |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sunghyun (CORTIS) | 282,130 | 1 | 356,277 | 1 | +26.3% |
| Kunho (CORTIS) | 228,120 | 2 | 298,801 | 2 | +31.0% |
| Junho (2PM) | 15,240 | 3 | 16,282 | 4 | +6.8% |
| Juhoon (CORTIS) | 7,363 | 4 | 4,142 | 5 | -43.7% |
| Jin (BTS) | 4,574 | 5 | 19,663 | 3 | **+329.9%** |

Every other name in the top five moved by 43.7% or less, in either direction, over the same eight
days. Jin's count more than quadrupled.

## Junho didn't fall — he was passed

It's worth being precise about what moved Junho out of third place. His own vote count rose too,
by 6.8% (1,042 more votes), over the same window — a real gain, not a decline. He dropped a rank
because Jin added 15,089 votes in the same span, roughly 14 times as many votes as Junho added,
not because Junho's own support shrank. The one name in the top five that did shrink in absolute
terms was Juhoon, down 43.7% (3,221 fewer votes).

At the top of the category, nothing shifted. CORTIS's Sunghyun and Kunho both grew their totals
and held first and second — the gap between first place and third place widened over the window,
even as third place itself changed hands.

## What this does and doesn't tell us

A vote total counts people motivated to cast a vote, repeatedly, for a name over a short window —
it is not a measurement of how many people are reading about that name, and we're not
substituting one for the other here. We also can't say *why* Jin's count rose this much this fast:
an award-show appearance, a fan-campaign push, a comeback-adjacent event, or ordinary week-to-week
swings in a vote total could each produce a move this size, and STARPOLL's public page gives us no
way to isolate which. What we can say, narrowly, is what the leaderboard itself recorded twice,
eight days apart: one name in a five-name category multiplied its vote count while the other four
each moved by less than half of that, in either direction.

This is a different vote from the one behind our [68-week streak piece on Jin](/article/68-weeks-of-fan-votes-and-fifth-place-in-lookups)
— that one tracked a separate weekly idol poll against Wikipedia lookups. STARPOLL's Male Idol
leaderboard is its own panel, with its own five names and its own week-to-week movement.
