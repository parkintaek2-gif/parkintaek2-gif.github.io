Netflix has published a global Top 10 every week since July 2021. Over 265 consecutive weeks, **294 Korean titles** have appeared on it, and together they account for **27.7 billion hours viewed**.

That is the headline number people quote. The shape underneath it is more interesting, because it is not a wave. It is a spike.

## One show is a fifth of everything

*Squid Game* has been watched **5.05 billion hours** — **18.2%** of all Korean viewing that reached the global chart in five years. One title out of 294.

Widen it and the concentration barely loosens.

| Titles | Hours | Share of all Korean viewing |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Top 1 | 5.0bn | 18.2% |
| Top 3 | 6.4bn | 23.0% |
| Top 5 | 7.7bn | 27.7% |
| Top 10 | 10.1bn | 36.4% |
| Top 20 | 13.2bn | 47.6% |
| Top 50 | 18.5bn | 66.7% |

Fifty titles — 17% of the catalogue — carry two-thirds of the hours. The remaining 244 share the other third between them.

This is what a hit-driven business looks like from the outside, and it is worth saying plainly because the phrase "the Korean wave" implies breadth. On this measure the breadth is real — 294 titles is a lot of titles — but the viewing is not spread across it.

## Reaching number one doubles the hours. It does not lengthen the run.

Among the 50 biggest titles we know each one's best chart position and how many weeks it stayed. Thirty of them reached number one; twenty peaked lower.

| | Titles | Average hours | Average weeks on chart |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Peaked at #1 | 30 | 472m | 9.5 |
| Peaked below #1 | 20 | 216m | 9.4 |

The hours differ by 2.2 times. The run length differs by one-tenth of a week.

So the top spot is not a foothold that keeps a show on the chart longer. It is a measure of how hard the show landed in the weeks it was already going to have. *Squid Game* is the extreme case: 42 weeks on the chart at 120 million hours per week, where a typical long-runner in this set turns over 15 to 20 million.

The inverse also shows up. *Alchemy of Souls* stayed 27 weeks at 16 million hours a week; *Run Away* managed 60 million a week and was gone in four. Endurance and intensity are separate things, and the chart rewards them separately.

## What this does not tell you

Netflix publishes ten rows a week and nothing below them. A Korean title watched steadily by a large audience that never breaks the top ten does not appear here at all. Everything above is about the top of the chart, which is the part of the market that gets talked about — and, on this evidence, the part where almost all the measured viewing sits.
