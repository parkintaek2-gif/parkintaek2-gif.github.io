Netflix has published a global Top 10 every week since July 2021. Over 265 consecutive weeks, **237 Korean titles** have appeared on it, and together they account for **23.8 billion hours viewed**.

That is the headline number people quote. The shape underneath it is more interesting, because it is not a wave. It is a spike.

## One show is a fifth of everything

*Squid Game* has been watched **5.05 billion hours** — **21.2%** of all Korean viewing that reached the global chart in five years. One title out of 237.

Widen it and the concentration barely loosens.

| Titles | Hours | Share of all Korean viewing |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Top 1 | 5.0bn | 21.2% |
| Top 3 | 6.4bn | 26.7% |
| Top 5 | 7.6bn | 31.8% |
| Top 10 | 9.8bn | 41.1% |
| Top 20 | 12.7bn | 53.2% |
| Top 50 | 17.3bn | 72.7% |

Fifty titles — 21% of the catalogue — carry nearly three-quarters of the hours. The remaining 187 share the rest between them.

This is what a hit-driven business looks like from the outside, and it is worth saying plainly because the phrase "the Korean wave" implies breadth. On this measure the breadth is real — 237 titles is a lot of titles — but the viewing is not spread across it.

## Reaching number one multiplies the hours. It does not lengthen the run.

Among the 50 biggest titles we know each one's best chart position and how many weeks it stayed. Twenty-six of them reached number one; twenty-four peaked lower.

| | Titles | Average hours | Average weeks on chart |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Peaked at #1 | 26 | 485m | 9.8 |
| Peaked below #1 | 24 | 196m | 9.0 |

The hours differ by 2.5 times. The run length differs by less than a week.

So the top spot is not a foothold that keeps a show on the chart longer. It is a measure of how hard the show landed in the weeks it was already going to have. *Squid Game* is the extreme case: 42 weeks on the chart at 120 million hours per week, where a typical long-runner in this set turns over 15 to 30 million.

The inverse also shows up. *Alchemy of Souls* stayed 27 weeks at 16 million hours a week; *Can This Love Be Translated?* managed 54 million a week and was gone in five. Endurance and intensity are separate things, and the chart rewards them separately.

## Almost all of it is television

Split the same 237 titles by Netflix's own chart category and the catalogue is not evenly divided.

| | Titles | Hours | Hours per title |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Series | 151 | 22.29bn | 148m |
| Films | 86 | 1.53bn | 18m |

Korea puts films on the global chart nearly as often as it puts series there — 86 against 151 — and gets **one-fifteenth** of the hours from them. Twenty-two of those 86 films reached number one, a rate barely below the series rate, so the films are not failing to get noticed. They are gone in 2.4 weeks against 5.3. That comparison has [its own page](/screen-split).

## What this does not tell you

Netflix publishes ten rows a week and nothing below them. A Korean title watched steadily by a large audience that never breaks the top ten does not appear here at all. Everything above is about the top of the chart, which is the part of the market that gets talked about — and, on this evidence, the part where almost all the measured viewing sits.
