STARPOLL is a fan-voting app run by STARNEWS, official partner of the AAA 2026 awards. Its
public leaderboard, "Star Ranking," updates continuously and is visible without logging in — only
casting a vote requires an account. On 16 September 2026, its Female Idol category read: Minji
(NewJeans) first with 18,270 votes, Haerin (NewJeans) second with 15,716, and Red Velvet's Irene
third with 14,811.

**A fan vote counts people motivated to vote, repeatedly, for a name. It is not a measurement of
how many people look that name up.** We hold a different number for that: English Wikipedia
pageviews, which we've used all year to check reported K-pop news against actual reading. So we
checked whether the two numbers agree on who's "first."

They don't.

## What we measured

We pulled 30 days of daily English Wikipedia pageviews (17 August-15 September 2026, the day
before the vote snapshot) for every name in STARPOLL's top five we could match to an individual
page.

| | STARPOLL vote (16 Sep) | Vote rank | 30-day Wikipedia total | 30-day rank |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Minji | 18,270 | 1 | 10,786 | 2 |
| Haerin | 15,716 | 2 | 8,427 | 3 |
| Irene | 14,811 | 3 | **12,257** | **1** |
| Hyein | 12,491 | 5 | 8,241 | 4 |

Irene placed third in the vote, behind two NewJeans members. On our reading count, she leads all
three — 1.14x Minji's total, the vote's actual first-place name. The gap is real but not large;
we're not overstating it.

We could not match STARPOLL's fourth-place name in this category to a specific English Wikipedia
page with confidence, so we've left that row out rather than guess.

## The men's category — a wider gap, running the other way

STARPOLL's Male Idol vote on the same day: Sunghyun and Kunho, both of CORTIS, took first and
second by a wide margin (282,130 and 228,120 votes), ahead of 2PM's Junho, CORTIS's Juhoon, and —
fifth — BTS's Jin, with 4,574 votes.

Jin is the only one of the five with his own English Wikipedia page in our panel: 55,153 views
over the same 30 days, or 1,838 a day — roughly 12 times his own vote count, and higher than any
of the four women's totals above. We have no individual figure for Sunghyun, Kunho, Junho or
Juhoon to set next to his; none of the four has an individual page in our panel yet.

At the group level, the pattern reverses from the women's category. BTS's page drew 218,592 views
over the same window — CORTIS's drew 58,269, about a quarter of that. CORTIS's two members took
the vote's top two spots by a wide margin; BTS's only vote-ranked member placed fifth with a small
fraction of their votes. The reading gap between the groups runs opposite to the vote gap between
their members.

## What this does and doesn't tell us

A fan vote and a pageview count measure different behaviors, and neither one is "the real"
popularity. In the women's category, the gap between the two numbers was narrow. In the men's
category it was wide, and pointed the other way at group level. We're not resolving either into
one ranking. We're reporting what each number actually counts, and where they agree and where
they don't.
