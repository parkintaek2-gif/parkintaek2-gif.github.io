Pick one ordinary day. On 10 September 2026 the two Korean entertainment desks in our
archive published 99 headlines between them. Strip out the ones with nothing countable and
22 remain, and those 22 name **19 people**.

We asked one question about those 19: does the English-speaking world look them up at all?

## Sixteen of eighteen are findable. One is not, and one we cannot confirm

Eighteen of the 19 could be tested — the nineteenth is set aside below. Of those eighteen,
**16 have an English Wikipedia article we could verify and count.**

**3,005,232 lookups. Median 101,338.** Top to bottom of the measured list is **267 times**.

The two at the bottom of the table are different kinds of gap, and we keep them apart.
**Yoon Ga-i** returned nothing, and two separate searches found no English article under any
spelling we tried — as far as we can measure, she does not exist in English. **Wang
Yen-cheng** does have an article, under `Wang Yanzheng`, but it carries no Korea, K-pop or
KBO category, so our check could not confirm the article is the pitcher the Korean desk
wrote about. We publish him as unconfirmed rather than counting a number we cannot vouch for.

## The day tilts to actors two to one. Attention abroad barely tilts at all

Sorted by what the desk was writing about that day:

| Kind | People named | Verified | Lookups | Per person |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Actors | 8 | 7 | 1,817,029 | **259,576** |
| K-pop | 4 | 4 | 921,600 | **230,400** |
| Sport | 3 | 2 | 186,994 | 93,497 |
| Other musicians | 2 | 2 | 76,706 | 38,353 |
| TV personality | 1 | 1 | 2,903 | 2,903 |

Actors are **twice as many names** as idols in that day's copy, and their lookup total is
**1.97 times** the idol total. That looks like a story about actors dominating.

It is mostly a story about headcount. Divide by the number of people and the gap is
**1.13 times** — 259,576 against 230,400. Eight actors and four idols were named; per
person, the world looks them up at nearly the same rate.

If you read only the aggregate column you would conclude Korean actors draw twice the
foreign attention of Korean idols. They do not, on this day, by this count. The desk simply
wrote about more of them.

## What the single largest number is

It is an actor, not an idol: Son Ye-jin at 776,348, ahead of Jin at 670,861. Third is
Hyun Bin at 618,858 — and the day's two largest names are married to each other, each
appearing in the other's orbit in the same morning's copy.

We are not going to build a theory on one day. That is the point of dating the file: this
is 10 September 2026's cast list, not a ranking of Korean entertainment.

## Two things went wrong before this published

**An empty cell is not an absence.** Two names first came back with nothing: our guessed
article titles, `BamBam (rapper)` and `Lee Sang-woo (actor, born 1980)`, do not exist. The
real titles are the bare `BamBam` and `Lee Sang-woo`, and once asked properly they returned
107,896 and 53,013. Had we published the first run, two people who are perfectly findable
in English would have been reported as invisible.

**Our own verification check excluded a correct person.** To stop a redirect landing on a
different human, every article has to prove it belongs to this subject before its number
counts. We first wrote that test as "is this person South Korean" — and it threw out
BamBam, who is Thai and works in a Korean group. The test was measuring nationality when
the axis is the Korean entertainment desk. We widened it to accept Korea, K-pop and KBO
categories and ran the whole thing again.

Both fixes changed the headline numbers in this piece. Neither was caught by the data
looking wrong; they were caught by asking whether an empty cell had earned its emptiness.

## What this does not tell you

Lookups are lookups. They cannot separate a fan from someone who read a name in a headline
five seconds earlier, and they say nothing about records sold, tickets moved or fees paid.

Our check confirms that a verified article is about a Korean-entertainment figure. It cannot
confirm the article is about *the* person in the headline — several of these romanisations
are shared by more than one Korean public figure, and where the ambiguity was flat-out
unresolvable, as with Park Ji-yoon, we left the name out entirely instead of picking one.

And the sample is one day of two outlets. A different day, or a desk that covers film
instead of television, would name a different 19 people. The method is the durable part;
the cast list is not.
