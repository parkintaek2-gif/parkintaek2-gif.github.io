Netflix has published a per-country weekly top 10 for **268 weeks**. In that time **94 countries**
have handed the number one place to a Korean title at least once, and **84 different Korean titles**
have taken it somewhere.

Both of those numbers sound like a wide, moving picture. Counted per country, it is not.

**The median country has given number one to two Korean titles. Ever.**

## Eight countries have a rotating cast. The rest do not

| Country | Distinct Korean titles that reached number one |
| --- | ---: |
| South Korea | 37 |
| Vietnam | 12 |
| Indonesia | 8 |
| Thailand | 8 |
| Hong Kong | 8 |
| Malaysia | 7 |
| Singapore | 6 |
| Japan | 5 |

That is the complete list of countries with five or more. **All eight are in Asia.** The other 86
countries sit at four or fewer, and half of them at two or fewer.

In **eight countries the only Korean title that has ever been number one is Squid Game**.

## Outside Korea, nobody holds it for long

Korea's own series chart kept a Korean title at number one for 87 consecutive weeks. Vietnam managed
34, Indonesia 25, Thailand 21 — and in every one of those runs the place changed hands repeatedly.
Vietnam's 34 weeks passed through eleven titles.

Outside Korea, the longest any **single** Korean title has held number one is **eight weeks** —
Squid Game in Germany, from 26 September 2021. Seven-week reigns happened in **fifteen more
countries** — Bahrain, Bolivia, Bulgaria, Croatia, Cyprus, Estonia, Finland, Greece, Romania,
Serbia, Slovenia, South Africa, Spain, the United Arab Emirates and Venezuela. Every one of them is
Squid Game, and fourteen of the fifteen start in the same week — 26 September 2021. The fifteenth
starts a week later.

In **155 of the 180 country-and-category runs we measured, one title accounts for the whole run.**
Squid Game is that title in 70 of them.

## What the two shapes mean

They are not the same event described twice.

A relay — Vietnam, Indonesia, Korea — means the audience is there continuously and the catalogue
keeps feeding it. A single reign means a title arrived, took the top place, and nothing Korean
replaced it.

Most of the world has had the second kind. When a headline says Korean titles dominate Netflix, it
is usually describing eight countries.

## What this does not say

It says nothing about hours watched. A number one place in Iceland and a number one place in Brazil
count the same here, and they are not the same audience. It also says nothing about titles that
charted without reaching the top — most Korean titles never do.
