**Faker** and T1 are now 2-1 in the Swiss Stage ([their win over CTBC Flying Oyster](/article/t1-beat-ctbc-flying-oyster-2-1-worlds-2026-nine-of-twelve-qualified)). Who is reading about him?

His Wikipedia article exists in 24 languages. We compared each edition's reading on the first two full days of the Swiss Stage, 24 and 25 October, with its usual day in early October.

## The rise, edition by edition

| Edition | Usual day | 24 Oct | 25 Oct | Times usual |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| German | 64 | 282 | 229 | **3.99** |
| Polish | 24 | 80 | 67 | 3.06 |
| Spanish | 111 | 381 | 296 | 3.05 |
| Italian | 22 | 67 | 55 | 2.77 |
| Portuguese | 32 | 109 | 67 | 2.75 |
| French | 142 | 444 | 256 | 2.46 |
| Turkish | 20 | 40 | 39 | 1.98 |
| Russian | 44 | 87 | 74 | 1.83 |
| English | 1,203 | 2,406 | 1,871 | 1.78 |
| Vietnamese | 93 | 115 | 105 | 1.18 |
| Chinese | 532 | 622 | 605 | 1.15 |
| Japanese | 164 | 171 | 149 | 0.98 |
| Korean | 314 | 270 | 225 | **0.79** |

The typical edition read him at **about twice** its usual rate (median 1.98 times). The spread runs from almost four times in German to below usual in Korean.

## Two different kinds of reader

The editions that rose most are European ones with small usual audiences. On a usual day German Wikipedia reads Faker 64 times; on 24 October it read him 282 times. That pattern fits readers who look a player up when a tournament puts him in front of them.

The Asian editions barely moved. Chinese, Japanese and Vietnamese stayed within 18% of usual, and Korean dropped. These editions already read him every day: Chinese Wikipedia's usual day is 532 reads, larger than every European edition's peak.

English is in between: the largest edition by far, and up 1.78 times.

## What this does not say

Wikipedia reads count look-ups, not fans or viewers. Most Korean readers use Namuwiki, which publishes no counts, so a falling Korean figure says nothing about interest in Korea. Two days is a short window, and the ratio for an edition with 20 or 30 usual reads moves a lot with a few readers. Team-by-team reading and the full language breakdown are on [our Worlds 2026 page](/worlds-2026).
