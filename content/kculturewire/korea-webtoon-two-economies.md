Korea's webtoon business had 396 companies in it in 2024, and between them they average **₩8.06 billion** of webtoon revenue each.

That number is true and it describes almost nobody. **76.4% of these businesses earn under ₩1 billion.** The mean sits eight times above the bracket that three-quarters of the industry lives in, because a small group at the top is very large.

## Two economies, sharing a name

The survey splits the industry two ways: **platforms**, who distribute, and **content providers**, who make the work. There are 28 of the first and 368 of the second.

| | Businesses | Average webtoon revenue | Earning under ₩1bn | Earning ₩10bn+ |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Platforms | 28 | **₩94.5bn** | 51.8% | 39.3% |
| Content providers | 368 | **₩1.5bn** | 78.3% | 5.3% |
| All | 396 | ₩8.1bn | 76.4% | 7.7% |

A platform averages **63 times** what a content provider averages. Seven per cent of the businesses account for the shape of the whole distribution.

Break it down by what a company actually does and the same line appears in a different place.

| Main business | Businesses | Average revenue |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Webtoon service provision | 31 | ₩85.3bn |
| Distribution and brokerage | 65 | ₩1.6bn |
| Planning and production | **247** | **₩1.4bn** |
| Publishing | 28 | ₩1.5bn |
| Other | 24 | ₩2.0bn |

**247 of the 396 businesses — the clear majority — are the ones that plan and produce the comics.** They average ₩1.4 billion. The 31 that provide the service average sixty times that.

This is not an accusation of anything. Distribution businesses are capital-heavy and serve the whole market at once; production studios are small by nature. But it is worth stating plainly, because "the Korean webtoon industry" is usually described with a single number, and a single number here averages two economies that do not resemble each other.

## The smaller you are, the more exposed you are

The survey asks a second question: what share of your total revenue comes from webtoons?

| | 75%+ from webtoons | Under 25% from webtoons |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Under 10 staff (214) | **67.5%** | 21.3% |
| 10–49 staff (129) | 55.8% | 26.3% |
| 50+ staff (53) | **36.1%** | 35.4% |

Two-thirds of the businesses with fewer than ten people get three-quarters or more of everything they earn from webtoons. Among firms of fifty or more, only a third do.

So the companies with the least cushion are the ones with the least diversification. If webtoon demand moves, it moves through the smallest firms first and hardest — and those firms are 214 of the 396.

## Why this number is worth having

Webtoons are cited constantly as the next Korean cultural export, usually with a market-size figure attached. The market size is real. What the size does not tell you is that the industry behind it is 28 large distributors and several hundred small studios, and that most of the studios are earning under a billion won while carrying nearly all of their risk in one product.

Counting the businesses rather than the market is the difference between knowing an industry is big and knowing who is in it.
