In 2012, 80.6% of everything Korea's music industry exported went to Japan. It was less a global business than a bilateral one with a large neighbour.

In 2024 Japan's share was **35.5%**.

The obvious reading is that Korea lost Japan. It did not. Japanese sales in 2024 were **$639.2m**, up from **$367.3m** in 2018 — a 1.7-fold increase over six years. Japan grew the whole time. It just stopped being most of the total, because the rest of the world grew far faster.

## Twenty years

| 2011 | 196,113 | 80.5% | 3.5% | 13.1% | 0.3% | 2.4% |
| 2015 | 381,023 | 63.6% | 23.6% | 10.6% | 0.3% | 1.3% |
| 2018 | 564,236 | 65.1% | 19.8% | 12.3% | 1.3% | 1.2% |
| 2019 | 756,198 | 51.5% | 15.5% | 17.1% | 10.6% | 3.0% |
| 2021 | 775,274 | 40.1% | 18.9% | 18.1% | 14.7% | 4.4% |
| 2023 | 1,222,535 | 35.1% | 26.1% | 12.9% | 14.4% | 3.5% |
| 2024 | **1,801,449** | **35.5%** | 21.1% | 14.4% | 15.4% | 7.4% |

Total exports went from **$22.3m to $1.80bn** — 81 times over 19 years, 26.0% a year compounded. Nothing in the table is flat.

## The break is 2019, and it is North America

Look at the North America column. It sits at 0.3% of exports in 2011 and 1.3% in 2018 — a rounding error on a business worth half a billion dollars. In 2019 it is 10.6%, and in 2024 it is 15.4%.

In money: **$7.2m in 2018 to $278.1m in 2024, a 39-fold increase in six years.** Europe over the same span went from $7.0m to $133.2m, 19-fold.

Set those next to Japan's 1.7-fold and the shape of the change is clear. This was not a pivot away from a saturated market. Japan kept growing at a perfectly respectable rate for a mature market; two immature markets went up by an order of magnitude and reset the denominator.

## What the concentration number was hiding

There is a general point here worth keeping. "Japan is 80% of K-pop exports" was true in 2012, and a reader could reasonably have drawn from it that the business was fragile — one market, one set of tastes, one currency.

The fragility was real, but it was not resolved the way the number implied it would be. Nobody had to lose Japan for the concentration to fall. A share is a ratio, and ratios move when either side moves. Watching only the share would have told you Korea's dependence halved; watching the underlying values tells you Japan nearly doubled while the world multiplied.

The 2024 figure is the one to sit with: exports grew **47.4% in a single year**, from $1.22bn to $1.80bn, and every region in the table grew with it.
