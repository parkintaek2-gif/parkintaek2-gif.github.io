A title that travels sits on several country charts at once, and that number rises and then falls.
The week it is highest is the week the title is at its widest — and that is the week everything
behind it has already done its work.

We have measured [what an opening position buys](/opening) and [how long a title
holds](/run-length). This is the other timing question: **when does a title reach its widest, and is
there anything left to push after that?**

## Almost never in week one, if it is Korean

| | Titles | Median peak week | Widest in week 1 | Widest by week 4 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Korean series | 209 | 2 | **20.1%** | 80.4% |
| Every other series | 1,990 | 2 | **44.7%** | 92% |
| Korean films | 109 | 2 | 29.4% | 82.6% |
| Every other film | 3,231 | 2 | 36.5% | 78.6% |

Other series are at their widest on the first Monday **44.7%** of the time. Korean series manage it
**20.1%** of the time — less than half as often.

That is a large gap and the obvious explanation is not the interesting one.

## Most of it is length, and we can show that

Korean series [last longer than other series](/run-length) — 3.72 weeks against 2.71. A longer run
has more later weeks in it, so peaking late is partly just having a late to peak in.

So the same measurement, inside bands of equal run length:

| Weeks on a chart | Korean series | Every other series |
|---|---:|---:|
| 1–2 weeks | 50% *(6 titles)* | 83.5% *(461)* |
| 3–4 weeks | 38.1% *(21)* | 46.2% *(504)* |
| 5–8 weeks | 26.6% *(79)* | 28.6% *(567)* |
| 9 weeks or more | **9.7%** *(103)* | **24%** *(458)* |

**The gap narrows sharply, and in the middle it nearly disappears.** Among titles that lasted five to
eight weeks, Korean series peak in week one 26.6% of the time and other series 28.6% — a difference
of two points, which is nothing.

So most of the raw 20.1%-against-44.7% gap is length: Korean series are concentrated in the long
bands, where everybody peaks late.

**But it does not vanish everywhere.** Among titles that lasted nine weeks or more, Korean series
peak in week one **9.7%** of the time against **24%** for other series — still two and a half times
apart, on 103 titles against 458. The long Korean series really do keep widening after their first
week in a way that other long series do not.

⚠ The top band is six Korean titles. We are printing it because leaving a row out of a table is its
own kind of claim, but nothing should be read from it.

## What this is worth knowing for

Eight in ten Korean series have reached their widest point **by week four**. That is the window.
Inside it, the median Korean series is widest in **week two**, not week one — so a push timed to the
first Monday is timed to a week that, four times in five, is not the widest one.

After week four there is very little left: the remaining fifth is spread across everything from week
five to week 34.

## What this cannot tell you

**Width is a count of charts, not of people.** A title can be in more countries and be watched less,
and a title one place outside a top 10 is invisible here at any level of viewing.

It also cannot say what made a title widen. A marketing push, a holiday, a dubbing release and a
cast member in the news all look identical in a rank table. The peak week is when it happened, not
why.

And this is a distribution, not a rule. Some Korean series are widest in week one; some are widest
in week thirty-four. The table says how often, not what will happen to any one title.
