*Crash Landing on You* held a place in **Japan's** Netflix series top 10 for **72 weeks**. No Korean
title has spent longer in any single country's top 10 in the five years Netflix has been publishing
these lists.

Two numbers have to sit beside that one or it says something untrue.

The 72 weeks are spread across a **96-week** stretch of calendar — from the first week we hold,
2021-07-04, to 2023-04-30. And the longest **unbroken** run inside them is **53 weeks**. So it left
the chart, came back, and left again; it was not there for a year and a half straight.

## The ten longest, with the span beside them

| Korean title | Country | Weeks in top 10 | Weeks it spans | Longest unbroken |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Crash Landing on You | Japan | **72** | 96 | 53 |
| Itaewon Class | Japan | 64 | 71 | 24 |
| Boys Over Flowers | Bolivia | 49 | 51 | 35 |
| Vincenzo | Pakistan | 45 | 53 | 25 |
| Squid Game | India | 43 | **201** | 25 |
| Alchemy of Souls | Thailand | 42 | 45 | 29 |
| Squid Game | Pakistan | 39 | 202 | 19 |
| Reply 1988 | Vietnam | 39 | 76 | 15 |
| Squid Game | Bangladesh | 37 | 201 | 17 |
| Alchemy of Souls | Nigeria | 37 | 38 | 36 |

Read the last two columns against the first and the table splits in two.

*Itaewon Class* in Japan, *Boys Over Flowers* in Bolivia and *Alchemy of Souls* in Nigeria are
essentially continuous: the span barely exceeds the count. Nigeria's 37 weeks sit inside a 38-week
window.

*Squid Game* in India is the opposite. Its 43 weeks are spread over **201 weeks** — nearly four
years, from September 2021 to July 2025. Twenty-five of them are unbroken; the other eighteen are
scattered across the years that followed. A sentence that turned that into "43 straight weeks in
India's top 10" would be describing something that did not happen.

## Why the two Japanese titles are at the top

*Crash Landing on You* and *Itaewon Class* both reach these numbers in **Japan**, and both are from
2019–2020 — they were old shows by the time this window opens. That is the shape of the Japanese
chart in this data rather than a fact about those two shows: Japan's series list turns over slowly,
and a Korean title that gets into it tends to stay near it for a long time.

We are not going to explain why Japan behaves that way. Release timing, licensing, how much else
Netflix Japan puts out in a week and how many people are watching would all produce the same
pattern, and the file cannot separate them.

## The largest counter in the whole file is not Korean

It is *Lottie Dottie Chicken* — a Brazilian children's title — at **133** weeks in Brazil, spread
over 166 with an unbroken run of 82. Children's titles sit at the top of this particular measure in
most countries, because they are watched again and again rather than watched once.

That is worth knowing before treating a long counter as a mark of impact. **The field rewards being
re-watched, not being talked about.**

## The number this article is built on needs reading carefully

The weeks-in-top-10 field is a **cumulative** count, not a streak: on consecutive weeks it adds
exactly one, and after a gap it keeps counting 98% of the time rather than restarting. We measured
that separately, and it is
[the reason every span and streak in the table above is computed by us](/article/netflix-weeks-in-top-10-is-not-weeks-in-a-row)
from the weeks themselves.

One more limit, and it cuts against the headline: our window opens on **2021-07-04**, the first week
Netflix published these lists. Every counter in the file starts at 1 there. *Crash Landing on You*
was released in December 2019, so if it was in Japan's top 10 before July 2021 — and it very likely
was — those weeks are not in the 72. **The figure is a floor, not a total.**
