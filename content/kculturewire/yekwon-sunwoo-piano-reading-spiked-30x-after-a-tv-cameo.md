Yekwon Sunwoo, the pianist who won the gold medal at the 2017 Van Cliburn International Piano
Competition, averaged 14.8 English Wikipedia reads a day for the nine days before 6 September, 2026.
On 6 September, his reading hit 315. The day after, it hit 447 — 30.2 times his baseline, the
largest single-cause multiple we have recorded in this series so far.

## What happened

Seoul Economic Daily reported that Sunwoo made a surprise cameo in the fourth episode of "Four
Hands," a Korean television drama built around classical music, broadcast 6 September. In the scene,
he played Franz Liszt's piano arrangement of Schubert's "Gretchen am Spinnrade" ("Gretchen at the
Spinning Wheel"). The same report notes Sunwoo's solo album "Liszt," released in May, was being
pressed to vinyl with preorders opening two days later, on 8 September — a separate piece of news
that lands after the reading had already peaked.

| Day | Views | Multiple of the 14.8/day baseline |
|---|---|---|
| 28 Aug-5 Sep average (baseline) | 14.8 | 1.0&times; |
| 6 September (episode aired) | 315 | 21.3&times; |
| 7 September (day after) | 447 | 30.2&times; |

## Why the timing points at the cameo, not the vinyl news

The reading rose on the broadcast date itself and peaked the day after — both before the 8 September
preorder announcement. A classical pianist who is not a regular television presence appearing,
unannounced, inside a mainstream drama is the kind of single, dateable event this series looks for:
one audience (English Wikipedia readers, likely including viewers curious who the real musician
behind the scene was), one clear trigger, no competing news in the same 48 hours that we found.
