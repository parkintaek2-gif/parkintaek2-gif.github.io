U-Know Yunho, half of TVXQ, is in the middle of his first solo tour of Asia: seven cities, Seoul in July
through Hong Kong on 26 September. Tonight is Taipei. Jakarta is next week.

Every outlet has the dates. We wanted a different number: **in the languages those seven cities actually
speak, how many people open an encyclopedia article about him on an average day?**

## The answer, ranked

| Language | Views a day | Tour cities that speak it |
|---|---:|---|
| English | 120.7 | Singapore |
| Chinese | 75.9 | Macau, Singapore, Taipei, Hong Kong |
| Korean | 27.6 | Seoul |
| Thai | 8.3 | Bangkok |
| Indonesian | 6.4 | Jakarta |
| Portuguese | 2.4 | Macau |
| Malay | no article | Singapore |
| Tamil | no article | Singapore |
| Cantonese | no article | Hong Kong |

Daily average, 5 August to 3 September 2026.

## What stands out

**English reads him most, and English is official in one stop out of seven.** 120.7 views a day, against
6.4 in Indonesian — 18.9 times as many — and Jakarta is a stop while no English-speaking city is,
except Singapore, where English shares official status with three other languages.

**Korean is third.** His own language runs 27.6 a day, well below Chinese at 75.9. The Chinese figure is
one well shared by three of the seven cities, which is part of why it is large; we would rather say that
than let the table imply three separate audiences.

**Three languages have no article at all.** Malay, Tamil and Cantonese carry nothing about him. In
Singapore, two of the four official languages are blank. In Hong Kong, the Chinese edition covers him but
the Cantonese one does not.

That last row is the one we refuse to write as a zero. "No article" and "an article nobody opens" are
different facts, and folding them together is the fastest way to produce a confident wrong number.

## What this is not

It is not ticket sales. It is not attendance. It is not popularity, and it is certainly not an argument
that the tour picked the wrong cities — venues, promoters, visas and who will actually buy a ticket decide
that, and we can measure none of them.

What we can say is narrower and still worth knowing: **the readership is largest in a language that one
stop on this tour speaks.** For an artist whose Korean readership is 23 percent of his English one, that is
a fact about where the audience already is, sitting next to a schedule about where the stage will be.
