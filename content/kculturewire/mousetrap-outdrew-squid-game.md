On **29 August 2026**, the English Wikipedia article for *Mousetrap* (들쥐) — the Korean series with **Sul Kyung-gu, Ryu Jun-yeol and Lee Kyu-hyung** — was opened **23,512 times**.

That put it at **rank 157 of every article on English Wikipedia that day**, out of roughly six million.

Two days earlier it had been opened **658 times**.

## The two days

| Date | Reads | Change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 26 Aug | 522 | — |
| 27 Aug | 658 | ×1.3 |
| **28 Aug** | **13,190** | **×20.0** |
| **29 Aug** | **23,512** | ×1.8 · world rank 157 |
| 30 Aug | 21,033 | ×0.9 · world rank 186 |

**Thirty-six times its Wednesday, by Friday.**

## Against the benchmark everyone uses

*Squid Game* is still the reference point for how big a Korean title can get. Over the same thirty days, its English Wikipedia article was opened **82,425 times in total**, and its single best day was **5,205**.

**One day of *Mousetrap* was 4.5 times *Squid Game*'s best day of the month.**

That is not a claim that *Mousetrap* is bigger than *Squid Game*. A seven-year-old show sits at a settled baseline; a new one is at its opening peak, and the peak always wins that comparison. What it does show is the size of the spike: a Korean series can still move a global encyclopedia by an order of magnitude in forty-eight hours.

## It was not alone

*Mousetrap* was one of **two Korean articles in the world's thousand most-read** on each of those days.

- **29 August** — *Mousetrap* at #157 (23,512) and *Hope*, the 2026 Korean film, at #895 (8,610)
- **30 August** — *Mousetrap* at #186 (21,033) and *Four Hands, Two Sonatas* (포핸즈) at #340 (15,420)

*Four Hands, Two Sonatas* ran the same pattern one day behind: 1,425 on 27 August, 15,420 on 30 August — **ten times over in three days**.

**Two out of a thousand.** Korean culture's share of the world's daily reading attention, on the days two Korean series were breaking out, was 0.2%. Both figures are true at once, and the second one is the part that rarely gets written down.

## How this was counted, and what broke while we counted it

We took Wikimedia's top-1,000 list for each day, resolved every ranked article to its Wikidata item, and asked whether that item's country of origin or its subject's citizenship is South Korea.

**Our first pass got it wrong in both directions, and it is worth saying how.** We resolved article titles with redirects turned on, which silently swapped the title we asked about for the title we landed on — so *Mousetrap (TV series)* came back attached to the ordinary English word *Mousetrap*, and checking that word told us the article was about a device for catching mice. It is: they are two different pages. The Korean series is at `Mousetrap_(TV_series)`, and that is the page holding rank 157.

The lesson is narrow and we are keeping it: **check the exact identifier the data gave you, never the name you would have typed.** A hand-retyped title dropped a parenthesis and nearly lost us the whole story.

## Where this fits

If you follow K-drama, *Mousetrap* breaking out is news you already have. What this page adds is the **denominator** — where it sits among everything the English-reading world looked up that day, and what the previous benchmark actually measures when you put the two side by side in the same month.

Nobody publishes that number, so we counted it.
