On 11 September 2026 we published [a count of Seventeen's thirteen members against their own
group's Wikipedia page](/article/seventeen-thirteen-members-none-close-to-the-group), and ended the window there on purpose: English Wikipedia's pageview data
is not final for a day or two, so the day of Hoshi's injury report — 12 September — was not yet
measurable. We said a follow-up would have something to compare it against. It does now.

## The baseline, and the one day that broke it

| | 11-day baseline (1-11 Sep), views/day | 12 September | Change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Hoshi's page | 183.7 | 551 | 3.0x |
| Seventeen's group page | 1,099.5 | 1,054 | -4% |

Hoshi's individual page nearly tripled against its own recent average on the day Star News
reported his torn anterior cruciate ligament, roughly six months before his scheduled discharge
from military service. The group's own page did not rise at all — it landed 4% under its
11-day baseline, which is inside the day-to-day noise this data normally shows.

## The injury moved one page's share of the other, on a single day

Our 12 September piece measured Hoshi at 17.3% of the group page's own traffic across a full
twelve-month window, and this 11-day baseline lands at 16.7% — close enough to call it the same
number measured two ways. On the injury day itself, that share more than tripled: Hoshi's page
drew 52.3% of what the group page drew, the highest single-day share in the data we hold for him.

Both titles were checked to resolve without a redirect mismatch before anything here was counted,
the same check applied in the 12 September piece.

## What this does and doesn't show

A pageview is a lookup, not a sentiment. This does not say whether readers were concerned,
curious, or simply following a story already moving through Korean entertainment press — English
Wikipedia's pageview count cannot separate those motives, and we do not guess at them. What it
does show, cleanly, is that the attention landed on the person's own page and not on the group's,
even though the story was reported as news about a member of a specific group. Whether that
elevated level holds for a second day, or fades, is a question the next day's data will answer —
not this one.
