IU released her first single at 15. Im Yoon-ah of Girls' Generation debuted at 17, Bae Suzy of
miss A at 16, Cha Eun-woo of ASTRO at 19. Ma Dong-seok did not start until he was 32.

Across the Indonesian, Vietnamese, Thai and Malay Wikipedias, the ones who started young are read
far more today. The median Korean star who debuted before 18 draws **11.89** reads per million
reads of those editions. The median one who started at 26 or later draws **2.43** — almost five
times less.

Then there is Go Youn-jung, who started at 23 and is read more than anyone else in the panel.

## The ladder

We can date the start of a career for 584 Korean stars. Sorted by how old they were, the middle of
each group looks like this:

| Debut age | Stars | Median reads per million | Most-read in that band |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Under 18 | 105 | 11.89 | IU, 208.57 |
| 18 to 21 | 253 | 6.68 | Cha Eun-woo of ASTRO, 200.33 |
| 22 to 25 | 159 | 4.80 | Go Youn-jung, 273.06 |
| 26 or older | 67 | 2.43 | Ma Dong-seok, 59.84 |

Every step falls. From end to end it is 4.89 times.

## The obvious objection does not hold

Someone who started at 15 has had more years to collect readers than someone who started at 32. It
is the first thing anyone would say, and it is testable, so we tested it.

The median career length is **22 years** for the under-18 band, 22 for the 18-to-21 band, 20 for
22-to-25, and 22 for those who began at 26 or later. Within two years of itself, everywhere. These
are not young stars being compared against veterans. They are people who have all been working
about as long, who started at different ages.

We went further and held career length inside a single window — only stars whose careers run 10 to
20 years — and measured again. The ladder survives at 4.63 times. One thing does change: the two
youngest bands come together, 13.42 against 12.65, so it becomes three steps rather than four.

## What it does not tell you about any one star

A median describes a group. The question a reader usually has is about a person.

Take one star at random from the under-18 band and one from the 26-and-over band. How often is the
younger starter the more-read one? **72.7 per cent** of the time. Fifty would mean debut age tells
you nothing about an individual; a hundred would make it a rule. This is neither.

Go Youn-jung is the proof. She started at 23, in the second-lowest band, and at 273.06 reads per
million she is read more than IU, more than Cha Eun-woo of ASTRO, more than anyone we measured.
Kim Seon-ho also started at 23 and sits at 220.56. Both of them are above every single person in
the band that is supposed to be ahead.

This figure has a name: the common-language effect size, proposed by McGraw and Wong in 1992 for
exactly this problem — group differences reported in units nobody can picture. It equals the
Mann-Whitney U statistic, from 1947, divided by the number of pairs.

## Which way it runs

We do not know.

Starting young may build a readership that lasts. Or the kind of person who is put on stage at 15
may already be the kind of person a company invests in for twenty years. Or a third thing — an
agency, a training system, one breakout role — may produce both the early start and the readers.
Nothing here separates them.

The bands are ours: we cut at 18, 22 and 26 because those splits leave every band with enough
people to measure, and a different cut would give different medians. Reads count people opening an
encyclopaedia article, which is not fame and not income. And a start date on Wikidata is the date
of a first credited work, which is not always the date someone would name themselves.

The four bands, the career-length control and the workings are on
[when they started](https://www.kculturewire.com/debut-age).
