*Four Hands, Two Sonatas* (포핸즈) entered the **thousand most-read articles on English Wikipedia** on 30 August, at rank 340 with 15,420 reads — one of only two Korean articles in that list that day.

It is set in an arts high school.

Those schools exist, and a surprising share of the people whose names you already know went to one.

## The seven arts high schools

Of the 4,535 Korean entertainers in our roster with a school on record, **366 attended an arts high school**.

| School | People | Names you may know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| School of Performing Arts Seoul | 125 | **Jisoo**, **Jungkook**, Kang Seul-gi |
| Hanlim Multi Art School | 123 | Kim Da-hyun, **Cha Eun-woo**, Krystal Jung |
| Anyang Arts High School | 42 | Oh Yeon-seo, Park So-yeon, Im Soo-hyang |

| Sunhwa Arts High School | 16 | Sumi Jo, Moon Chae-won, Park Han-byul |

**Two schools hold 248 of the 366** — two thirds of everyone in this category went to one of two buildings.

## Blackpink and BTS, same corridor

**Jisoo of Blackpink and Jungkook of BTS both attended the School of Performing Arts Seoul.** So did Kang Seul-gi of Red Velvet, and 122 others in our roster.

We cannot tell you whether any of them overlapped. Wikidata records the school but rarely the years, so "same school" here means the same institution across an unknown span of time — not the same classroom.

What it does mean is that **one school in Guro-gu, Seoul has 125 people in a roster of 9,249 Korean entertainers**. Nothing else in our school data concentrates like that except the universities.

Six arts universities account for **629 people**. Together with the high schools, **995 of 4,535 — 21.9% — of Korean entertainers with a recorded school went to an arts institution.**

**Kim Go-eun appears twice**, at Kaywon High School of Arts and then Korea National University of Arts. People with both a school and a university on record are counted once in each row, which is why the two tables overlap.

## The half we cannot see

This is the part that decides how much the 21.9% is worth.

**4,714 people in our roster have no school recorded at all** — slightly more than the 4,535 who do. If arts-school alumni are more likely to have that fact written down (and there is a plausible reason they would be: a school with famous alumni maintains the list), then 21.9% is too high. If the missing half looks like the visible half, it holds.

**We cannot tell which, and we are not going to pick the reading that makes the number sound better.** The count is 995 out of 4,535 recorded. That is the sentence we can defend.
