Of the **397 Korean titles** that have reached a Netflix Top 10 somewhere in Southeast Asia since
2021, **140 have never once appeared on Netflix's Top 10 in Korea.**

That is 35% of the panel, and it is not a rounding of the tail. It is a structurally different group.

| | Titles | Avg. markets reached | Avg. weeks | Reached #1 |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Also charted in Korea | 257 | **4.67** | 6.9 | 50.2% |
| Never charted in Korea | 140 | **2.21** | 3.4 | **14.3%** |

A title that plays at home reaches more than twice as many Southeast Asian markets and is three and a
half times as likely to reach number one in one of them.

## The relationship is real but loose

Weeks on Korea's chart and markets reached in Southeast Asia correlate at **r = 0.51** across the 397.
That is strong enough to be the main story and weak enough that the exceptions are the interesting part.

| Weeks on Korea's chart | Titles | Avg. Southeast Asian markets |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| None | 140 | 2.21 |
| 1–3 | 70 | 3.51 |
| 4–9 | 149 | 5.01 |
| 10 or more | 38 | 5.50 |

Read down the right-hand column and the ladder is clean. Read the ends and it breaks: **15 titles
reached all six Southeast Asian markets without ever charting in Korea**, and 78 of the 122
single-market titles never charted at home either.

Put the other way: **8.4% of the titles that swept the region skipped the Korean chart entirely, and
63.9% of the single-market titles did.**

## What the exceptions are

The titles that travel without charting at home are mostly **broadcast dramas**. *Young Lady and
Gentleman* and *The Penthouse: War in Life* each held eleven weeks across all six Southeast Asian
markets; neither appears on Korea's Netflix chart at all. *Dr. Romantic* and *Sell Your Haunted House*
are the same shape.

This is not a mystery, and it is the reason we will not call these titles unpopular in Korea. They
aired on Korean television. A KBS or SBS drama at nine in the evening is watched on the broadcaster,
not on Netflix, so it never competes for a Netflix chart slot at home — while abroad, Netflix is the
only place it exists.

The reverse case is smaller and more surprising: titles that hold Korea's chart for weeks and reach
exactly one Southeast Asian market. *Guardian: The Lonely and Great God* charted fourteen weeks in
Korea and one market abroad. *Nocturnal* held thirteen at home and two weeks in one market.

## Films skip home more than series do

**51.9% of the films in this panel never charted in Korea, against 24.1% of the series.** A Korean
film often has its domestic life in cinemas, arrives on Netflix months later, and by then the
domestic audience has seen it — while for a Southeast Asian viewer the Netflix release is the release.

That is a distribution fact, not a taste fact, and it is why a chart comparison across countries is
harder to read than it looks.

## Why this matters for anyone using the numbers

**Netflix's Korean chart is not a smaller version of its regional charts.** It is a different chart,
because it competes with a domestic television industry the other markets do not have.

Anyone using Korean Netflix performance as a proxy for how a title did in Korea will be wrong in a
predictable direction: they will undercount broadcast drama and overcount Netflix originals. We use
it here for exactly one purpose — as a second signal on whether a title with an ambiguous name is the
Korean work — and even for that we treat absence as a reason to look, never as an answer.

**And 140 is a floor, not a count of failures.** It includes every title released before July 2021,
which had its domestic run before this data starts. We cannot separate those out, so the honest
reading of the 35% is: at most that many skipped Korea's chart, and some of them never had the chance.
