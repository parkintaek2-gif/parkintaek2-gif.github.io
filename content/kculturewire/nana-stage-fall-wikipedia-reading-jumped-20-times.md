Nana, of the K-pop group After School, averaged 918 English Wikipedia reads a day for the week
before 18 September, 2026. In the two days the news broke that she had fallen from a stage during a
pre-taping and been hospitalized, that number went to 9,691, then 18,446 — 20.1 times her baseline
on the second day, the largest single-day multiplier we have measured for anyone in this panel.

## What happened, and when

Etoday reported that Nana fell while stepping down from the main stage after finishing a
pre-recording for MBC Music's *Show Champion* on the morning of 19 September, Korea time. An initial
check found nothing serious, but a follow-up exam found a severe pelvic contusion, and she was
admitted to hospital. She paused all scheduled activities; the group continued its promotion for the
single "First Love" as a six-member act.

Wikimedia's pageview days run on UTC, which is nine hours behind Korea. That means the "18 September"
pageview day we cite below covers roughly 09:00, 18 September to 09:00, 19 September, Korea time —
the exact window the fall and the first wave of reporting fell into. The second, larger wave lands
squarely in the following UTC day, 19 September.

| Day (Wikimedia UTC pageview day) | Views | Multiple of the 918/day baseline |
|---|---|---|
| 10-17 September average (baseline) | 918 | 1.0&times; |
| 18 September | 9,691 | 10.6&times; |
| 19 September | 18,446 | 20.1&times; |

## Why we're flagging this one

Most of the reading spikes we track in this series come with a competing explanation attached — a
comeback, an award, a rumor, an anniversary landing in the same week as something else. This one is
unusually clean: a single reported event, on a single day, with no other Nana-related news in the
same window that we found. That makes it one of the more legible cause-and-effect pairs we've
published, alongside the reputation-index comparisons and campaign-versus-birthday test on our
[what-moves-english-reading](/what-moves-english-reading) hub.
