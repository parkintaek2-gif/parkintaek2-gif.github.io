**T1** are in the quarter-finals of the 2026 World Championship. They beat **Movistar KOI** 2-0 in a best-of-three at 05:00 Korea time on 29 October and finished the Swiss Stage at **3-1**, the third team through after Dplus KIA and Gen.G.

Their only loss came in the second round, to Dplus KIA. Since then they have beaten CTBC Flying Oyster and Movistar KOI.

## The two games, from Riot's live stats

| Game | Kills | Gold | Towers | Dragons | Barons |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **20**–10 | 82,899–79,836 | 10–5 | 4–3 | 1–1 |
| 2 | **22**–9 | 57,518–45,208 | 9–0 | 3–0 | 0–0 |

Game 1 was close on gold, about 3,000 apart at the end, with a baron each. Game 2 was not: Movistar KOI did not take a tower or a dragon. Faker played Syndra and then Orianna.

## Where 3-1 teams finished, 2024 and 2025

| Year | Team | Swiss record | Went out in / won |
|---|---|---|---|
| 2024 | Top Esports | 3-1 | Quarter-final |
| 2024 | Hanwha Life | 3-1 | Quarter-final |
| 2024 | **T1** | 3-1 | **Won the title** |
| 2025 | Hanwha Life | 3-1 | Quarter-final |
| 2025 | G2 | 3-1 | Quarter-final |
| 2025 | Gen.G | 3-1 | Semi-final |

Six teams, three outcomes: one title, one semi-final, four quarter-final exits. The 3-1 route is the one T1 took to the 2024 title. In 2025 they won from **3-2**, the last team in.

Across the same two years, the record a team qualified with did not line up with how far it went. Of the six 3-2 teams, four reached at least the semi-finals; of the six 3-1 teams, two did; of the four 3-0 teams, two did. Sixteen teams over two years is too few to call that a pattern. It is a reason not to read the Swiss record as a ranking.

## Who read T1 that day

The match started at 20:00 UTC on 28 October. That day the **T1 (esports)** Wikipedia article was read **2,184** times across its 17 language editions, about twice its usual day of 1,079. The next day it fell back to 1,794.
