Five days ago we [counted the reads for South Korea's first Asian Games fighting-games
gold](/article/south-korea-won-its-first-asian-games-esports-gold-and-still-reads-10-to-1-behind-faker)
and found the gold medallist drawing about a tenth of the reads of a teammate who had not yet played
a match. That teammate was Lee "Faker" Sang-hyeok, and the obvious question was what would happen
once he actually played.

He played one game.

South Korea won the League of Legends title on 2 October, beating Chinese Taipei 3-0 and finishing
the tournament without dropping a single game — a second straight Asian Games gold after Hangzhou.
Across the whole run, Faker appeared in one match, against India. Zeka played the rest,
including every game of the final.

## The reads on the day of the final

| Player | Played the final | Reads, 2 Oct |
|---|---|---|
| Faker | No | 1,405 |
| Keria | Yes | 235 |
| Gumayusi | Yes | 210 |
| Zeus | Yes | 204 (on 3 Oct) |
| Zeka | Yes, all three games | — |
| Canyon | Yes | — |

The two em dashes are not missing data. **Zeka and Canyon have no English Wikipedia article.** We
checked "Zeka (gamer)", "Canyon (gamer)" and the plain-name variants; all come back missing. There
is no page to read, so there is no number to print, and we are not going to print a zero in a cell
that means something else entirely.

Zeka is the player who started the final in Faker's place and played all three games of it.

## The other way to read the same day

Faker's 1,405 is the largest number in the table and the smallest movement in it. Against each
page's own eleven-day average, Faker rose 1.4×, Keria 2.3×, Gumayusi 2.6× and Zeus 3.3×. His
ordinary day is about 990 reads — a gold medal moved him less than half again, because his floor is
already higher than his teammates' ceilings.

So the final produced two true sentences that point in opposite directions. The player who did not
play it was read more than everyone who did. And the players who did play it were the ones whose
reading actually changed.

## Where the fighting-games gold sits now

Bae "Knee" Jae-min, whose 27 September win was South Korea's first Asian Games gold in fighting
games, peaked at 74 reads on 3 October against an eleven-day average of 34. His best day of this
Games is about 5 per cent of Faker's best day, and roughly a third of what Keria drew on the night
of the final.

Five days ago we measured that gap at seven to ten times. It is now wider, and the reason is not
that Knee was read less — he was read more than before. It is that the final gave everyone else a
day, and his was already over.

## What this does not say

An English Wikipedia article is a record of English-language coverage, not of achievement. Zeka
started and won an Asian Games final; the absence of a page is a fact about what has been written
in English, and we are not treating it as a verdict on the player. Likewise, a page view is a page
opened — it rises for good reasons and bad ones, and it is not a vote.
