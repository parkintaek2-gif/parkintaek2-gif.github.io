**Minnie** of i-dle released *i love you to the moon and back* on **23 October 2026**, her birthday. It is her first record in Thai, her first language, and her first solo release in a year and nine months. A pop-up in Bangkok ran alongside it, and a fan live is set there for 25 October.

So did Thai readers come looking? They did. But they were not the biggest audience.

## Release day across eight Wikipedias

| Wikipedia | Usual day (median, 1–18 Oct) | 23 Oct | Rise |
|---|---|---|---|
| English | 252 | **943** | ×3.7 |
| Chinese | 162 | **648** | ×4.0 |
| Thai | 60 | **407** | **×6.8** |
| Japanese | 60 | 100 | ×1.7 |
| Korean | 18 | 78 | ×4.5 |
| Spanish | 17 | 45 | ×2.7 |
| Indonesian | 5 | 29 | ×6.4 |
| Vietnamese | 12 | 5 | — |

**Thai Wikipedia** had the steepest rise: almost seven times its usual day. In plain reads, though, it came **third**. Chinese Wikipedia drew 648 and English 943.

## Why the Chinese page is so big

Minnie's Chinese-language page draws more than her Thai one on an ordinary day too, about 160 reads against 60. i-dle has long had one of K-pop's largest Chinese-speaking audiences, since the group was formed with Chinese and Taiwanese members, and Chinese Wikipedia serves readers in Taiwan, Hong Kong and Singapore as well as the mainland. A Thai-language EP did not change that order. It only narrowed the gap.

## The birthday problem

23 October is both the release day and Minnie's birthday, so these numbers cannot say how many readers came for the record and how many came for the date. One clue: on **19 October**, four days early, English Wikipedia had already risen from about 250 to **396** reads and stayed near 400 until release day. We did not check what lifted it.

## What this does and does not say

Wikipedia reads count how many people looked her up, in which language. They do not measure streams or sales. What they show is that a record made for Thai listeners got its biggest proportional lift in Thai, and its biggest audience in English and Chinese.
