Two things could be true about a market that sees a lot of Korean titles. It might be **churning**
through them — many titles, each on the chart briefly. Or it might be **holding** them — many
titles, each staying a long time.

Across 93 Netflix markets it is the second, and the effect is large.

| Market | Distinct Korean titles | Chart places | Weeks per title |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Vietnam** | 261 | 1,617 | **6.2** |
| Indonesia | 275 | 1,550 | 5.6 |
| Malaysia | 259 | 1,346 | 5.2 |
| South Korea | 592 | 2,871 | 4.9 |
| Japan | 245 | 1,096 | 4.5 |
| France | 81 | 194 | 2.4 |
| Germany | 60 | 144 | 2.4 |
| United States | 39 | 85 | 2.2 |
| **United Kingdom** | 47 | 95 | **2.0** |
| Iceland | 43 | 77 | 1.8 |

Depth and shelf life rise together — correlation **0.72** across the 93. The median market keeps a
Korean title for **2.9 weeks**. Vietnam keeps it for 6.2, Iceland for 1.8.

## The gap is a market difference, not a title difference

A Korean series that reaches the UK chart is usually the same series that reaches Vietnam's. It is
not a shorter show in Britain. It leaves sooner.

Ten Asian markets — Korea, Indonesia, Thailand, Vietnam, Malaysia, Taiwan, the Philippines,
Singapore, Hong Kong and Japan — sit at a median of **4.9 weeks per title**, and the lowest of the
ten is 4.5. The other 83 markets sit at a median of **2.6**.

Five of those 83 clear 4.5 anyway, and they are worth naming: **Bangladesh (5.3), Pakistan (5.1),
Bolivia (5.0), Ukraine (4.8) and India (4.6)**. Three of them — Bangladesh, Pakistan and India —
hold a Korean title longer than **Japan** does, on 538 to 766 chart places each. That is not a
rounding artefact of a thin market; those are real runs, in countries that appear on no list of
K-drama markets we have seen.

Ukraine is the one that is thin: 92 places across 19 titles, where a single long run moves the
figure a long way. We separate it rather than average it in.

**Vietnam gives a Korean title 3.1 times the shelf life Britain gives it.**

## Why "how many countries" undersells it

Reach is the number most often quoted about a Korean title, and it treats every country as one
unit. By that measure Britain and Vietnam count the same.

They do not behave the same. Vietnam gave Korean titles **1,617 chart places** out of 5,360 — 30.2%
of everything on its top 10 for five years. Britain gave them **95**, or 1.8%. That is seventeen
times the places, from a market of comparable chart size.

A title that "charted in 60 countries" spent most of those weeks in a handful of them.

## What this cannot say

It cannot say why. Catalogue availability, dubbing and subtitling, promotion, chart size and simple
habit are all plausible, and this data contains none of them. Weeks per title is also a mean: a
market carrying one unusually long run will read higher than its typical title does.
