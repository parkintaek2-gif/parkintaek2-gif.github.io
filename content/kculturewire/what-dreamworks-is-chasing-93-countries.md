A trade headline on 5 September says DreamWorks is answering *KPop Demon Hunters* with a film
called *Forgotten Island*, out on 25 September. We have not seen it and this piece does not measure
it.

What we can measure is the thing being answered. We went to Netflix's weekly country tables — 269
weeks of them — and counted every week *KPop Demon Hunters* appeared in a country's top 10.

| | |
|---|---:|
| Countries it charted in | 93 |
| Countries where it reached number one | 76 |
| Distinct weeks it was on some chart | 62 |
| Longest run in one country — Latvia | 62 weeks |
| United States | 49 weeks |
| Korea | 32 weeks |
| Median country | 32 weeks |

**Korea sat exactly at the median.** Forty-six countries held it longer than its home market did.

## The longest runs are in the Baltics, not the big markets

The five countries that held it longest are Latvia (62 weeks), Estonia (60), Lithuania (59),
Serbia (57) and Mexico (51). The United States, the largest market Netflix publishes, is well
behind at 49.

This is a presence count, not an audience count — a small country's top 10 is easier to stay in
than a large one's, and we are not claiming Latvians watched it more than Americans. What the
number says is narrower and still worth knowing: **in 46 markets this film was a fixture of the
list for longer than it was in Korea.**

## It did not sit there. It kept coming back

The number most people would expect — one long unbroken run — is not what the tables show.

**Seventy-eight of the 93 countries have a gap.** The title fell out of the top 10 and returned,
sometimes more than once. Only 15 countries held it without interruption.

That distinction only exists because the weeks and the longest unbroken run were counted
separately. A single "weeks on chart" figure would have hidden it, and would have made the run look
like something it was not.

## The singer is the other half of the measurement

We spent this morning counting how English Wikipedia titles Korean stars, and one name came out of
that work with an odd shape. **Ejae** — Kim Eun-jae, who provided the singing voice for Rumi and
co-wrote songs on the soundtrack — has **3.92 million** reads on English Wikipedia and **not one
disambiguation bracket**.

That combination is unusual. Among the 100 most-read Korean stars we hold, the names that get read
at that level normally collect brackets, because the encyclopedia has to tell them apart from
someone else called the same thing. Nobody else is called Ejae. A studio chasing this film is
chasing something with no established name to compete with, which is a harder thing to reproduce
than a chart position.

## What this cannot tell you

It cannot tell you whether *Forgotten Island* will do any of this. It is not released, we have not
seen it, and a chart count of one film says nothing about another.

It cannot tell you how long anyone watched. Presence in a top 10 is presence, not hours.

And our tables stop on **23 August 2026**. If the run continued past that week, we did not measure
it, and we are not going to write as though we did.
