Eleven Korean esports players sit in our Southeast Asian panel. All eleven have their busiest month in November 2025. Not most of them — all of them.

Nine Korean footballers sit in the same panel. Three share a busiest month. The other six are spread across five different ones.

If a year's reads were spread evenly, the busiest month would hold 8.3% of them.

## How much of a year lands in one month

| | People | Median busiest-month share | Range | Commonest peak | Sharing it |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Esports | 11 | 36.6% | 30.1–58.4% | November 2025 | 11 of 11 |
| Football | 9 | 26.5% | 10.7–60.3% | June 2026 | 3 of 9 |

The medians are not far apart. The ranges are what separate them. The *least* concentrated esports player in the panel still puts 30.1% of a year into one month. The least concentrated footballer puts in 10.7%, which is close to flat.

That footballer is Park Ji-sung, who retired in 2014. He is the useful control here: someone with no fixtures, no transfers and no tournament, read at roughly the rate an even year would produce. Every figure above him is a departure from that baseline, and the esports column departs further and more uniformly.

Son Heung-min is read more over the year than any esports player here — 194,608 against Faker's 153,241 — and reads him spread across the calendar rather than pooling. Two different shapes of attention, at similar volume.

## One year is one point

A single year proves nothing about a sport. So we ran the same measure back through 2022, counting a person for a year only if their article was already being read before that year started.

| Year | Esports sharing a peak month | Football sharing a peak month |
|---|---|---|
| 2022 | 1 of 1 — all | 4 of 9 |
| 2023 | 2 of 2 — all | 4 of 9 |
| 2024 | 4 of 5 | 4 of 9 |
| 2025 | 9 of 9 — all | 3 of 9 |

The footballers reach agreement in 0 of those 4 years; their best is 4 of 9. The esports players reach it in 3 of 4, and in the fourth, 4 of 5 still land on November.

That rule about articles already being read matters more than it sounds. On the first pass, four esports players showed exactly **100%** of their 2024 reads in December. That was not a spike in attention — their articles were created that month, and a measure that cannot tell "no article yet" from "nobody read it" turns a birth date into a surge. Those four are now excluded from 2024 and the year reads 4 of 5 rather than 4 of 9.

## What was on those months

Two dates, taken from Wikidata rather than from our own data:

- The **2025 League of Legends World Championship** ran from 14 October to 9 November 2025. The final fell in November.
- The **2026 FIFA World Cup** opened on 11 June 2026.

Those are printed here because a reader deserves to see them next to the counts. They are not a finding. What we measured is that a month is crowded; that a tournament ended in the same month is a fact from a calendar. We did not measure that one caused the other, and this piece does not claim it.

What can be said without that leap: the November concentration is unanimous across eleven people who play the same game, and it is not matched by any group of footballers in the panel, including three who peak in the month a World Cup opened.

## What this cannot say

**Narrowed after publication.** Splitting these players by game shows that 85.7% of the ones we could measure play League of Legends, so "esports players" is wider than the evidence. The honest scope is League of Legends players. We could not test the other games because too few of their Korean players are read in these editions at all — see [what we called an esports pattern was one game](/article/it-was-one-game).

A month with more reads is a month with more reads. It does not say why someone opened the page.

This counts only the Indonesian, Vietnamese, Thai and Malay Wikipedias. Readers in those countries also use the English edition, which cannot be split by country, so every count here is a floor. The panel is the twenty most-read Korean athletes in those four editions, not a sample of Korean sport.

And a concentrated year is not a smaller year. Faker's 153,241 reads arrived mostly in one month; they still arrived.
