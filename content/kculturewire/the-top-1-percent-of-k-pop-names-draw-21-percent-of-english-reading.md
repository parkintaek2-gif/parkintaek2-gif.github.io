Two dozen names account for a fifth of a month's English Wikipedia reading across the entire Korean music roster we track. More than a thousand names at the bottom account for well under a twentieth.

## What the numbers say

We took every Korean singer, rapper, composer, and music group on Wikidata with an English Wikipedia article — 2,372 in total — and counted each one's reading over a 30-day window, then checked how much of the total the top slices of the roster account for.

| Slice of the roster | Names | Share of total reading |
|---|---|---|
| Top 1% | 24 | 21.5% |
| Top 5% | 119 | 50.2% |
| Top 10% | 238 | 66.0% |
| Bottom 50% | 1,186 | 3.7% |

Half the roster — 1,186 names — together draw less reading than the top 24 names alone, which pull in roughly 21% of the total (21.5% exactly). The top tenth of the roster draws two-thirds of all the reading in the window.

## Groups concentrate harder than solo names

Split by type, groups are more top-heavy than individuals: the top 1% of the 822-group roster (9 groups) draw 29.0% of group-only reading, against 19.2% for the top 1% of the 1,550-person roster (16 people). BTS alone leads the entire combined panel at 8,689 reads/day, ahead of So Ji-sub (5,227), Blackpink (4,499), Jennie (3,996), V (3,948), and Steven Yeun (3,701).

## What this can and cannot say

This cannot say why reading concentrates this way — debut year, label resources, group versus solo status, and pre-existing international fame could all play a role, and a 30-day snapshot cannot separate them. It also is not a claim about talent or deserving the attention; it counts one specific kind of visibility on one specific reference site. What it does establish cleanly is the shape of the distribution: a small top slice of the roster accounts for the large majority of measured English Wikipedia reading, and the bottom half accounts for a small fraction of it.
