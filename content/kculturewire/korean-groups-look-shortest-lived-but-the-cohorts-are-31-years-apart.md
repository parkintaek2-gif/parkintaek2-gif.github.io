We set out to compare how long Korean, Japanese, American and British music groups last before disbanding, using the dissolution year Wikidata records for each group. The number came out cleanly — and we are not publishing it as a ranking, because the dataset itself rules that out.

## What the raw numbers show

| Country | Groups countable | Have a recorded end date | Median lifespan | Ended within 10 years | Median founding year (recorded-ended) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| South Korea | 691 | 67 (9.7%) | 3 years | 97.0% | 2011 |
| Japan | 1,373 | 110 (8.0%) | 6 years | 70.9% | 1999 |
| United States | 10,844 | 223 (2.1%) | 7 years | 70.4% | 1990 |
| United Kingdom | 3,768 | 143 (3.8%) | 5 years | 69.9% | 1980 |

Read on its own, the Korean row looks like the shortest-lived group culture of the four. It is also the youngest one, by a lot.

## Why we're not calling this a ranking

The median founding year for groups with a recorded end date spans 31 years across the four countries — 2011 for the Korean cohort down to 1980 for the British one. A group that Wikidata says formed in 2011 has had, at most, 15 years to accumulate a recorded run; a group from 1980 has had 46. Comparing "how long did this group last" across cohorts that far apart in age is comparing groups that have not had equal time to prove out a long run, not comparing four countries' actual group culture.

This is also why the share with any recorded end date at all varies so much — 9.7% for Korea against 2.1% for the US. That gap could reflect real differences in how actively each country's fan and editor communities maintain group pages, how many groups have quietly stopped without anyone recording it, or something else the data does not show. We do not know which, so we do not guess.

## What we can say

The one thing that holds regardless of cohort age: across all four countries, a large majority of the groups that do end are recorded as ending within 10 years of forming (69.9% to 97.0%). A group lasting past its first decade is the exception everywhere we measured, not the rule — even if we cannot say which country's exception rate is genuinely lowest.
