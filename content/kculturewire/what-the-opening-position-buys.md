On the first Monday there is exactly one fact: the title is on a country's chart, at some position.
Everything else — how it will do, how long it will hold — is not known yet.

We have measured [how a title leaves](/exit) and [how long it stays](/run-length). We had never
connected the two ends. So: **does the opening position say anything about the length?**

## For a Korean series, a great deal

| Opened at | Runs | Median weeks | Lasting 4+ weeks | Gone after one week |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1 | 506 | **6** | 89.9% | 0.4% |
| 2 | 496 | 5 | 73.8% | 3.2% |
| 3 | 642 | 4 | 61.4% | 6.5% |
| 4 | 745 | 3 | 49.5% | 12.2% |
| 5 | 831 | 3 | 44.6% | 19.1% |
| 6 | 892 | 2 | 34.1% | 28.1% |
| 7 | 916 | 2 | 26.9% | 36.4% |
| 8 | 1,058 | 2 | 24.6% | 41.5% |
| 9 | 1,057 | 2 | 21% | 49.9% |
| 10 | 1,181 | **1** | 16.4% | 56.1% |

A Korean series that opens at number one runs a median of **six weeks**, and nine in ten of them last
a month or more. One that opens at number ten runs **one week**, and more than half are gone the
following Monday.

The middle of the table is where it turns. Above position five, most runs reach four weeks. Below
it, most do not.

## The same measurement for everyone else

Entering higher could simply last longer on every chart in the world. So each group was measured the
same way, and the relationship reduced to one number: **how many weeks one position is worth**,
taken as the gap between the two ends of the table divided by the nine steps between them.

| | Runs | Median weeks opening at 1 | Opening at 10 | Weeks per position |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Korean series | 8,324 | 6 | 1 | **0.56** |
| Every other series | 79,582 | 5 | 1 | **0.44** |
| Korean films | 4,197 | 4 | 1 | **0.33** |
| Every other film | 118,252 | 3 | 1 | **0.22** |

Two things fall out of that column.

**Position is worth more in series than in films** — 0.56 and 0.44 against 0.33 and 0.22. A film's
run is short whatever it opens at, so there is less room for the opening to matter.

**And position is worth more for Korean titles than for the rest, in both formats.** Korean series
get 0.56 weeks a step where other series get 0.44; Korean films 0.33 where other films get 0.22. On
these charts the opening position is a sharper signal about a Korean title than about anything else.

That is a different statement from "Korean titles last longer", which we [measured
separately](/run-length) and which is true of series only. This one says the *first week tells you
more* — the spread between a good opening and a bad one is wider.

## What this cannot tell you

**It is not a forecast.** These are averages over thousands of runs, and the spread inside every
position is wide: a quarter of the runs that opened at position 8 still reached four weeks, and one
in ten that opened at number one did not.

It also cannot tell you *why*. A title that opens at number one had something behind it — a
marketing push, a returning cast, a season two — and none of that is in a rank table. The opening
position is a symptom of whatever produced it, not a cause of what follows.

And a chart is not viewing. A title one place outside a top 10 is invisible here at any level of
viewing, so everything above is about time on a rank list and nothing else. Netflix publishes no
country-level viewing figures at all.
