Over three days, two Korean entertainment outlets named a BTS member in a headline 19 times. The member with the most English Wikipedia reading of the seven was named in none of them.

## What the numbers say

We matched our own three-day archive of Korean entertainment headlines against 30-day English Wikipedia reading for each of the seven BTS members.

| Member | Headline mentions (3 days) | English Wikipedia reads/day |
|---|---|---|
| V | 0 | 3,948 |
| Jung Kook | 0 | 3,458 |
| Jimin | 9 | 2,285 |
| Jin | 12 | 2,161 |
| RM | 0 | 2,120 |
| Suga | 0 | 2,075 |
| J-Hope | 0 | 1,780 |

V leads the group's own members in English Wikipedia reading and was not named in a single one of the 19 member-naming headlines across the window. Jin, named 12 times, reads fourth of the seven. Jung Kook, second in reading, also appeared in zero headlines.

## Two different things being counted

A Korean-outlet headline count captures what two specific newsrooms chose to report on in a three-day window — often tied to a specific event, appearance, or announcement involving that member. English Wikipedia reading captures ongoing global lookup interest over 30 days, independent of that week's Korean press cycle. The two can point in opposite directions for the same person at the same time, which is what happened here.

## What this can and cannot say

This cannot say why the pattern holds — it may reflect what events each member was involved in in early September, differences in how Korean and international audiences follow different members, or simply which member's activities happened to generate headline-worthy news that particular week. The press archive is also thin: three days, two outlets. A longer window, or a different pair of outlets, could show a different mention pattern. What this snapshot does establish is that a member's headline presence in Korean entertainment press and that member's English Wikipedia reading are not the same measurement, and for this group in this window, they moved in opposite orders.
