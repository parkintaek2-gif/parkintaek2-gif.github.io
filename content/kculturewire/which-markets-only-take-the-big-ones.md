Every Korean title that charts anywhere has a **breadth** — the number of countries it reached at
some point. Some markets carry titles that went almost nowhere else. Others only ever show the ones
that went everywhere.

For a distributor that is the release-order question, and we had never measured it. So: for each
market, the **median breadth of the Korean titles that appeared there**.

## The two ends

| Only the widest titles get in | Median breadth | Korean share of that chart |
|---|---:|---:|
| Estonia | 78 | 1.8% |
| Slovenia | 76 | 2.2% |
| Switzerland | 72 | 2.6% |
| Croatia | 71 | 2.1% |
| Lithuania | 71 | 2.1% |

| Narrow titles get in too | Median breadth | Korean share of that chart |
|---|---:|---:|
| South Korea | 2 | 51.4% |
| Japan | 18 | 20.1% |
| Vietnam | 21 | 29.3% |
| Thailand | 21 | 23.4% |
| Taiwan | 21 | 23.6% |

**On Korea's own chart the median Korean title reached two countries in total** — its own and one
other. 63% of the Korean titles that chart in Korea reached five markets or fewer. Nowhere else
comes close: in Estonia, not one of the 36 Korean titles that charted there had a breadth of five or
under.

We are not publishing the other 83 markets in order. A league table of countries invites a reading
this data cannot support, and the figure that explains most of the difference is in the second
column of both tables.

## The second column explains a lot of it

A market that rarely plays Korean titles at all will show only the biggest ones — not because it is
hard to reach, but because there is one Korean slot and the biggest title takes it. That is a
different statement, and the two are entangled.

So the same measurement, with markets grouped by how much of their chart is Korean:

| Korean share of the chart | Markets | Median breadth | Range within the band |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Under 5% | 49 | 63 | 48.5 |
| 5–8% | 12 | 52 | 6 |
| 8–12% | 18 | 46 | 9 |
| 12% or more | 14 | 21 | 43 |

**It falls straight down: 63, 52, 46, 21.** The more of a chart is Korean, the narrower the titles
that get onto it. Most of what looks like "hard to reach" is "rarely plays Korean titles".

**But it is not all of it.** Inside the under-5% band — 49 markets that all play Korean titles about
as rarely as each other — the breadth still ranges across 48.5 markets' worth. Two countries with the
same Korean share can want very different titles.

⚠ We cannot take that further. Separating "this market is hard" from "this market has one Korean
slot" would need release and licensing data, and Netflix publishes none.

## What this cannot tell you

It says which titles appeared, not why. **Licensing windows, dubbing, local release dates and how
crowded a chart already is would all produce this pattern**, and none of them is in a rank table. A
title may be unavailable in a market rather than unwanted by it, and those look identical here.

It is also not viewing. A title one place outside a top 10 is invisible at any level of viewing, so
a market with a short Korean list may still be watching a great deal of it.

And breadth is a lifetime count, not a simultaneous one — a title that spread to 60 countries slowly
counts the same as one that opened in 60 at once. We measure that difference separately, on
[when a Korean title is at its widest](/time-to-peak).
