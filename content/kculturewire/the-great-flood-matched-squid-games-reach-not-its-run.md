Three Korean Netflix titles reached the same widest tier of country coverage — all 93 tracked markets. One of them stayed on the charts more than five times as long as another.

## What the numbers say

We read every weekly Netflix Top 10 country table for 974 Korean titles and counted, per title, how many of the 93 tracked markets it ever reached number one in, against how many weeks it appeared on any chart at all.

| Title | Markets reaching #1 | Weeks on a chart |
|---|---|---|
| Squid Game | 93 | 60 |
| All of Us Are Dead | 93 | 34 |
| The Great Flood | 93 | 11 |
| Hellbound | 92 | 9 |
| Carter | 92 | 5 |

Squid Game and The Great Flood both hit number one in every one of the 93 markets Netflix's public tables track. Squid Game held some chart presence for 60 weeks. The Great Flood held it for 11 — 5.5x shorter, despite reaching the identical peak.

## Why breadth and duration are different measurements

Reaching number one in 93 countries measures how far a title's launch-week pull carries. Staying on a chart for weeks or months measures something else — whether people keep watching, rewatching, or newly discovering it well after release. A title can max out the first measurement in its opening days and still fall off every chart within weeks, which is what happened to The Great Flood.

## What this can and cannot say

This cannot say why one title held on and the other didn't — genre, a second season, or where a title landed on the release calendar could each explain a longer run, and this count has no way to separate them. What it establishes cleanly is that "reached number one everywhere" and "stayed on the charts a long time" are not the same claim, and a title can be true on one count while being nowhere close on the other.
