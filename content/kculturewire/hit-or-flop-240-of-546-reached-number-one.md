Twice in the last four weeks, somebody reached this site by typing a Korean film's name followed by
**"hit or flop"**. Once for *The 8th Night*, once for *Concrete Utopia*. They did not click. We had
nothing on the page that answered them, because we have never used those two words.

We still will not use them. But the question underneath is a fair one, and the Netflix files can answer
a version of it — just not the version people usually mean.

## What the files can and cannot say

Netflix publishes weekly top ten lists per country. From those we can count, for any Korean title that
ever charted, three things: **how many countries it reached, how many weeks it stayed, and the best rank
it managed.** On 30 August we placed each of those three, for 546 of the 974 Korean titles, as a position
within the whole set.

What the files never contain is money, hours watched, budget, or whether the thing was any good. A title
that charts in one country for one week at No.9 may have cost nothing and earned plenty. Nothing here can
tell you that, and a page that claims to is not reading these files.

## Reaching No.1 is the common case

This is the number that undoes the question. Of the 546 titles with a standing, **240 reached No.1 in at
least one country — 44.0%.**

Not the top ten. The top of it. Nearly half.

So "it hit No.1" is not a claim that separates anything. It is closer to a description of the median
Korean title that charted at all. Of those 240, **26 reached No.1 in exactly one country** and nowhere
else.

## The three measures disagree with each other

If "hit" meant one thing, the three counts would move together. They do not. **50 titles — 9.2% — sit
more than 50 percentage points apart depending on which measure you use.**

*12.12: The Day* is the clearest case. It reached **one country**, stayed **twelve weeks**, and peaked at
**No.1**. Read by rank it is at the very top of the set. Read by countries reached it is below 54% of
everything else. Both readings are correct. They are answers to different questions.

*Check In Hanyang* runs the other way: **nine countries, four weeks, best rank 6**. Wider than most,
never near the top.

## The two titles people actually asked about

Both reached No.1. That is where the similarity ends.

- ***The 8th Night*** — **10 countries, 5 weeks, best rank 1.** Wider than 77% of the set.
- ***Concrete Utopia*** — **2 countries, 9 weeks, best rank 1.** Longer-running, far narrower.

If someone tells you one of these was a hit and the other a flop, the files do not support the sentence
in either direction. They support two different sentences, and both are about reach and duration rather
than success.

## Why we leave the word alone

**26.7%** of these titles charted in exactly one country. The middle of the set — half above, half
below — reached **7 countries** and stayed **6 weeks**. A person deciding whether a film "flopped" is usually
comparing it against an imagined norm; the actual distribution is the thing worth seeing, and it is
published — [every title we have standing for](/titles), [every market side by side](/catalogue-depth),
and [all 93 countries](/by-country) counted the same way.

We will keep counting. We will not be the site that tells you a film failed.
