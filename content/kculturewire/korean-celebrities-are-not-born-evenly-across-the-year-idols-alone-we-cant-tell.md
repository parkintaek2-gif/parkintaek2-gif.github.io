Among 9,249 Korean public figures with a full birthdate on record, February is the most common month to be born — 30.6 people per day on average, 21% above what an even 12-way split would produce — and June the least, at 14% below. A 10,000-draw randomness test put the odds of a spread this uneven happening by chance at effectively zero.

Narrow that same question to 1,317 K-pop group members with a known birthdate, and the answer changes. January and March are tied for most common there, July the least — a wider swing in raw terms than the broad list showed. But the identical randomness test, run on this smaller list, could not rule out chance: the odds came back at 1, meaning this specific pattern is statistically indistinguishable from noise.

## What we counted

| List | People | Most common month | Least common month | Chance this is random |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Broad (all Korean public figures) | 9,249 | February (1.21x even) | June (0.86x even) | ~0 |
| Narrow (K-pop group members) | 1,317 | January / March (1.19x even) | July (0.68x even) | 1 (cannot rule out) |

Both counts excluded a handful of dates — 1 January and 28 February chief among them — that show an unusually large cluster of births, 1.5 to 1.9 times a typical day. Those look like recording defaults rather than real birthdates, and running each test again without them did not change which list passed and which did not.

## Why we report both, not just the one that "worked"

If we quoted only the broad list, a reader would reasonably assume the same held for K-pop idols specifically — it does not, at least not to a degree this test can confirm. If we quoted only the narrow list, we would be reporting a null result and hiding that the wider population shows a real pattern. Which list a claim is about changes whether it is true.
