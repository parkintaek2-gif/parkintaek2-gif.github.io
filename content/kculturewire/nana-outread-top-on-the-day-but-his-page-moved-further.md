On 2 October the agencies for T.O.P and Nana confirmed that the two are in a relationship, after
Dispatch reported it that morning. Within the same twenty-four hours, ten separate Wikipedia reading
series — two names, five language editions — all hit their highest point of the past three weeks.
Not one of them peaked on a different day.

That is the easy part. The interesting part is that the two obvious ways to measure the day give two
different winners.

## Nana was read more. In every edition.

| Edition | Nana, 2 Oct | T.O.P, 2 Oct |
|---|---|---|
| English | 51,120 | 20,568 |
| Vietnamese | 2,460 | 1,305 |
| Indonesian | 1,661 | 542 |
| Thai | 1,604 | 367 |
| Malay | 35 | 12 |

Five editions, five times the same direction. In English the gap is roughly 2.5 to 1.

## T.O.P's page moved further from its own normal. In four of five.

Raw counts reward the page that was already busier. So we also measured each page against itself:
the peak day divided by that page's own average across the other twenty days in our window.

| Edition | T.O.P | Nana |
|---|---|---|
| Thai | 22.6× | 6.9× |
| English | 13.6× | 8.7× |
| Indonesian | 10.8× | 5.5× |
| Malay | 6.9× | 5.5× |
| Vietnamese | 12.9× | **18.2×** |

By this measure T.O.P takes four of the five, and Vietnamese is the one edition that disagrees with
both of its neighbours.

The reason the two tables point different ways is visible in the week before the news. On 1 October,
Nana's English page drew 2,018 reads and T.O.P's drew 909. She started from more than twice his
footing, so the same event could leave her ahead in total while leaving him further from where he
had been.

Day over day, the two are almost indistinguishable: 909 to 20,568 for T.O.P, 2,018 to 51,120 for
Nana — about 23× and 25×.

## One reason her multiple is understated

Our twenty-day baseline window opens on 13 September, and Nana's English page was not sitting still
in it. It reads 5,108 on 25 September, 4,692 the next day, then falls week by week to 2,018 by 1
October. That decline is the tail of a different story: [her fall from a stage and the
hospitalization that followed](/article/nana-stage-fall-wikipedia-reading-jumped-20-times), which we
measured in September at a peak of 18,446 reads and called our clearest single-cause spike at the
time.

So part of what we are dividing by, for her, is the leftover of an earlier event. Her baseline is
inflated, which makes her 8.7× smaller than it would be against a quiet month. T.O.P's window has no
comparable event in it. We are not correcting for this — we are telling you it is there.

It is also worth recording that 2 October replaced that September figure outright: 51,120 against
18,446, close to three times the spike we had called our largest.

## What the Malay column is doing here

Thirty-five reads and twelve reads are small numbers, and small numbers move for small reasons. We
print them because leaving them out would quietly make the table look steadier than the data is. The
Malay edition agrees with the other four on direction; it should not be read as agreeing with the
same confidence.

One thing we checked before publishing: both names do have articles in all four Southeast Asian
editions, under local titles — "Nana (penyanyi)", "Nana (ca sĩ)", "นานา (นักร้อง)",
"ทีโอพี (แร็ปเปอร์)" and so on. Asking each edition for its own title, rather than assuming the
English one, is the difference between four measured columns and four empty ones.

## What this is not

A page view is a page opened. It is not a ticket sold, a stream played or an opinion held, and it
rises for good news and bad news alike. Nothing here says which of these two people is better known
or better liked — only which article more people opened on one particular Tuesday, and how far each
of those days sat above its own ordinary.
