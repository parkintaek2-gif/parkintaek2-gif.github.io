Two things happened in the same morning feed on 10 September 2026. A BLACKPINK member was
announced as the new face of an iPhone camera. A BIGBANG member was announced as the new face of
McDonald's. Four separate items covered the first and one covered the second, and between them
they described a kind of brand deal that has become ordinary.

We have a panel for exactly this question. It measures how much a brand is read about across the
four Southeast Asian Wikipedia editions our readers use — Indonesian, Vietnamese, Thai and Malay —
on one scale, reads per million reads, so a large edition and a small one can be compared. It has
been running since August.

It covered luxury houses, jewellers and watchmakers, everyday fashion labels, German car makers
and Korean car makers.

It did not cover phones. It did not cover burgers.

## The gap was the finding

That is not a small omission dressed up as a story. A panel that cannot say a single number about
the two kinds in today's news is a panel pointed at where brand deals used to go. Luxury, jewellery,
fashion, cars — that is the shape of the ambassador world of a decade ago, and it is the shape we
had built.

So we added two kinds — technology makers and fast food and coffee chains, four brands each — and
re-measured everything over the **same** twelve months, August 2025 through July 2026. Pageviews
can be asked for past months, which matters: a kind added late does not come out looking small
because it was added late. It sits on the same window as the rest.

The panel now holds 30 brands across seven kinds.

## What the numbers say, and what we nearly said instead

The kind totals came out like this, in reads per million across the four editions:

| Kind | Total | Most-read brand in it |
|---|---:|---|
| Technology makers | 1,382.54 | Google — 75.1% of the kind |
| Luxury houses | 264.74 | Gucci — 22.0% |
| Fast food and coffee chains | 183.67 | McDonald's — 45.8% |
| German car makers | 165.34 | BMW — 40.4% |
| Jewellers and watchmakers | 53.30 | Cartier — 34.4% |
| Everyday fashion labels | 8.75 | Tommy Hilfiger — 100% |
| Korean car makers | 0 | none measurable |

The first line invites a headline: technology is read **5.2 times** as much as every luxury house
here put together. We were about to publish it.

Then we counted inside the kind. Google alone is 1,038.59 of that 1,382.54 — three quarters of it.
Nobody makes a search engine the face of a campaign. Take Google out and technology is 1.30 times
the luxury houses, not 5.2.

**The kind was not overwhelming. One entry was.**

So the comparison we publish is brand against brand, which is smaller and survives scrutiny:

- The phone company is read **172.97** per million. The most-read luxury house on this panel,
  Gucci, is **58.33**. That is about **three times**.
- McDonald's is **84.19**. That is about **one and a half times** Gucci.

Both of the brands that took a K-pop face this week are read more, in these four countries, than
any single luxury house we measure. Not more than the category. More than any one house in it.

## Read that carefully

A technology company's article is read for reasons that have nothing to do with wanting the
brand. People look up a phone model, a company, a founder, a court case. A luxury house's article
is read mostly by people interested in the house. Those are not the same curiosity, and this panel
cannot separate them.

What the numbers do support is narrower, and still worth knowing: **far more people in Indonesia,
Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia end up on the phone company's page than on any single luxury
house's page.** If a face is placed there, more people walk past it.

Reads are not sales. We hold no sales data and are not pretending otherwise.

## The country order changes with the kind

This is the part a single ranking destroys. Thailand reads technology and fast food more than the
other three do. Malaysia leads on luxury houses. Indonesia leads on German cars. The same four
countries change places depending on what kind of brand you ask about — Indonesia sits anywhere
from first to fourth across the kinds thick enough to read.

A league table of 30 brands would have given each country one position and hidden all of that.

## What we could not measure

Burger King has no article on the Thai edition, so it cannot form a four-edition total and is
excluded from the fast-food number — named here rather than quietly dropped.

The same rule is why Korean car makers still read zero. Hyundai, Kia and Genesis are each missing
from at least one of the four editions. Three Korean brands, and not one of them clears the bar
that Mercedes-Benz, BMW and Porsche all clear. We are not filling that in with an estimate. The
zero is a statement about coverage, not about interest, and it is the most uncomfortable number on
the panel.

Everyday fashion labels come with a warning too: only one brand in that kind has all four
articles, so the kind is one brand wearing a category's name. The panel marks it thin.

## Method, in one paragraph

Brand identity and article links come from Wikidata, whose structured data is released under CC0.
Reads come from the Wikimedia Pageviews API, human traffic only, and Wikimedia's own legal page
places its analytics datasets under CC0 as well — we read that page on 10 September 2026 rather
than assuming, having previously filed it under a guess. Every figure is per million reads on that
edition, and the four denominators are published. A brand enters a kind total only when all four
editions carry an article about it.

The full panel, including the country-by-country split and the edition denominators, is at
[brand kinds](/brand-kinds).
