Take everything we have measured across Indonesia, Vietnam, Thailand and Malaysia, and ask a narrow question of each category: what share of the four-country reading happens in the Malay edition?

| Category | Subjects | Malaysia's share |
|---|---|---|
| Groups and bands | 255 | 8.6% |
| Solo musicians | 1,446 | 8.5% |
| Actors | 1,023 | 7.8% |
| Athletes | 900 | 7.6% |
| Luxury, fashion and car brands | 22 | **23.0%** |

Four categories of people sit inside a 1.0-point band. Brands sit at 2.8 times their average.

That is worth stating carefully, because the obvious reading is wrong. This is not "Malaysia is a small market so everything is small there." Every figure above is already divided by the size of each Wikipedia. Malaysia's share of *people* is low. Its share of *brands* is not.

Balenciaga and Prada are read more in the Malay edition than in the other three combined, on this scale. Dior is the exception in the other direction and sits close to the people band.

SISTAR disbanded in 2017. Ahn So-hee left Wonder Girls in 2013. Cho Kyu-hyun and Kim Jae-joong debuted in the mid-2000s. The names Malaysia over-reads relative to its neighbours skew towards an earlier decade of K-pop than the names that lead the region overall — BTS, Babymonster, Go Youn-jung.

We are not going to tell you why. A share is not a reason, and we did not measure when anyone's audience formed.

## What this cannot say

The Malay Wikipedia is not Malaysia. Malay is read outside the country, and many Malaysian readers use the English or Chinese editions instead — the English Wikipedia alone drew 82 billion reads in this window and cannot be split by country. So this measures an edition, not a nation, and every figure is a floor.

Subjects with a four-country total below 3 are left out of the two lists above. At that size one burst of reading turns into a large-looking share.

The brand list is 22 names we chose and resolved to Wikidata items by their English article titles. It is a sample, not a census, and a different 22 would move the 23% figure.
