The press line on *KPop Demon Hunters* has settled into one sentence: a year on Netflix's Top 10.
It is repeated with the number and without the place. **"On the Top 10" is not one chart.** It is
93 separate country charts, and the sentence is true in almost none of them.

We hold the weekly country tables — 501,040 rows across 269 weeks. **3,043 of those rows are this
film.** They cover 93 countries and **62 distinct weeks**, from 2025-06-22 to 2026-08-23.

## 62 weeks is the ceiling, and one country hit it

Sixty-two is not a country's score. It is the number of weeks the film appeared *anywhere*. So a
country reaching 62 means it never missed a week the film was on any chart on earth.

**One did.**

| Country | Weeks on chart | Longest unbroken run | Times it dropped out | Peak rank |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| **Latvia** | **62** | **62** | **0** | 1 |
| Estonia | 60 | 51 | 2 | 1 |
| Lithuania | 59 | 51 | 3 | 1 |
| Serbia | 57 | 51 | 2 | 1 |
| Mexico | 51 | 45 | 5 | 1 |
| United States | 49 | 46 | 2 | 1 |
| **South Korea** | **32** | **32** | **0** | 1 |

Four countries of 93 passed 52 weeks. Three of the four are Baltic states; the fourth is Serbia.
Estonia, Lithuania and Serbia each ran 51 weeks unbroken and then came back after a gap — so by
the strict reading, **a continuous year happened in exactly one country.**

## Korea is the median

The film is Korean. Korea held it **32 weeks** — and 32 is the **median of all 93 countries**.
Forty-two countries held it longer than the country that made it.

| Weeks on chart | Countries |
| --- | ---: |
| 60–62 | 2 |
| 52–59 | 2 |
| 40–51 | 21 |
| 30–39 | 29 |
| 20–29 | 31 |
| under 20 | 8 |

Korea's 32 weeks were **unbroken** — it never dropped out and came back. That is the rarer shape:
only **15 of 93** countries have a zero in the drop-out column, and most of those are short runs.
Seventy-eight countries lost it and got it back at least once.

## Dropping out is the normal case

This is the part the single number hides. Across the 93 countries, the film left the Top 10 and
returned in 78 of them. The Netflix column that reports weeks-in-top-10 counts **cumulatively, not
consecutively** — we checked that separately against the raw rows — so a country credited with 50
weeks may have been off the chart repeatedly inside that span.

Mexico is the clearest case: 51 weeks on the chart, five separate exits, longest continuous run 45
weeks. By the cumulative column Mexico "made a year." By the calendar it did not.

| | Countries |
| --- | ---: |
| Never dropped out | 15 |
| Dropped out at least once | 78 |
| Reached rank 1 at some point | 76 |

Seventy-six of 93 countries put it at number one at least once. **Reaching the top was ordinary.
Staying was not.**

## What this does not say

It does not say Latvia watched it most. Latvia has about 1.8 million people and ten Top 10 slots
to fill each week, and a country with a thinner competing catalogue holds a hit longer for reasons
that have nothing to do with enthusiasm. Weeks on a chart measure **how long a title kept a slot
against local competition**, not how many people pressed play. We do not add the weeks across
countries, and we do not convert them into viewers.

It also stops at 2026-08-23. That is the last week in the tables we hold. Whether the film is on
a Top 10 this week is something we have not measured, and we will not write it down as zero.
