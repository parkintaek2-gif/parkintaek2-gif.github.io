A culture-industry forum in Seoul this week put a number on where Korean content could go next — a 400 trillion won K-culture economy — and named the unfinished task plainly: visitors arrive, and most of them stay in and around the capital. Spreading that demand to smaller cities was described as the turning point still ahead.

We went to the admissions table to see what is already there. It says something different from the framing, and it also says the map everyone is arguing over has holes in it.

## The capital region is not where most counted foreign admissions are

Korea's statistics portal publishes admissions to **counted paid tourist sites**, splitting domestic from foreign. For 2023, the latest year published, the province rows hold **7,961,838 foreign admissions**.

| | Foreign admissions | Share |
|---|---|---|
| Capital region — Seoul, Gyeonggi, Incheon | 3,358,821 | **42.2%** |
| Everywhere else | 4,603,017 | **57.8%** |

A majority is already outside the capital region. And the comparison that usually gets skipped: **domestic visitors are less capital-weighted than foreign ones, not more.** 32.0 percent of domestic admissions fall in the capital region, against 42.2 percent of foreign. Foreign visitors do lean toward the capital — but the lean is ten points, not the whole picture.

## The single biggest district is Gangneung, not Jongno

At district level the order is not the one the framing implies.

| # | District | Foreign admissions | Running share |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Gangneung, Gangwon | 1,847,775 | 23.4% |
| 2 | Jongno, Seoul | 1,683,727 | 44.8% |
| 3 | Namwon, North Jeolla | 783,010 | 54.7% |
| 4 | Chuncheon, Gangwon | 778,148 | 64.5% |
| 5 | Yongin, Gyeonggi | 671,390 | 73.1% |

Five districts hold **73.1 percent** of district-level foreign admissions; ten hold **90.8 percent**. That concentration is real and it is the story worth telling — but three of the top five are not in the capital region, and first place is a city on the east coast.

## Where foreign visitors weigh most heavily is also outside Seoul

Raw counts favour big places. The other question is what share of a district's visitors are foreign — who depends on them.

| District | Foreign admissions | Foreign share of that district's admissions |
|---|---|---|
| Gangneung | 1,847,775 | **39.5%** |
| Namwon | 783,010 | **39.4%** |
| Hongcheon | 102,788 | 24.0% |
| Jongno | 1,683,727 | 19.5% |
| Chuncheon | 778,148 | 12.5% |

Two out of every five people entering a counted site in Gangneung or Namwon are foreign. In Jongno — the heart of the Seoul tourist circuit — it is one in five. On this measure the provinces are not waiting to be reached. They are already the places where foreign visitors matter most.

## The part that should stop the argument: 112 districts count nobody

Of 206 district rows, **112 record no foreign visitor at all.** Among them is **Jung District, which contains Myeongdong** — one of the most-visited shopping areas in the country.

That is not a finding about Myeongdong. It is a finding about the ruler. The table counts **paid** attractions. A district whose draw is a street, a market, a shopping quarter or a café strip can record nothing here while being full of visitors every day. We wrote up [the same gap for Seoul's 25 districts](/article/where-the-counting-stops) in August; at national scale it is wider. The district figures, set beside what people actually read about those places, are on [read against visited](/read-vs-visited).

So the honest reading is in two parts, and both have to be said together:

- Of the foreign admissions Korea can count, most are already outside the capital region, and the biggest single draw is Gangneung.
- More than half of all districts are invisible to this count, so nobody — including us — currently knows the true national distribution.

A plan to move visitors into smaller cities is a plan to change a number. Before it can be judged, the number has to exist for the places it is meant to change. Right now, for 112 districts, it does not.

## What this cannot tell you

It cannot tell you why anyone came, and it cannot attribute a single admission to a concert, a film or a drama. It counts entries, not people — one visitor entering three sites is three. It is 2023, the latest year published, so it is not a reading of last year's arrivals. And it does not measure spending or nights stayed, which is what the forum was actually arguing about.

What it does is set a floor under the conversation: whatever the plan, it should start from a map that counts more than 94 of Korea's 206 districts.
