A Korean gaming forum ran a thread this week with a plain title: why lesser-known idol
groups are said to lose money on every event they play. Twenty replies, the usual mix of
insiders and guessing.

We cannot answer it. We hold no fee data, no ticket data and no attendance data for any
Korean act, and nothing below is a claim about anybody's finances. But the thread is
reaching for something that **is** measurable — the distance between the acts everyone has
heard of and everyone else — so we measured that.

## 24 acts of 774

In the single month of August 2026, 774 Korean music acts drew **2,670,482** reads on
English Wikipedia between them. Counting down from the most-read:

| The most-read | Share of the month's reading |
|---|---:|
| Top 1 of 774 | 9.7% |
| Top 3 | 18.0% |
| Top 5 | 22.9% |
| Top 10 | 33.5% |
| Top 20 | 47.0% |
| Top 50 | 64.6% |
| Top 100 | 78.4% |
| Top 200 | 89.8% |

**Twenty-four acts hold half of it** — 3.1% of the list. BTS alone accounts for 9.7%, more
than the bottom 387 acts put together.

That last comparison is worth stating twice, because the symmetry is easy to miss: the top
3.1% of the list and the bottom 50% of the list hold **the same share** of the month's
reading, 3.1% each.

## The median act was looked up 519 times

Not 519 thousand. **519.** In a month, in English, worldwide.

That number is the one that makes the forum thread legible. Whatever the economics of
playing an event are, the act in the middle of this list is attempting them at a level of
outside recognition of about seventeen page-opens a day — and a page-open is not a person,
since one reader returning three times counts three. The act at the top is attempting the
same thing at 260,085 reads a month.

We are not saying the second act earns more. We are saying they are not doing the same job.

## Two counts, and the one place they disagree

A single month could be a fluke — a comeback lands, an act spikes, the shape distorts. So
we ran the same measurement on a different act set over a different window, collected
separately: 380 acts, twelve months, act pages only.

| The most-read | One month, 774 acts | Twelve months, 380 acts | Gap |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Top 1 | 9.7% | 9.8% | 0.0 points |
| Top 10 | 33.5% | 36.4% | 2.9 points |
| Top 20 | 47.0% | 49.9% | 3.0 points |
| Top 100 | 78.4% | 84.9% | 6.5 points |

At the very top they are almost identical — 9.7% against 9.8%, which is closer than we
expected and not something we arranged. Further down they drift, by up to 6.5 points at the
top hundred.

We are printing the drift rather than the flattering row, and the reason for it is
structural rather than interesting: the twelve-month set contains fewer acts, so "the top
hundred" is a larger slice of a shorter list. What survives both counts is the finding.
Roughly a tenth of everything sits on one act, and about half of everything sits inside the
top twenty, whether you look at one month or twelve.

## What we are not saying

A Wikipedia read is not money. An act can be read rarely and tour profitably, playing rooms
that fit its audience; an act can be read constantly and lose money on a production that
does not. We have no data that would let us tell those cases apart, and this article does
not try.

What the count does establish is that the two ends of Korean music are not operating in the
same conditions of recognition, and that the middle is much closer to the bottom than to the
top. Anyone arguing about the economics is arguing about that shape, whether or not they
have seen it as a number.
