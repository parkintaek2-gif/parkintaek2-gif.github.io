Mattel has dated its Saja Boys figures. LEGO sets are being talked about for 2027. The
merchandise for *KPop Demon Hunters* is arriving now — and the film has already been
sitting in national Netflix Top 10s for well over a year.

We counted how many weeks it actually charted, country by country, from the raw weekly
tables rather than from anyone's summary. 93 countries put it in a Top 10 at least once.

## The longest runs are not where you would guess

| Country | Weeks in the Top 10 | Longest unbroken run | Weeks it dropped out and came back |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Latvia | **62** | **62** | 0 |
| Estonia | 60 | 51 | 2 |
| Lithuania | 59 | 51 | 3 |
| Serbia | 57 | 51 | 2 |
| **Mexico** | **51** | 45 | 5 |
| Bulgaria | 49 | 44 | 4 |
| **United States** | 49 | 46 | 2 |
| Taiwan | 44 | 43 | 1 |
| **South Korea** | 32 | 32 | 0 |

Latvia never let it fall out once — 62 weeks, unbroken, which is every week the title
charted anywhere. Mexico gave it 51 weeks, two more than the United States. South Korea,
where the film is set, gave it 32, and also never dropped it.

## Mexico is worth its own line this week

Mexico ranks fifth in the world by chart weeks for this title, and Canal 5 has just
picked up a dubbed Korean drama. Those are two separate facts about the same audience,
arriving in the same week.

Mexico's run was not smooth — the film fell out of the Top 10 and climbed back five
separate times, tied with Bolivia for the most re-entries among the eight longest runs.
That pattern is the opposite of Latvia's. One country held it steadily; the other kept
going back to it.

## 76 of the 93 countries put it at number one

Reaching the top spot turns out to be the common outcome, not the exceptional one. What
separates countries is not whether they got there but how long they stayed.

| | Countries |
| --- | ---: |
| Charted at all | 93 |
| Reached number one | 76 |
| Charted 50 weeks or more | 5 |
| Never dropped out once | 15 |

The fifteen that never dropped out are a strange list: Latvia, Hong Kong, Peru,
Argentina, South Korea, Colombia, Jamaica, the United Arab Emirates, the Bahamas,
Germany, Qatar, Trinidad and Tobago, the Netherlands, Bangladesh, Pakistan. Three are in
South America, three in the English-speaking Caribbean, two in the Gulf, and two in South
Asia. Only two are in western Europe.

## What a year on the chart looks like next to everything else Korean

A long Netflix run is one thing. Attention outside the platform is another, and it fades
much faster.

We have measured, separately, how long a Korean title holds the *reading* it gains: for
[26 Korean titles with enough months to measure](/half-life), the median time to lose half
of their peak Wikipedia readership was **two months**. Encyclopedia reading and chart weeks are
not the same measure and we do not add them together — but the contrast is the point.
The thing that lasts 62 weeks on a chart can lose half its lookups in eight.

The same asymmetry shows up in comics. Korean webtoons that get adapted are [read
**fourteen times** more](/webtoon-adaptations) once the drama exists than they were as comics; Japanese manga in
the same position, only 3.7 times. Korean stories, in English, tend to acquire their
audience from the adaptation rather than bring one to it. *KPop Demon Hunters* is that
pattern at full size — an animated film with a fictional K-pop group inside it, charting
for 62 weeks, with the toys arriving afterwards.

## Method

Every country-week here was read from the published weekly Top 10 table for that country,
501,040 rows in all. Russia is excluded, as in all our by-country counts. A week counts
only if the title appears in that country's own published Top 10 for that week; we never
add weeks across countries into a single total, because a week in Latvia and a week in
the United States are not the same quantity of anything. The data ends with the week of
2026-08-23. Whether the film is still charting after that date, we have not measured, and
we do not fill it in.
