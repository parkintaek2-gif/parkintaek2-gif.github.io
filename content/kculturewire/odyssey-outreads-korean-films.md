Korean Wikipedia opened the article on *The Odyssey* — Christopher Nolan's 2026 film, with Matt Damon as Odysseus and Anne Hathaway as Penelope — **119,356 times** during August.

Fourteen Korean films released in 2026 have a Korean Wikipedia article. Added together, over the same thirty days, they drew **19,822**.

**The ratio is 6.0 to 1**, on the Korean-language encyclopedia, about films in a Korean release year.

## One day against a month

*The Odyssey*'s biggest single day was **7,569 reads, on 8 August**.

The best Korean film of 2026 on this measure is *Hope* (호프), with **5,633 reads across the entire month**.

So one Tuesday of the Nolan film outdrew every Korean 2026 release's whole August. Not the best of them — every one of them.

**Eight of the fourteen drew under a thousand reads in a month.** Five drew under 250 — about eight people a day, across a country of fifty-one million.

## What we could not measure, and are not going to pretend we did

The number attached to *The Odyssey* in Korean coverage is nine million admissions. **We did not measure that and we are not reporting it as ours.** Korea's admissions data lives in KOBIS, the Korean Film Council's service, and reading it requires a key we do not hold. We asked for one; until it arrives, ticket sales are a hole in this page and we are marking it as a hole rather than filling it with someone else's arithmetic.

What we can measure is what people looked up. That is a different thing, and it is worth being precise about which one you are reading.

## The honest caveat about timing

These fourteen films were released at different points in 2026, and we compared them over one shared window in August. A film that opened in February has spent six months decaying; a film that opened in August is at its peak. That works **in the Korean films' disfavour for the older ones and in their favour for the newer ones**, and we cannot separate the two effects with this data.

It does not close a gap of six to one.

## Why this is a K-culture story

Korean film's export decade is well documented — *Parasite*, *Squid Game*, the festival circuit. This is the same measurement pointed the other way: what the domestic audience was reading about in the month the year's biggest picture was a Hollywood one.

A single foreign release taking six times the encyclopedia attention of an entire domestic release year is a fact about the home market, and it is measurable without any box-office data at all.
