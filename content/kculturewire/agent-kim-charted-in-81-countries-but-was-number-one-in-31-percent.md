Four Korean Netflix titles carried in dozens of countries this year did not top the chart in most of them. One, Agent Kim, was carried in 81 countries and topped the chart in 25 of them — 30.9%, or 31% rounded.

## What the numbers say

We read Netflix's own weekly per-country Top 10 tables and counted, for each title, how many countries ever carried it against how many of those countries it ever reached number one in.

| Title | Countries carrying it | Countries reaching #1 | Share reaching #1 |
|---|---|---|---|
| KPop Demon Hunters | 93 | 76 | 81.7% |
| Teach You a Lesson | 91 | 46 | 50.5% |
| Our Sticky Love | 68 | 27 | 39.7% |
| Agent Kim | 81 | 25 | 30.9% |

Being carried widely and topping the chart widely are different things. KPop Demon Hunters did both — carried in 93 countries, number one in more than four out of five of them. Agent Kim was carried almost as broadly, 81 countries, but topped the chart in fewer than a third.

## Why this matters for reading a headline

A press line describing a title as topping "the non-English chart" or "charts in dozens of countries" is describing reach, not dominance within that reach — and the two move independently. Teach You a Lesson and Our Sticky Love sit in between: carried nearly as widely as the two extremes, topping the chart in roughly half and roughly a third of those countries respectively.

## What this can and cannot say

This is four titles chosen by how often they appeared in our own collection that week, not a random sample of Korean Netflix output, and it says nothing about why a widely carried title fails to top the chart in most of the places carrying it — release-week crowding, dubbing timelines, and genre fit could all play a role, and this count cannot separate them. What it does establish is that "carried in N countries" and "number one in N countries" are two different counts, and a title can score high on one while scoring low on the other.
