People plan around a K-drama season the way they plan around a football season. We had no idea
whether one existed, so we counted it: across 93 markets and 265 weeks, what share of Netflix's
published top-10 places did Korean titles hold in each calendar month?

## Twelve months, 2.2 points

| Month | Korean share | Without that month's biggest title |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| January | 8.4% | 6.8% |
| February | 7.6% | 6.7% |
| March | 7.4% | 6.8% |
| April | 7.8% | 7.1% |
| May | 7% | 6.5% |
| June | 6.7% | 6.2% |
| July | 8.5% | 7.3% |
| August | 7.7% | 7.3% |
| September | 8.8% | 8.2% |
| October | 7.9% | 6.9% |
| November | 6.6% | 5.8% |
| December | 7.4% | 6.6% |

The whole year sits at 7.7%. The best month is 8.9% and the worst is 6.7%. **That is the entire
range: 2.2 points.**

For comparison, our [world-share page](/world-share) records the highest single week at 16.1% and
the lowest at 2.0% — a range of **14.1 points, 6.4 times the monthly one.** Whatever moves this
number, it is not the month of the year.

## The obvious suspect, and what happened to it

A two-point wobble across twelve months could easily be one show. *Squid Game* is the largest
Korean title in **five of the twelve months**, and in January it alone accounts for a fifth of every
Korean chart place in the month.

So we removed each month's own biggest title and measured again — that is the right-hand column
above. If the swing were a title effect, the band should collapse.

It does not. The band goes from 2.2 points to **2.4**. Taking the biggest title out of every month
leaves the monthly pattern slightly *wider* than before, which is the opposite of what a
one-title explanation predicts.

What we are left with is a very small monthly pattern that we cannot attribute to a single release
and cannot explain. We are printing the twelve numbers and stopping there.

## Why this is worth knowing anyway

A finding of "no pattern" is worth as much as a pattern when people are already acting as if the
pattern exists. If you are deciding when to look for Korean titles on Netflix, the honest answer
from five years of charts is that the calendar will not help you. The week will.

That also sets the scale for anything else we publish about this data. A monthly figure that moves
by two points is close to flat; when we report a change of ten points — as we did for
[the nine Asian markets that fell from 30.3% to 19.5%](/article/the-share-that-did-not-grow) — that
is five times the size of the biggest seasonal swing in the same data, which is part of why we
believe it.

## What this does not say

It does not say Korean releases are evenly spread through the year. Netflix publishes charts, not
schedules, and a title released in March can hold places in June. The two questions are different
and only one of them is answerable here.

It does not say September is a good month to release into. A 2.2-point band across five years,
which does not survive as a story once you look at what is inside it, is not a planning tool and we
would rather say so than dress it up as one.
