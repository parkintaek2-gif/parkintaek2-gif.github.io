Korean entertainers credited on Wikidata as doing only music or only acting are measurably read on English Wikipedia about one time in eight. People credited with both are read about one time in two.

## What the numbers say

We split every Korean person Wikidata credits as a musician or an actor (or both) into three groups, then checked how many of each group had any English Wikipedia reading at all in a 30-day window.

| Group | Roster | Measurably read | Capture rate | Median reads/day (if measured) | Median language editions |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Music only | 3,671 | 463 | 12.6% | 25 | 1 |
| Acting only | 4,554 | 638 | 14.0% | 123 | 1 |
| Both | 992 | 471 | 47.5% | 76 | 10 |

Acting-only names that do get measured are read more per person (123 vs 25) than music-only names. But far fewer of either single-craft group clear the bar of having any English reading at all. The crossover group clears that bar at more than three times the rate — and their Wikipedia articles exist in ten language editions at the median, against one for a single-craft name.

## Who is in the crossover group

So Ji-sub (5,227 reads/day), Steven Yeun (3,701), Eric Nam (2,957), Jisoo of BLACKPINK (2,936), and Cha Eun-woo (2,363) lead the 992-person crossover list by daily reads. Six of the top ten are solo acts rather than group members — doing both crafts, not group size, is what this list has in common.

## What this can and cannot say

This cannot separate cause from effect. It is plausible that already-wide attention is what gets a musician cast in a drama, or an actor signed to a label, rather than doing both crafts creating the attention. What the data does show cleanly is the co-occurrence: whichever direction the arrow runs, being credited in both crafts tracks with a Wikipedia footprint — measured in language editions, not just raw reads — that single-craft names mostly do not reach.
