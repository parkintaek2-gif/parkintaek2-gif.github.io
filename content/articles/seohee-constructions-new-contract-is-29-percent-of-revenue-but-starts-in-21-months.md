Seohee Construction (KOSDAQ: 035890) filed a single-sales-contract disclosure with Korea's
Financial Supervisory Service on 21 September, 2026, for an apartment construction project in
Jeonpo-dong, Busan. The confirmed contract value is 318.27 billion won &mdash; 28.93% of the
company's most recently reported annual revenue, by the filing's own calculation.

## What the filing says

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Counterparty | Jeonpo 1-dong Regional Housing Association |
| Confirmed contract amount | &#8361;318,270,000,000 |
| Most recent annual revenue (per filing) | &#8361;1,100,130,344,137 |
| Contract as % of revenue | 28.93% |
| Contract start date | 30 June 2027 |
| Contract end date | 29 February 2032 |
| Advance payment | None |
| Payment terms | Billed and paid every two months, based on construction progress |

We checked the revenue figure independently against our own DART-financials archive: Seohee's
FY2025 consolidated revenue was 1,100,130,344,137 won &mdash; an exact match to the number cited in
today's filing.

## Why the start date matters here specifically

The counterparty is a 지역주택조합 (regional housing cooperative), a Korean development structure
where residents pool funds and contract a builder before the cooperative has necessarily secured
land rights, member financing, or regulatory approval to build. Construction on today's contract is
not scheduled to begin for about 21 months.

That gap matters for this company in particular because it has a documented history with this exact
setup. Etoday reported that Seohee had an 88.3 billion won contract with a Changwon Jinhae-area
cooperative cancelled, and a separate 76.66 billion won contract with a Yangsan cooperative also
cancelled, in both cases before the projects were completed. A separate report describes Seohee
losing a first-instance lawsuit last May after a Gumi-area cooperative cancelled its contract with
the company and moved to a different builder.

None of that is evidence about what happens to this Busan contract. Regional-housing-cooperative
deals are also common business for large Korean builders, and a signed contract is routine
disclosure, not a red flag by itself. What we can say is narrower: a contract representing close to
a third of last year's revenue has been filed, and it will not begin generating work for close to
two years, during which the underlying cooperative still has to complete the steps that, in this
company's own recent history, more than one similarly structured deal did not survive.
