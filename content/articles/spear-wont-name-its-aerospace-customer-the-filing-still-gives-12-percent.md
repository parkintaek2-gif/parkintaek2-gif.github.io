Spear (KRX: 347700) filed a single sale/supply contract disclosure on 23 September, 2026, for a
special-alloy supply deal worth 12.10% of its revenue. Korean disclosure rules let a company withhold
a counterparty's name when the counterparty requests confidentiality — this filing uses that
provision, identifying the buyer only as "a US global aerospace launch company."

## The filing, as stated

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Contract content | Special-alloy supply contract |
| Contract amount | ₩11,576,320,000 |
| Ratio to revenue | 12.10% |
| Counterparty | Withheld: "US global aerospace launch company" |
| Counterparty's business | Withheld: "counterparty's business-confidentiality request" |

## What the filing withholds, and what it doesn't

The identity of the buyer and its line of business are the two fields the filing withholds, both
citing the same stated reason — the counterparty's own request. Everything else required under
Korea's single sale/supply contract disclosure rule is reported in full: the contract amount, the
ratio to Spear's revenue, and the type of goods (special alloys). This is a narrower withholding than
the embargo mechanism used elsewhere in Korean disclosures, where an entire amount can be withheld
for a stated period — here, only identity fields are blanked, and no embargo deadline is listed.

## What this filing does and doesn't tell you

The filing confirms the size and category of the deal without confirming who is buying. "US global
aerospace launch company" narrows the field of plausible counterparties but does not identify one,
and we have not independently confirmed the buyer's identity for this piece. What the filing does
establish, on its own terms, is that a materials contract equal to roughly one-eighth of Spear's
revenue was signed with a customer large enough, and sensitive enough about its supply chain, to
require anonymity as a condition of the deal being disclosed at all.
