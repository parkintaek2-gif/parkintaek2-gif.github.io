Korea files who runs its listed companies — every officer, with a gender beside the name. Add it up and the boardroom is one of the least mixed rooms in the economy: **7.2%** of officers are women, and most companies have none at all.

## Seven percent, and it isn't evenly thin

Across 35,004 disclosed officers, 2,531 are women. But the 7.2% is a market average hiding a sector split. Health care seats women in about **one board seat in ten** (10.5%), finance close behind at 9%. At the other end, information technology — half the market's value — sits at **4.2%**, and heavy industry at 4.9%. The sectors that carry the market's weight carry the fewest women at the top.

## More than half have none

The starker number is per company, not per seat. Of 2,922 listed firms, **1,281 — 44% — disclose at least one woman officer.** The other **56% disclose none.** A majority of Korea's listed companies file a board and management with not a single woman on it.

## What the number is, and isn't

This counts officers as filed — it does not sort executive from outside directors, or owner from hired hand. Those splits exist in the data and are their own stories. What this one says is plain and hard to round away: at the level of who is named as running a Korean listed company, women are one in fourteen, and at more than half of firms, none. It is filed, every year, by the companies themselves.
