Every so often a familiar headline runs: **Korea's number-one trading partner.** It is a clean rank, and it invites a clean conclusion — that the country at the top is the one Korea can least afford to lose. The rank is real. The conclusion does not follow.

A trade rank measures **volume**. Dependence is about something else: whether the thing traded can be replaced, and whether the same partner sits on both sides of the ledger. On both counts, the ranking quietly misleads.

## Selling and buying point at different countries

The first thing a single "top partner" list hides is that Korea's **export map and its import map are not the same map**. Sort by who Korea sells to and you get one order; sort by who it buys from and you get another. [Korea sells to and buys from different countries](https://seoulmarkets.com/article/korea-sells-and-buys-different-countries): Hong Kong takes about 6.7% of exports but supplies only ~0.6% of imports; Japan supplies about 7.5% of imports but takes only ~3.5% of exports. A country can be near the top of one list and far down the other. "Biggest partner" collapses two different relationships into one rank.

## Concentration is not irreplaceability

The second thing the rank hides is that being large is not the same as being essential. Korea's trade is highly concentrated — measure it any way and [the top handful of partners are about half of everything](https://seoulmarkets.com/article/korea-everything-comes-down-to-four). But concentration tells you where the volume sits, not how hard it would be to source the same goods elsewhere. A large partner in an easily substituted commodity is less of a dependence than a small partner who is the only supplier of one critical input. The rank cannot see that difference; it only counts.

## What we publish, and what we don't

We build Korea's trade pages from customs data using **shares and ranks only** — never single-month absolute dollar figures, which our own dataset flags as unreliable. So we will show you [the partner-by-partner shares](https://seoulmarkets.com/trade) and let you compare the export and import maps yourself. What we will not do is hand you a single "most important partner" and call the question answered. The rank is a starting point, not a verdict.

## The debate

So: does "biggest trading partner" tell you who Korea most depends on — or just who it trades the most with? Volume is easy to rank; dependence is not. **What do you think?** This is a talking point, not a verdict — argue it out below.
