The Korean stock market is, by value, a technology market — electronics and chips are more than half of it. So it is worth knowing that technology is not where the market pays best. That is finance, and it is not close.

## Finance pays double

Taking the median listed company in each sector, average pay runs from **115.8 million won** a year in finance down to **53.7 million** in consumer staples. Information technology — half the market's capitalisation — sits at **57.8 million**, near the bottom of the table, paying barely more than supermarkets and less than shipbuilders.

| Sector | Median average pay (₩/yr) |
| --- | ---: |
| Finance | 115,840,575 |
| Materials | 61,618,414 |
| Industrials | 61,015,687 |
| Communication services | 60,857,351 |
| Information technology | 57,843,971 |
| Consumer discretionary | 57,444,444 |
| Health care | 57,139,073 |
| Consumer staples | 53,717,391 |

Finance pays roughly **twice** what technology does. The heavy-industry sectors — materials, machinery, chemicals — cluster just above IT at around 61 million. Everything from tech down to staples sits in a narrow band between 54 and 58 million; finance stands alone at the top.

## Value and pay are different maps

The gap between where the market puts its value and where it pays its people is the point. A KOSPI investor is buying, overwhelmingly, technology. A well-paid Korean is, disproportionately, in finance. The market cap says which companies the market believes in; the pay says which ones share the most with the people inside — and in Korea those are not the same sector.

The caution, as always with a pay column: this is the median company's average, not any one paycheque, and bonuses and hours vary. But the shape is clear and it does not move under a median. Finance is where listed Korea pays, and technology, for all its weight, is not.
