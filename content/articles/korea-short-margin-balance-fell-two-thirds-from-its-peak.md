Korea's short-margin balance — shares retail investors have borrowed specifically to sell short
through margin accounts — stood at 31.3 billion won on September 2, 2026. That is down 43.1
percent from 54.9 billion won a year earlier, and less than a third of the series' all-time high
of 94.0 billion won, set on February 20, 2023.

## A different line item from margin debt

| | Value |
|---|---:|
| Short-margin balance, Sept 2, 2026 | 31.3 billion won |
| Short-margin balance, Sept 2, 2025 | 54.9 billion won |
| Year-over-year change | −43.1% |
| All-time high (Feb 20, 2023) | 94.0 billion won |
| Current level vs. all-time high | −66.7% |

KOFIA reports short-margin balance (신용대주) separately from margin financing (신용융자, money
borrowed to buy shares) and from pledged-securities loans. The three do not move together by
construction — one reflects leveraged buying, one reflects a specific short-selling channel open
to retail accounts, and one reflects loans collateralized by securities already held. This piece
covers only the short-margin figure.

## What a falling balance does and does not tell you

A smaller short-margin balance means fewer shares are currently borrowed through this specific
retail channel than a year ago, or at the 2023 peak. It does not tell you whether retail
investors have become more bullish, whether short-margin trading has shifted to another channel,
or how this retail-only figure relates to institutional and foreign short-selling activity, which
KRX reports through an entirely separate disclosure system this dataset does not cover.

## Method

Figures come from KOFIA's published credit-balance statistics, which report short-margin
balance as its own category alongside margin financing and pledged-securities loans. We compare
the same calendar date one year apart and report the series maximum without adjustment. Not
investment advice.
