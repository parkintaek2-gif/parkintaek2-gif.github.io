THE CODI (KOSDAQ: 224060) filed two convertible bond issuance decisions with Korea's Financial
Supervisory Service on consecutive days in September 2026. The first, board-approved 7 September,
would convert into more shares than the company had outstanding before the filing. The second,
approved one day later, is a fraction of the size.

## What the two filings say, side by side

| Field | Bond filed 7 Sep | Bond filed 8 Sep |
|---|---|---|
| Face value | &#8361;30,000,000,000 | &#8361;3,000,000,000 |
| Conversion price | &#8361;3,550 | &#8361;4,463 |
| Convertible shares | 8,450,704 | 672,193 |
| Dilution (filing's own figure) | **163.75%** | 13.03% |
| Coupon (stated / yield) | 3.00% / 4.00% | 3.00% / 4.00% |
| Maturity | 6 October 2029 | 6 October 2029 |

The first bond's own dilution figure &mdash; 163.75% &mdash; means the shares it could create outnumber
the shares that existed before the filing. Working backward from that percentage and the 8,450,704
share count, pre-issuance shares outstanding come to roughly 5.16 million. At the 3,550 won conversion
price, that implies a whole-company value of around 18.3 billion won standing against a single 30
billion won bond.

## What changed between the two filings

Everything about the second bond that could be compared to the first is smaller or different: a tenth
of the face value, a conversion price 25.7% higher, and a dilution figure roughly an eighth the size.
Both mature on the identical date and share the same coupon structure. Nothing in either filing's
structured data explains the one-day gap or the price difference, and we have not found independent
reporting that does.

## What we're not claiming

We are reporting two DART filings' own stated figures, not forecasting whether either bond converts,
what the company's share count looks like today, or why the financing was structured this way. A
163.75% dilution figure on paper describes what full conversion would create, not a certainty that it
will happen.
