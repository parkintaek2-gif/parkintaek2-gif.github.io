Korea's market has one number everyone repeats: the KOSPI is **up about 125% in two years**. It is true, and it is the least informative thing you can say about where the market is now.

## The headline hides the turn

The same index is **down 31.3% from its peak** on **22 June 2026**. And that peak is the right place to look from, because when you measure every index from it, the market that the +125% headline describes has already turned.

Of the **160 tradeable indices** the exchange publishes, only **12 are above their 22-June level**. **148 are below it.** The **median index is down 19.3%** from the peak. This is not a market pausing near a high — it is a market that ran, topped, and broadly reversed, with the average corner well into a correction.

## The rally was chips. So is the correction.

The two-year gain was never the whole market; it was a narrow set of leaders. The KRX **Electronics** index is **+238.8%** over two years — nearly double the KOSPI's rise. But narrow leadership cuts both ways. Measured from the June peak, the leaders have fallen the hardest:

- **Information technology — 40.9%**
- **Semiconductors — 40.5%**
- **Electronics — 38.7%**
- KOSPI — 31.3%

Everything that led is down more than the index. What is *up* since the peak is the opposite kind of stock — the defensive, dividend, slow-growth kind:

- **Banks + 6.1%**
- **Consumer staples + 3.6%** (and +4.7% on the KRX 300 cut)
- **Utilities + 0.1%**

Banks never even peaked in June; their high was back in February, and they have given up less than 10% since.

## Why this is the story, not the +125%

A single index number is a weighted average, and in a market this [concentrated](/data/concentration) the average is the few largest names. When two chip stocks are [half the trading](/article/korea-market-barbell-silent-tail) and the biggest sector indices are semiconductors, the KOSPI *is* the chip trade — on the way up and on the way down. The breadth number (12 of 160) and the median (−19.3%) say what the headline cannot: most of the Korean market is not where the KOSPI is.

We are not calling a top or a bottom, and this is a dated reading (early August). The point is narrower and, we think, more useful than a forecast: **when you look at the whole distribution instead of one number, Korea's rally was never broad, and its correction already is.**

Not investment advice — percentage changes from official KRX index closes, every figure dated.
