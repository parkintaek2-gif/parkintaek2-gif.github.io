Korean press this week returned to a familiar complaint about foreign money: overseas retail investors want Korean stocks, but research on Korean companies is hard to reach, and hardest for smaller KOSDAQ names. Large investment banks have their own channels. Individuals abroad do not.

Foreign retail interest is measurable. Through omnibus accounts alone, foreign investors traded **5.8 trillion won** of Korean stocks and net bought **2.2 trillion** between 26 April and 15 May, the Seoul Economic Daily reported ([how the omnibus account works](/article/korea-omnibus-account-foreign-retail-investors-etf-access-timeline-2026)).

How thin is the research those investors could read? We counted it in our own archive.

## What we counted

Our archive holds **1,139 single-stock reports** from **21 research houses**, naming **496 subjects**. Almost all of it (1,132 records) falls between 19 August and 26 October 2026. We matched every subject to the Korea Exchange listing of 23 October.

| | KOSPI | KOSDAQ |
|---|---|---|
| Companies listed* | 829 | 1,753 |
| Appeared in at least one report | 228 (27.5%) | 248 (14.1%) |
| Share of all 1,139 reports | 67.3% | 32.5% |
| Reports per company that appeared | 3.36 | 1.49 |

*Preferred-share lines and SPACs removed.

So **1,505 of 1,753 KOSDAQ companies (85.9%)** drew no report in our sample. On KOSPI the figure was 601 of 829 (72.5%), but the KOSPI companies that did appear hold **90.0%** of KOSPI market value. On KOSDAQ, the companies that appeared hold **38.9%**.

## Big names first, then a cliff

Attention follows size. Of KOSPI's 100 largest companies, **88** appeared. Of the 329 smallest, **6** did.

KOSDAQ falls off earlier. Only **52** of its 100 largest companies appeared. Six of its ten largest by market value drew no report in our ten weeks: EcoPro BM, Jusung Engineering, Rainbow Robotics, HLB, Eo Technics and Sam Chun Dang Pharm. Below rank 500, 87 of 1,253 KOSDAQ companies appeared.

The ten most-written-about companies, all on KOSPI, took **144 reports (12.6%)**. The top 50 took **36.3%**. At the other end, **268 companies** drew exactly one report.

## A quarter of KOSDAQ research did not come from brokers

Of the 370 KOSDAQ reports, **98 (26.5%)** came from three houses that are not brokerages: the Korea IR Council (83) and two credit-information firms (15). For **82 KOSDAQ companies**, these were the only reports in our sample. Without them, KOSDAQ's count would fall from 248 companies to 166.

That matters for the policy debate. The proposals in the news, such as stronger disclosure of intellectual property and better information on KOSDAQ technology companies, are aimed at exactly this layer.

## The language question

Every report in our archive is a Korean-language listing. We have **not measured** how many of these reports exist in English, so we make no claim about the English share. What we can say: the gap above is a gap in Korean, before any translation.

## How to read this

Our archive is a **sample** of retail-board research, collected daily from a public research listing and broker websites. A company that did not appear drew no report *in this sample*. It may well be covered by analysts whose reports we did not collect, by institutional-only research, or by research outside our window. Read the numbers as how concentrated the visible research is, not as a census of coverage.

*Statistics, not advice. Appearing or not appearing in broker research is neither a buy nor a sell signal.*
