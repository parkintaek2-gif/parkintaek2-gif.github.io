Another number was circulating on Reddit's r/korea: Korea's August exports jumped 68.7% to $98.25 billion. Following the same rule we applied to a KOSPI figure making the rounds this week, we checked it against the primary source before repeating it — in this case, the Korea Customs Service's own official release.

## The number holds up

| Metric | August 2026 | Year-on-year |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Total exports | $98.25bn | **+68.7%** |
| Semiconductor exports | $46.7bn | +209% |
| Trade surplus | $34.75bn | — |

*These are the Customs Service's own reported figures for the month, cited directly. We have not rebuilt this total from primary transaction-level data ourselves — see below for why.*

The r/korea figure matches the government release exactly. The release itself adds more: semiconductors alone were $46.7bn, up 209% year-on-year, and now the largest single contributor to the export total. The month's trade surplus, $34.75bn, was the third consecutive month above $30bn. Customs also describes August as the 15th straight month in which that calendar month set a new record — a claim we're reporting as the agency's own, not one we traced back through a full multi-year series ourselves.

## Why we didn't rebuild this number ourselves

For most of our data journalism, the point is reproducing a government aggregate from the underlying microdata so we can show our own work. We're not doing that here, and it's worth saying why: our own monthly, by-country trade dataset has a documented measurement break starting in March 2026 — average monthly export levels jump by roughly 43% at that boundary and stay elevated, in a way that doesn't match any known real-world shift. We've written about that break itself in an earlier piece. Because of it, we don't currently trust absolute-dollar totals rebuilt from that pipeline, so rather than risk repeating a broken number with false confidence, we're citing the Customs Service's own published total directly, attributed, the way any outlet would cite a single official source.

This is not investment advice, and the streak claim above is the agency's characterization, not our independent finding.

*We've written before about [why our own monthly trade totals can't be trusted at face value](/article/korea-2026-trade-surplus-data-artifact) — this piece is the practical version of that caution: cite the primary source directly rather than pass a broken pipeline's number through as our own.*
