A country's biggest customers and its biggest suppliers are usually assumed to be roughly the same places. For Korea they are not. The list of countries Korea **sells** to and the list it **buys** from overlap at the top — and then diverge sharply.

## The two maps do not match

![Grouped bar chart: Korea's share of exports (green) versus share of imports (blue) for China, the United States, Vietnam, Taiwan, Japan and Hong Kong. Hong Kong's export bar is tall and its import bar almost zero; Japan's import bar is far taller than its export bar.](/charts/korea-export-import-partners.svg)

| Partner | Share of exports | Share of imports |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| China | 19.5% | 23.5% |
| United States | 17.7% | 11.9% |
| Vietnam | 9.0% | 5.1% |
| Taiwan | 7.4% | 5.3% |
| **Japan** | **3.5%** | **7.5%** |
| **Hong Kong** | **6.7%** | **0.6%** |

Two partners tell the story. **Hong Kong** takes **6.7% of everything Korea exports** — its fifth-largest export market — but supplies just **0.6% of imports.** Korea sells there and barely buys; Hong Kong is a pass-through port more than a source of goods. **Japan** is the mirror image: it supplies **7.5% of Korea's imports**, its third-largest source, but takes only **3.5% of exports.** Korea buys far more from Japan than it sells back.

Even the giants tilt. Both maps are led by **China**, but Korea imports more from it (23.5%) than it exports to it (19.5%). The **United States** runs the other way — **17.7% of exports** against **11.9% of imports**, a market Korea sells into more than it sources from.

## Reading it honestly

This is deliberately a map of **shares, not dollars.** Korea's monthly trade totals in dollars have an unexplained jump from early 2026, so absolute amounts and a headline surplus are unreliable; shares are not, and they let the two maps sit side by side. We also leave out the very largest rank gaps — places like Bermuda and the Marshall Islands — because those are ship-registry flags of convenience, not real trade.

What is left is a clean and slightly counter-intuitive fact: **Korea's selling map and buying map are not the same map.** It sells to Hong Kong and the United States more than it buys from them, and buys from Japan more than it sells there.
