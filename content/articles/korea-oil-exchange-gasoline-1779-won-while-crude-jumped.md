International crude has been volatile this year. In mid-July the September Brent contract opened at **$78 a barrel**. By 10 September it settled at **$101.21**, and it had been above $100 since 3 September. That is a rise of about 30% in two months.

On the Korea Exchange's oil market, the gasoline price did not move.

## One number, 54 times

| Fuel | Days with an auction price, 1 Jul – 22 Oct | Price on most of those days | Days at that exact price |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gasoline | 56 | **1,779 won/litre** | **54** |
| Diesel | 55 | **1,768 won/litre** | **52** |
| Kerosene | 6 | 1,375 won/litre | 6 |

The two gasoline days that differed were both in July: 1,782.85 won on 15 July and 1,783.24 on 22 July. Since then, every competitive gasoline auction on the exchange has cleared at 1,779 won.

## The negotiated price barely moved either

The exchange also reports a negotiated-trade price. [In August we found](/article/korea-oil-exchange-two-prices) that the two prices often disagree. Over these four months, neither travelled far:

| Fuel | Negotiated price, lowest | Highest | Range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Gasoline | 1,780.00 | 1,785.83 | 0.3% |
| Diesel | 1,770.00 | 1,778.93 | 0.5% |

A 30% move in crude and a 0.3% range in exchange gasoline.

## What this does not say

This is a small wholesale venue, not the pump. Korean retail fuel prices include taxes and are set by refiners and stations, and we did not measure them. What the exchange's own file shows is that its gasoline and diesel prints are **not a reading of the oil market**: over a period when crude rose by more than $20 a barrel, they stayed within a few won. Anyone using the KRX oil price as a signal of Korean fuel costs should know that.
