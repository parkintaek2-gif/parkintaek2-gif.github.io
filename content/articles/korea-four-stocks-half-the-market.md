Korea has 2,764 companies listed on its two stock markets. Four of them are worth more than all the others combined.

![Line chart: cumulative share of total Korean market capitalisation against the number of stocks, on a log scale. Four stocks reach 52%, 57 stocks reach 80%, and all 2,764 reach 100%.](/charts/market-concentration-curve.svg)

## A few giants, a very long tail

Rank every listed issue by market value and add them up from the top. You pass **half of the entire market at the fourth name.** By the tenth you are at **60%**; by the fifty-seventh, at **80%**. The remaining **2,700-plus** companies — the vast majority of the market by count — share the last fifth between them. The median listed company is a rounding error next to the top: the largest issue is worth thousands of times the middle one.

At the very top sits one name.

![Bar chart: top 10 Korean stocks by share of total KOSPI+KOSDAQ market cap as of 2026-08-21. Samsung Electronics 26.7%, SK hynix 20.5%, then a steep drop to Samsung Elec. preferred 2.7%, SK Square 2.4% and smaller.](/charts/market-concentration-top10.svg)

**Samsung Electronics is 26.7% of the whole market** on its own — and once its preferred shares are added, the company is about **29%**. **SK hynix** is another **20.5%**. So two companies — both riding the memory and AI cycle that [tripled chip producer prices](/article/korea-producer-prices-chips-tripled) — are together nearly **half** of everything listed in Korea. After them the bars fall off a cliff: third place is under 3%.

## The two markets are not the same size at all

The lopsidedness has a second layer. Korea's main board, **KOSPI**, has 942 issues and holds **92.8%** of all market value. Its junior board, **KOSDAQ**, has almost twice as many companies — **1,822** — yet they add up to just **7.2%** of the total. KOSDAQ is where most Korean *companies* are; almost none of the *market value* is.

## What the numbers are, and are not

This is a portrait of **concentration**, and it is reported as ratios — the four-stocks-are-half figure does not depend on whether the won prices are high or low that day, only on how value is distributed across issues. Two honest caveats travel with it: preferred shares are counted as separate lines, so Samsung shows up twice, meaning its true company weight is even larger than the single 26.7% bar; and the issue count mixes common shares, preferred shares and holding companies rather than ultimate parents. As [an earlier piece](/article/kospi-is-a-semiconductor-index) put it, the KOSPI is effectively a semiconductor index — and this is the fuller picture behind that: a market where a handful of chip and industrial champions carry almost all the weight, and thousands of small companies carry almost none. This is a description of Korea's listed market on one day, **not investment advice**.
