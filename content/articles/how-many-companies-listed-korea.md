"How many companies are listed in Korea?" has a number attached to it — usually somewhere near 2,800 — and the number is not wrong so much as imprecise. It answers a different question than the one most people are asking. Here is the exact count, and why the honest answer is a range, not a figure.

## The headline count: 2,767

On 26 August 2026 the Korea Exchange's own daily files carry **2,767 stock lines**: **944 on KOSPI** and **1,823 on KOSDAQ**. That is the number to reach for if you want "how many stocks can I trade today" — every ticker, large and small, that the exchange lists.

But "stock lines" is not the same as "companies," and the gap is worth knowing.

## Subtract the preferred shares: about 2,652 companies

**115 of those 2,767 lines are preferred shares** — 109 on KOSPI, 6 on KOSDAQ. A preferred share is not a different company; it is a second class of stock in a company whose ordinary shares are already on the list. Samsung Electronics and Samsung Electronics preferred are one firm with two tickers.

Strip the preferred lines out and you are left with **2,652 ordinary stock lines** — a close approximation of the number of listed *companies*. It is not exact to the last name (a few firms carry more than one common line), but it is far closer to the real answer than 2,767 is. The preferred shares cluster almost entirely on KOSPI, which is why the blue-chip board looks larger by ticker count than it is by company count.

## And on any given day, some do not trade at all

A listing is a standing status, not a daily event. **134 of the 2,767 lines — about one in twenty — recorded zero trades on 26 August.** Many of them are the thinly held preferred lines; some are small companies that simply had no buyer and no seller that day. They are listed, and they are quoted, but on that date nothing changed hands.

So the count has three honest answers, depending on what you mean:

| The question | The number |
| --- | ---: |
| Tradable stock lines | **2,767** |
| Of those, that actually traded today | 2,633 |
| Companies (ex-preferred lines) | **~2,652** |

None of these is the "right" one. They are answers to three different questions that get asked with the same words. The number a market-cap table needs is not the number an index provider needs, and neither is the number you want if you are asking how many distinct businesses a Korean investor can actually buy a share of.

That is the whole point of counting carefully: the figure people quote is a stand-in for a question they have not quite pinned down. Pin it down and the exchange's own file will answer it — three different ways, each of them true.
