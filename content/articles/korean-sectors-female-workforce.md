Every listed company files how many of its people are women. Roll it up by sector and Korea's workforce splits along a line almost as old as its industries: the businesses that care, serve and finance are close to evenly split, and the ones that build, dig and power are not.

## Four to one

Taking the median company in each sector, the female share runs from **40% in health care** and **35% in finance** down to **13% in materials**, **14% in heavy industry** and **11% in utilities**. Top to bottom, roughly four women employed for every one — not in a single firm, but as the middle of an entire sector.

| Sector | Median female share |
| --- | ---: |
| Health care | 40% |
| Finance | 35% |
| Communication services | 35% |
| Consumer staples | 33% |
| Consumer discretionary | 30% |
| Information technology | 21% |
| Energy | 18% |
| Industrials | 14% |
| Materials | 13% |
| Utilities | 11% |

## What it tracks, and what it doesn't

The pattern is not surprising, but the size is worth a number. Pharmaceuticals, hospitals, banks and consumer businesses hire women at nearly twice the rate of shipbuilders, chemicals and power. Even information technology — half the market's value — sits at 21%, below the consumer sectors and far below health care.

One caution travels with the figure. This is who is *employed*, not who is *paid* — a sector can be 40% women and still pay them less, and that gap is a different column. Female share says where women work in Korea's listed economy. On that, the answer splits cleanly down the middle of the sector table.
