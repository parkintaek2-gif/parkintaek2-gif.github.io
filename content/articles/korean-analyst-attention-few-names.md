Korean brokerages publish a great deal of equity research. It does not spread across the market — it stacks on a short list of names, and most of the market goes unwritten-about. We keep a daily archive of single-stock broker reports, and rolled up for 2026 it draws a clear shape.

## A third drew a report; the rest drew none

Of the **2,819 listed companies** we track, **939 — 33.3% —** drew at least one single-stock report this year, out of **6,372 reports** from **20 brokerages**. The other two-thirds drew none in this archive. And "covered" is generous: the median covered company drew just **two** reports all year, **411** drew exactly one, and **459** were seen by a single brokerage.

At the other end, attention concentrates hard. The busiest single name drew **100** reports on its own — more than most whole sectors' long tails combined.

## The gap is a sector gap too

The share of a sector's listed companies drawing any report runs from **24% in financials** to **39.2% in information technology**. Real estate sits lowest at **6.7%**, on a small and holding-company-heavy base. Even the best-covered sector leaves six in ten of its listed companies unwritten-about in this sample.

## What this is, and what it is not

This measures **attention**, not coverage of the whole market, and not quality. Two honest limits sit on it. First, the raw ticker field is filled on only a fraction of reports — a gap in our scraper, not a market fact — so we join reports to companies by **name**; every uncoded report still names one stock. Second, the archive is a **sample** of brokerages' retail-board research. A company not appearing means it drew no report *in this sample* — not that no analyst covers it, and not a signal to buy or sell anything. What the numbers say plainly is narrower and still worth knowing: Korean research attention is concentrated, and most listed companies live outside it.
