There are two pay worlds inside the same Korean labour market, and the distance between them has hardly changed in fourteen years. A **regular employee** took home an average of **4.47m won a month in 2025**. A **temporary or daily worker** took home **1.74m** — about **39%** of the regular figure.

## A 40% ratio that will not move

![Line chart: temporary and daily workers' monthly pay as a share of regular workers', Korea, 2011 to 2025, hovering around 40%.](/charts/korea-temp-pay-gap.svg)

| Year | Regular pay | Temp/daily pay | Temp ÷ regular |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2011 | 2.81m won | 1.15m won | 41% |
| 2016 | 3.33m won | 1.29m won | 38.7% |
| 2020 | 3.72m won | 1.64m won | 44% |
| 2025 | **4.47m won** | **1.74m won** | **39%** |

For fourteen years the ratio has sat in a tight band — between about **39% and 44%**. It started at 41% in 2011 and ended at 39% in 2025. Both kinds of pay rose over the period, but they rose together, keeping the proportion roughly fixed. The one wobble is 2020: the ratio climbed to 44%, and not for a good reason — regular pay stalled that year while temporary pay was still rising, which narrows the gap on paper without the lower-paid group actually catching up.

## Read this number carefully

The honest caveat matters here. This is **total monthly pay, not an hourly wage.** Temporary and daily workers often work fewer hours, so part of the 40% gap is simply fewer hours on the clock, not only a lower rate per hour. A pure hourly comparison would be narrower — this data does not separate hours from rate, and we will not guess the split.

What the figure does show, cleanly, is the shape of the divide as workers actually experience it in a monthly pay packet: **a temporary or daily worker in Korea brings home roughly two-fifths of what a regular employee does, and that ratio has barely moved since 2011.** It sits alongside the other lines of the same market — pay also rises with [firm size at every step](/article/korea-bigger-firms-pay-more-every-step), and overall [real wages went flat after 2021](/article/korea-real-wages-flat-since-2021).
