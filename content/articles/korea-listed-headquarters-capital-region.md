Korea's formal economy is famously concentrated in and around Seoul, but the listed market is more concentrated still. Take the 2,364 listed companies that file a head-office location and sort them by region: **85.7%** sit in the capital region — Seoul, Gyeonggi and Incheon. The public market is, geographically, a capital-region market with a provincial minority.

## Seoul alone holds nearly half

**Seoul** is home to **1,097** listed head offices, **46.4%** of the total — almost one in two. Add **Gyeonggi** (828, **35.0%**), the ring of cities and industrial parks around the capital, and the two together hold **81.4%**. Fold in **Incheon** (100, 4.2%) and the capital region reaches **85.7%**. Everything outside it — thirteen regions covering most of the country's land and population — divides the remaining **14%**.

## The provinces, in the gaps

Below the capital the list thins fast. **Busan**, the second city, registers **83** listed head offices (3.5%). **Daejeon**, with its research institutes, holds **64** (2.7%); **Daegu 59** (2.5%); shipbuilding **Ulsan** just **32** (1.4%). These are not small economies — Ulsan builds much of what Korea exports — but their companies tend to be plants and subsidiaries of groups whose head offices, and whose listings, are registered in Seoul.

## More concentrated than the workforce

The striking part is the comparison. Our National Pension work put **61.2%** of all enrolled workers in the same capital region — itself a heavy concentration on under 12% of the country's land. Listed **head offices** run well past that, at **85.7%**. The jobs are concentrated; the head offices, and the capital-market presence that comes with them, are concentrated harder. Where a company keeps its people and where it registers its head office are not the same decision, and the second is more Seoul-bound than the first.

## What this is, and what it is not

This is a map of registered addresses, not a verdict. A provincial head office is not a weakness and a Seoul one is not a strength; this is **not investment advice**, and nothing here says a company's location predicts its returns. One limit matters most: a head office is not a footprint. A manufacturer registered in Seoul may run every factory in Ulsan or Vietnam, and this count would still place it in the capital. What the figures show is narrower and clean — that when Korean companies list, the address on the filing is a Seoul-area one far more often than the country's own distribution of work would lead you to expect.
