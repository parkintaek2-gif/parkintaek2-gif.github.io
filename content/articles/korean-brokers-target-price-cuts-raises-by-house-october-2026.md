A Korean broker report almost always says Buy ([why the Sell has vanished](/article/korean-brokers-the-vanishing-sell)). So the information is in the **target price**, and in whether the analyst moved it. Here is what each house did with its targets over one month, from the list behind our [target-change tape](/data/target-changes).

## The month in one table

The list holds **710 reports** dated 28 September to 23 October 2026: 313 on single companies, 183 on industries, 177 on the market and 37 others. Of the 313 company reports, **281** carried a target price. A change can only be counted when the list also gives a prior target: that is 222 reports.

## Three different habits

**Daishin cut.** Of the 16 targets it could compare with a prior, it cut **10**, or 63%.

**Yuanta held.** Of 45, it left **27** unchanged, or 60%, and cut only 5.

**Hanwha raised.** Of 14, it raised **9**, or 64%. Several of Hanwha's raises start from priors well below other brokers' targets for the same company ([Kia and Hyundai Mobis are two](/article/hyundai-mobis-kia-target-prices-before-q3-results-october-2026)). The list does not date a prior, so a big raise may close a gap that built up over more than one quarter.

The house habits are not a ranking. A broker that covers chemicals in a month when chemicals fell will cut more than one that covers banks. We did not adjust for coverage, and the samples are small: below 20 comparable targets for five of the ten houses that set targets.

## Two publishers that do not set targets

**Woori Bank** published 18 reports, all on foreign exchange. **Korea IR Council** published 20 company reports and gave a target on one.

## Why the denominator matters

"Analysts cut 69 targets" is true. It is 69 of **222** comparable targets, not 69 of 710 reports. Leaving out the reports with no target, or no prior, makes any share look larger than it is.

*This counts what brokers published. It is statistics about research, not a judgment of any analyst or company.*
