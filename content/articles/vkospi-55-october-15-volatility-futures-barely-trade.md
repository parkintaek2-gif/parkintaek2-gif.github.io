The **VKOSPI** measures how much movement KOSPI 200 options traders are pricing in. It is Korea's version of the VIX, often called the fear gauge.

In October it jumped.

## The gauge

| Date | VKOSPI | KOSPI that day |
|---|---|---|
| 8 October | 36.81 (low of our record) | −2.62% |
| 13 October | 48.53 | −5.46% |
| **15 October** | **55.38** (high) | **−7.57%** |
| 16 October | 48.89 | +2.05% |
| 22 October | 49.61 | −3.52% |

In five trading days the VKOSPI rose by half, to its highest level in the 34 sessions we hold. It has stayed near 50 since.

## The futures nobody trades

The Korea Exchange lists futures on the VKOSPI, so investors can trade the gauge itself. Almost nobody does.

| VKOSPI futures, 1 Sep – 22 Oct | |
|---|---|
| Busiest day | 84 contracts (8 September) |
| 15 October, the VKOSPI's high | **7 contracts** |
| Days with no trades at all | 4 |
| Open interest, highest | 43 contracts |

On the day Korea's stock market had its worst fall in our record and its fear gauge its highest reading, the market built to trade that fear changed hands **seven times**.

## What this does not say

The VKOSPI is calculated from KOSPI 200 options, which trade heavily. A thin futures market does not mean Korean investors ignore volatility; they trade it through options instead. What the futures file shows is that the dedicated product for it has, in practice, almost no market.
