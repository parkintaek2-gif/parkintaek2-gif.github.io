Korean household debt gets written about as a number that only goes up. It does: household credit, the Bank of Korea's broadest measure, has gone from **₩464.7 trillion at the end of 2002 to ₩2019.8 trillion in 2026Q2** — **4.35 times** in twenty-four years, in nominal won.

The part that is rarely counted is the other axis: **who is actually holding it.** On that, the answer is close to "the same people as before."

## Commercial banks: 47.8% then, 50.6% now

| Quarter | Total household credit | Commercial banks | Non-bank depository | Mutual savings banks |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2002Q4 | ₩464.7t | 47.8% | 11.8% | 1.5% |
| 2008Q4 | ₩723.5t | 53.7% | 17.5% | 1.0% |
| 2014Q4 | ₩1085.3t | 47.9% | 20.8% | 0.9% |
| 2017Q1 | ₩1359.0t | 45.5% | 22.0% | 1.4% |
| 2020Q4 | ₩1728.5t | 49.2% | 18.7% | 1.8% |
| 2023Q4 | ₩1882.6t | 48.7% | 16.7% | 2.1% |
| 2026Q2 | ₩2019.8t | 50.6% | 16.2% | 1.9% |

Over twenty-four years in which the total more than quadrupled, the commercial-bank share moved inside a band — **high of 57.0% in 2006Q4, low of 45.4% in 2017Q2, and 50.6% today.** Twelve points from top to bottom, across a period containing the global financial crisis, a housing boom, a pandemic and a rate cycle. Banks grew 4.61x; the whole market grew 4.35x. They roughly kept pace.

## The real swing was the non-bank depositories

This is the series worth watching. Non-bank depository institutions — the mutual credit cooperatives, community credit cooperatives and savings banks that sit outside the commercial-bank system — went from **11.8% of household credit in 2002 to a peak of 22.0% in 2017Q1**, then back down to **16.2% in 2026Q2**.

That rise and fall is the closest thing in this table to a story about regulation. It is also the piece most English-language coverage of Korean household debt leaves out entirely, because the headline number — the ₩2,000-trillion total — is easier to write about.

## Mutual savings banks: 5.67x, and still small

Mutual savings banks are the part of the Korean lending system that draws the most anxious commentary. They grew **5.67x** over the period, from ₩6.9 trillion to ₩39.3 trillion — faster than the market as a whole.

And they are still **1.9% of household credit.** Both things are true at once, and a reader deserves both. A sector can grow six-fold and remain a rounding error in the total.

## What this does not say

- These are **nominal won.** We have not adjusted for inflation, and we have not scaled to GDP or to household income. A 4.35x nominal multiple over twenty-four years is not a 4.35x real burden, and this table cannot tell you what the real one is.
- **Household credit is not mortgages.** It includes credit-card receivables alongside loans.
- The gap between the total and the depository institutions — roughly **33.1% in 2026Q2** — is insurers, card companies, pension funds and others. ECOS puts them in the total; we have not broken them out here, so we are calling it a residual rather than naming it.
- There are **no borrower counts, no rates and no delinquency by lender type** in this table. We looked.
