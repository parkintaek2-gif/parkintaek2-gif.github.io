Korea's stock market has a well-known shape: [four companies are half of all its value](/article/korea-four-stocks-half-the-market), and the junior board, KOSDAQ, is barely a rounding error in the total. But *value* and *trading* are two different maps. Look at where the money actually moved on a single day and KOSDAQ is not small at all.

![Grouped bar chart: KOSPI holds 92.8% of market value and did 84.1% of the day's trading; KOSDAQ holds 7.2% of value but did 15.9% of trading.](/charts/turnover-vs-cap-market.svg)

## The junior board punches above its weight

KOSDAQ is home to **1,822 of Korea's 2,764 listed companies** but only **7.2%** of their combined market value. On 2026-08-21, though, it accounted for **15.9%** of all trading — **more than double** its share of the market. KOSPI, the main board, is the mirror image: 92.8% of the value, but a smaller 84.1% of the trading.

Put the two on the same footing and the gap is stark. Measured as **turnover per ₩1,000 of market cap**, KOSDAQ ran at **12.3** against KOSPI's **5.1** — its shares changed hands roughly **2.4 times as fast**.

![Bar chart: daily trading value per 1,000 of market cap. KOSPI 5.06, KOSDAQ 12.31 — KOSDAQ more than double.](/charts/turnover-velocity.svg)

This is the difference between where wealth is *stored* and where it is *churned*. The big-cap board holds the value; the small-cap board does a disproportionate share of the buying and selling.

## Trading is concentrated, too — just like value

The [same two names that dominate market value](/article/korea-four-stocks-half-the-market) also dominate the tape. **Samsung Electronics was 22.4% of the entire day's trading value and SK hynix 21.6%** — together **44%** of everything traded across both markets. The top ten issues by turnover were **60.5%** of the day. So it is not only that a handful of stocks *are* the market's value; a handful of stocks are also most of its daily activity.

And at the other end, the tail goes all the way to silence: **137 of the 2,764 listed issues recorded no trades at all** that day. On the paper of the exchange they exist; in the flow of money they did not move.

## What this is, and is not

One caution travels with the trading figures that did not travel with the value ones. Market-value concentration is structural — it changes slowly. **A single day's trading share is not**: it swings with earnings, news and mood, so KOSDAQ's 16% is a snapshot, not a fixed law. The durable part is the *velocity* gap — the small-cap board consistently trades faster relative to its size, and one day shows it clearly. Every number here is a **ratio**, so none of it depends on the won price level. This is a description of where money moved in Korea's market on one day, **not investment advice**.
