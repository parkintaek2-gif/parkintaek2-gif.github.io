Every listed company in Korea reports its staff's average tenure once a year, in a legally required filing item called empSttus. We pulled it directly from all 2,924 listed companies' FY2025 annual reports and built a census — not a ranking of which industries hold workers longest, but a picture of where every company sits.

## Not an average — a distribution

![Distribution of average employee tenure across 2,839 Korean listed companies, FY2025.](/charts/tenure-census.svg)

| Average tenure | Share of 2,839 companies |
| --- | ---: |
| Under 3 years | 12.4% |
| 3–6 years | 35.3% |
| 6–10 years | 31.2% |
| 10–15 years | 15.4% |
| 15+ years | 5.7% |

*This is a statistic, not a portrait of any one workplace. Most companies cluster in the 3-to-10-year range, but the tails matter: roughly 1 in 8 companies sit under 3 years, and 1 in 17 sit at 15 years or beyond.*

## The number that summarizes it, and the number that doesn't

If you want one number: the median company in this dataset reports an average staff tenure of 6.19 years. But the single number hides real spread. Nearly half of all companies (47.7%) sit below that median band, in the under-6-year range — and within that group, 353 companies, about one in eight, average less than three years. At the other end, 162 companies keep their staff 15 years or longer on average, closer to a career than a job.

Together, the 2,839 companies in this census employ 1,872,947 people as of their FY2025 filings — this is not a small sample, it is most of Korea's listed corporate workforce.

## What this number can't tell you

This is an average tenure figure per company, not a breakdown of why people stay or leave. A company with a young workforce in a fast-growing sector will show low average tenure even with excellent retention, simply because it has not existed long enough to accumulate long-tenured staff; a company with heavy turnover among junior staff but a stable senior core can show a similar number for the opposite reason. DART's disclosure does not separate these cases, so we don't either.

This is a measure of the reported statistic, not investment or employment advice, and it is **not investment advice**.

*A related, narrower piece — [which industries hold onto workers longest, ranked by headcount-weighted average tenure](/article/korea-how-long-industries-keep-workers) — asks a different question of similar underlying data: not where each company sits, but where each industry sits.*
