Every week, the Korean outlet BigTaNews publishes an expected high and an expected low for the 15 largest KOSPI stocks by market cap, then — the following week — publishes how far off it was. Three of those scorecards are now public, back to back, for the weeks of 18 August, 24 August and 31 August. Line them up and one half of the forecast holds steady while the other keeps getting worse.

## The split

| Week | High-forecast error | Low-forecast error |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| 18 Aug | 3.74% | 2.89% |
| 24 Aug | 3.77% | 6.61% |
| 31 Aug | 2.56% | 8.10% |

The high-side error sits in a narrow band across all three weeks — never below 2.56%, never above 3.77%. The low-side error very nearly triples over the same stretch, from 2.89% up to 8.10%.

Both numbers are means of the absolute per-stock error across the same 15 names each week (Samsung Electronics, SK Hynix, SK Square, Samsung Electro-Mechanics, Hyundai Motor, LG Energy Solution, Samsung Life, Samsung C&T, Samsung Biologics, HD Hyundai Heavy Industries, SK, Doosan Enerbility, Kia, KB Financial and Hanwha Aerospace). Recomputing the average from BigTaNews' own published per-stock figures matches what they report, in all three weeks.

## Where it missed worst

The single biggest miss anywhere in the dataset is on the high side: Doosan Enerbility's forecast for the week of 24 August was off by 13.79%. But the low side produced the two next-biggest misses, both on the same stock — Samsung Electro-Mechanics missed its low forecast by 18.87% the week of 24 August, then by 16.31% the week after. SK Hynix and SK Square each missed their low forecasts by more than 17% in the week of 31 August.

Those are exceptions in degree, not in direction. In the week of 31 August, 12 of the 15 stocks' actual lows came in above the forecast low — meaning the stock didn't fall as far as BigTaNews expected. Only one came in below.

## What was happening in the market

This stretch overlapped with a KOSPI run higher — the index closed at a record 6,995 on 7 September, up 4.61% on the day. A market moving mostly one direction will tend to make "how low will it go" forecasts look too pessimistic in hindsight, and "how high will it go" forecasts easier to hit if the trend was already priced into them. That's a plausible backdrop, not a proof: this piece measures what BigTaNews published, not why its low-end forecasts undershot.

Not investment advice.
