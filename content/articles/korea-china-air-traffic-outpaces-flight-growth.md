Korea Airports Corporation's own monthly count of international arrivals shows a clear divide over the past year: air traffic from China is growing faster than the number of flights serving it, while traffic from the Middle East and Oceania is shrinking roughly in proportion to fewer flights.

## More passengers than more flights can explain

Comparing the same month — July — across three years removes the seasonal noise that a raw month-to-month read would carry (Oceania's traffic peaks in the Southern Hemisphere winter, for instance, so December and July are not comparable on their own).

| Arriving from China | Jul 2024 | Jul 2025 | Jul 2026 | Change (2yr) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Passengers | 656,191 | 788,273 | 968,990 | +47.7% |
| Flights | 4,999 | 5,606 | 6,067 | +21.4% |
| Passengers per flight | 131.3 | 140.6 | 159.7 | +21.6% |

The flight count serving the China route did grow — but passenger volume grew more than twice as fast. Each arriving flight is carrying, on average, 22% more people than it did two years ago.

Japan, the largest single source of arriving traffic, shows no equivalent gap:

| Arriving from Japan | Jul 2024 | Jul 2025 | Jul 2026 | Change (2yr) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Passengers | 1,037,581 | 1,028,634 | 1,293,316 | +24.7% |
| Flights | 5,807 | 6,533 | 7,337 | +26.3% |

Passengers and flights moved together — more flights, not fuller ones.

## Who is gaining and losing share

| Region | Jul 2024 share | Jul 2026 share |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Japan | 27.8% | 30.5% |
| China | 17.6% | 22.9% |
| Rest of Asia | 37.0% | 30.3% |

Japan and China together now account for over half of all international arriving passengers at Korean airports, up from about 45% two years earlier. That gain came largely from "rest of Asia" — a KOSIS catch-all region distinct from Japan and China — whose share fell from 37.0% to 30.3%.

## The two regions that shrank in the latest year

From July 2025 to July 2026, two of the eight KOSIS regions saw arriving traffic decline, and in both cases the flight count fell by nearly the same proportion as passenger count did — unlike China, where passengers grew faster than flights:

| Region | Passengers, Jul 2025 → Jul 2026 | Flights, same span |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Middle East | 46,924 → 36,950 (−21.2%) | 170 → 132 (−22.4%) |
| Oceania | 104,043 → 67,704 (−34.9%) | 562 → 404 (−28.1%) |

These look like fewer flights carrying proportionally fewer people, rather than the same flights running emptier or fuller.

This is a record of Korea Airports Corporation's own monthly counts, not a tourism or travel-demand forecast.
