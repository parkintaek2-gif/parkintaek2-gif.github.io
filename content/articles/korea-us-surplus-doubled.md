Korea's trade surplus is not evenly earned. Measured over the last year, most of it comes from a few partners, and the largest of them — the United States — is where the surplus has grown the fastest.

## From $23bn to $50bn in half a year

Split the year in two. In the **second half of 2025**, Korea's goods surplus with the US was about **$23.2bn**. In the **first half of 2026**, it was about **$50.0bn** — more than double.

![Bar chart of Korea's monthly goods trade surplus with the United States, USD billions: rising from about +$3.9bn in July 2025 to +$12.1bn in June 2026, a surplus every month and widening through 2026.](/charts/korea-us-trade-balance.svg)

The monthly figure tells the same story with more resolution: **+$3.9bn in July 2025**, climbing to **+$12.1bn by June 2026**. There is no month in the run that is not a surplus. The line only goes one way.

## Exports did it, not a drop in imports

The surplus widened because Korea sold more, not because it bought less. Monthly **exports to the US rose 94%** over the year — from **$10.3bn** to **$20.0bn**. Imports rose too, but only **23%**, from $6.4bn to $7.9bn. When sales nearly double and purchases barely move, the gap between them — the surplus — has to widen.

## Where it sits in the whole picture

The US is Korea's [largest two-way partner](/article/korea-trade-partners-two-giants-one-supplier), about $235bn a year, so this is not a small-country blip; it is the single biggest source of Korea's overall surplus. It also rhymes with what happened further east: over the same months, Korea's balance with China [flipped from deficit to surplus](/article/korea-china-trade-balance-flipped), and for the same reason — exports. Two of Korea's three biggest markets pulled in the same direction at once.
