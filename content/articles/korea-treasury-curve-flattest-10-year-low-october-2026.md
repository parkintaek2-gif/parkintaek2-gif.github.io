Korea's benchmark **10-year treasury yield closed at 4.154%** on 22 October, the lowest close in the 49 trading days we have collected since 10 August. It was 4.599% on 15 September. In five weeks it has fallen **44.5 basis points**.

The short end has not followed it down as far. That has flattened the curve.

## Three maturities, three dates

| | 10 Aug | High | 22 Oct |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-year | 3.780% | 4.122% (28 Sep) | 3.840% |
| 10-year | 4.241% | 4.599% (15 Sep) | **4.154%** |
| 30-year | 4.585% | 4.751% (18 Aug) | **4.242%** |

The 3-year yield is **higher** now than in early August. The 10-year and 30-year are lower. Over the whole window, the long end fell and the short end rose.

## The gap has nearly halved

| Spread | 10 Aug | Widest | 22 Oct |
|---|---|---|---|
| 10-year minus 3-year | 46.1bp | 53.8bp (19 Aug) | **31.4bp** |
| 30-year minus 3-year | 80.5bp | 91.7bp (19 Aug) | **40.2bp** |

In mid-August an investor got 92 basis points more a year for lending to the Korean government for 30 years rather than 3. Now it is 40. [In August we drew the whole curve for one day](/article/korea-government-bond-yield-curve); the same curve today is much flatter.

## The same weeks in stocks

The long-bond rally lines up with the stock slide. From 15 September, the day of the 10-year high, to 22 October, [the KOSPI fell from 6,627 to 5,534](/article/kospi-up-four-in-five-stocks-fall-breadth-october-2026). Falling long yields alongside falling stocks is the pattern of money moving toward safer assets, but these figures do not show who bought, and we have not tested the cause.

## What this does not say

A flatter curve is often read as a sign that markets expect slower growth or lower policy rates later. That is a reading, not a measurement. What the closes show is how much the long end has fallen relative to the short end, and that the 10-year is lower than on any day since 10 August, when our record starts.
