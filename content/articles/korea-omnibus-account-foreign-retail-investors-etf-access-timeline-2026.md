Foreign investors' selling has been blamed for Korea's recent market falls, and Korean press this week asked what would bring foreign money back. One answer keeps coming up: make it easier for individuals abroad to buy Korean shares. The tool for that is the **omnibus account**. Here is what it is and when each step happened, from regulators' and newspapers' published records.

## What an omnibus account is

Normally a foreign investor needs an account with a Korean broker and an investor registration. In an omnibus account, an **overseas broker** holds one account in Korea and trades for all its clients through it. The end investor uses the account they already have at home.

The system dates from **2017**. It was little used: the rules limited who could open such accounts. A guideline issued on **27 November 2025** by the Financial Services Commission, the Financial Supervisory Service, the Korea Financial Investment Association and the Korea Securities Depository set out how it works. Among the duties it lists: the overseas broker keeps records of each final investor's trades for **10 years** and reports to the Korean broker every month.

## Timeline

| Date | Step | Source |
|---|---|---|
| 2017 | Omnibus account system introduced | FSC |
| Apr 2025 | Sandbox designation lets small and mid-sized overseas brokers open accounts | FSC |
| Aug 2025 | First account: Hana Securities with Hong Kong's Emperor Securities | FSC |
| 27 Nov 2025 | Usage guideline issued; rule to lift opener limits targeted for 2 Jan 2026 | FSC |
| 28 Apr–7 May 2026 | Interactive Brokers starts through Samsung Securities (dates differ by report) | SE Daily, EBN |
| 21 May 2026 | FSC chairman says ETFs will be added | JoongAng Daily |
| 9 Sep 2026 | Amended rule notified | EBN |
| 15 Sep 2026 | ETFs and ETNs tradable via omnibus accounts; leveraged and inverse excluded | EBN |
| 1 Jan 2027 | Withholding-tax rule applies; industry expects full ETF trading | Money Today |

Two cautions. A rule being in force is not the same as every investor being able to trade: EBN noted that each Korean and overseas broker pair must change its contracts and systems first. And a Korean newspaper this week dated the guideline to April 2026; the guideline we could find is November 2025. We list that as unverified.

## How much has come in

Through omnibus accounts, foreign investors traded **5.8 trillion won** and net bought **2.2 trillion won**, according to the Seoul Economic Daily (period 26 April to 15 May 2026). Money Today later reported the same totals for 6 February to 15 May. We show both dates because the reports differ.

For scale, net assets of Korean-listed ETFs were **519.7 trillion won** on 25 June 2026 (Hankyung Magazine, citing the Korea Exchange). Until 15 September, none of that ETF market was open to omnibus accounts.

## Who already holds Korean securities

Our [foreign holdings page](/data/foreign-holdings) adds up what other countries report holding. At the end of 2024, 75 reporting economies held **636.2 billion US dollars** of Korean securities. The **United States** held 195.4 billion, **Singapore** 73.9 billion, and **Hong Kong** 39.0 billion, up from 31.2 billion a year earlier. Taiwan, often named as a source of retail demand for Korean chip stocks, does not report to the IMF, so its holdings are **not measured** here.

## Access is two things

Trading access is one half. The other is information, and it is still mostly in Korean. In our archive of Korean broker research, **86%** of KOSDAQ companies drew no report in ten weeks ([the research gap, counted](/article/korean-broker-research-gap-kosdaq-companies-foreign-investors-2026)). Opening an account does not translate a report.
