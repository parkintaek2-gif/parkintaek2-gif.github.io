A post on an Inven forum thread pointed our community desk to Korean press reports that Korea Electric Power Corp. (KEPCO) had asked Samsung Electronics and SK Hynix to prepay a combined ₩25 trillion in future electricity bills. We checked how much of that is confirmed versus reported.

## What's confirmed and what isn't

| Claim | Status |
| --- | --- |
| A prepayment proposal exists | **Confirmed** — KEPCO spokesperson on record |
| Samsung Electronics ₩20tn / SK Hynix ₩5tn | Reported by two outlets, not confirmed by KEPCO |
| ~5 years of each firm's 2025 electricity bill | Reported framing, not a KEPCO figure |
| Interest paid as a bill credit, rate between bond yields | Reported as "under discussion" |
| Either company's agreement to participate | Not found in any source we reviewed |

*KEPCO's own confirmation, per The CEN News, is that "participation, the interest rate, the prepayment size and the duration are not yet finalized" — a narrower statement than the widely repeated ₩25tn headline number.*

## Why prepayment, and why now

The reported purpose — funding a large power-grid buildout for the Yongin and Honam semiconductor clusters — points to a wider, familiar story: Korea's AI-driven chip investment boom needs grid capacity years before it needs to be paid for in the ordinary way, and KEPCO is a rate-regulated utility that can't simply raise prices on two customers to cover it. A prepayment structure — cash now, bill credits later — is one way to borrow against future demand without issuing public debt or waiting on a rate case. Whether this specific structure is the one that ships is still open; what's notable is that a state utility is asking two private chipmakers to help fund grid infrastructure at all.
