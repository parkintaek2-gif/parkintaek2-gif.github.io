Korea's trade has a shape as lopsided as [its stock market](/article/korea-four-stocks-half-the-market): a handful of partners account for almost all of it. In June 2026, **four countries were half of everything Korea exported, and four were half of everything it imported.** But the two halves are not the same shape — and that asymmetry is the story.

![Paired bar chart: Korea's top trading partners by share of exports (blue) versus share of imports (orange), June 2026. China imports 24.9% dominates; US and China tie near 19.6% of exports; Hong Kong 8.9% exports but 0.3% imports; Japan 7.6% imports but 2.8% exports.](/charts/trade-partner-shares.svg)

## Two destinations, one source

On the selling side, Korea leans on **two giants of almost identical size**: the **United States at 19.6%** of exports and **China at 19.6%** — nearly tied, and together **39.2%** of the total. No third partner comes close; the next, Hong Kong, is under 9%.

On the buying side the picture tilts hard toward a single country. **China alone supplied 24.9% of Korea's imports** — a bigger slice than *any* export destination takes. The United States, Korea's co-leading customer, is only its second-largest supplier at 12.0%. So Korea sells to a balanced pair but buys, more than anything, from one place.

## The tells hiding in the smaller bars

Two partners sit on opposite ends of a seesaw. **Hong Kong is 8.9% of exports but just 0.3% of imports** — the signature of an *entrepôt*, a waypoint where Korean goods are booked as they head onward, mostly into mainland China. Read that way, China's real pull on Korean output is larger than its direct 19.6% suggests. **Japan is the mirror image: 7.6% of imports but only 2.8% of exports** — still a supplier of parts and materials, no longer a major customer.

## Concentrated either way

Stack the partners up from the largest and both curves climb steeply. **Four partners reach half** of the total on each side; the **top ten are 77% of exports and 72% of imports.** The remaining 200-plus countries split what is left.

![Line chart: cumulative share of Korea's trade against number of partner countries on a log scale, exports and imports. Both reach 50% at four partners and about 77%/72% at ten.](/charts/trade-concentration-curve.svg)

## What these numbers are, and are not

Everything here is a **share** — the fraction of the basket each partner holds — and deliberately so. The underlying table's monthly dollar totals contain a scale break from March 2026, so the absolute figures are not trustworthy and are not used; the *shares*, by contrast, barely move across that break (China's export share reads 18.9%, 19.1% and 19.6% in February, March and June), which is why they can be reported with confidence. This is a description of *where* Korea's trade goes and comes from in one month, in relative terms — **not investment advice**.
