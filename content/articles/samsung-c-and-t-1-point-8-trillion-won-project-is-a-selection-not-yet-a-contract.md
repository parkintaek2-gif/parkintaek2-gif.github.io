Samsung C&T (KOSPI: 028260) filed a disclosure with Korea's Financial Supervisory Service on 21
September, 2026, saying its general assembly selection as builder for a Seoul redevelopment project
carries an expected value of roughly 1.83 trillion won &mdash; 4.49% of the company's most recent
annual revenue. The filing itself, though, is explicit that this number is not final and that no
contract has been signed yet.

## What the filing actually says

| Field | Value |
|---|---|
| Project | Seongsu Strategic Redevelopment Zone No. 3, Seoul |
| Selection date (cooperative general assembly) | 19 September 2026 |
| Expected construction amount | &#8361;1,827,500,000,000 (excl. VAT) |
| Share of FY2025 consolidated revenue | 4.49% |
| Contract signed? | Not yet, per the filing |

We checked the revenue figure independently against DART's own financial-statement API: Samsung
C&T's FY2025 (fiscal year 62) consolidated revenue was 40,742,240,967,149 won. Dividing the
expected project amount by that figure gives 4.49% &mdash; an exact match to the filing's stated
ratio.

## Selection is not a signature

Korean disclosure rules require companies to report a "material management matter" like this one
as soon as a general assembly names a builder, well before a formal construction contract is
negotiated and signed. The filing spells out the gap in its own text: the stated amount excludes
value-added tax, it "may change," and Samsung C&T says it will disclose the confirmed contract
separately once one is actually signed.

That is a different category of disclosure from a signed construction contract with fixed start and
end dates, and it is worth naming the difference plainly: today's filing tells readers a company was
picked, and roughly how large the eventual job might be, not that a binding agreement now exists at
that price. Both kinds of filings are routine and neither is a red flag by itself -- the distinction
matters only for anyone reading the headline figure as if it were already contracted.
