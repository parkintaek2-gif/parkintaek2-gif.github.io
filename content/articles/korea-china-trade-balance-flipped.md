Ask whether Korea runs a surplus or a deficit with China and the honest annual answer is: neither, really. Over the last twelve months the two [nearly cancel](/article/korea-trade-surplus-not-china) — a rounding error on about $330bn of two-way trade. But the annual number hides a turn that only shows up month by month.

## Deficit in 2025, surplus all through 2026

Split the year in half and the direction is clear. Across the **second half of 2025**, Korea ran a **net deficit of about $4.3bn** with China. Across the **first half of 2026**, it ran a **net surplus of about $13.5bn**.

![Bar chart of Korea's monthly goods trade balance with China, USD billions: deficits of −$1.4bn, −$0.6bn and −$1.8bn in mid-2025 turning to surpluses that widen from +$0.3bn in January 2026 to +$3.6bn in June 2026.](/charts/korea-china-trade-balance.svg)

Every month of 2026 was a surplus, and it grew — from **+$0.3bn in January to +$3.6bn in June**. The three deepest deficit months on the chart (−$1.4bn, −$0.6bn, −$1.8bn) are all back in 2025. The line did not wobble across zero; it crossed and kept going.

## It is an export story

The balance turned because exports moved, not because imports fell. Korea's monthly **exports to China rose 81%** over the year — from **$11.0bn** in July 2025 to **$20.0bn** in June 2026. Imports also rose, but by far less: **32%**, from $12.4bn to $16.4bn. Selling grew more than two and a half times as fast as buying, and that gap is the surplus.

## Why the annual number misses it

A yearly total is the sum of a deficit half and a surplus half, and here the two roughly offset — which is why "$330bn of trade, roughly balanced" is true and yet tells you nothing about where things are heading. The [monthly country-by-country data](/data/korea-trade-balance.csv), free to reproduce with credit, is where the direction lives. Korea's trade with its largest partner did not stay flat this year. It flipped.
