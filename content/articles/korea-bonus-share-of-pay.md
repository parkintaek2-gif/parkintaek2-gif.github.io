A Korean worker's monthly pay slip is not the same size every month, and it is mostly one line that does it. Beyond the fixed salary sits **special pay** — bonuses and one-off payments — and it is both a large share of the year and a very uneven one.

## About an eighth of pay, and slowly shrinking

Across all industries, special payments have made up **between roughly 12% and 15% of a regular employee's total earnings** every year since 2011. The share has drifted down — **15.3% in 2011 to 12.6% in 2025** — but the headline is how big it is at all: something like one part in eight of Korean pay is not the monthly salary.

| Year | Bonus share of pay |
| --- | ---: |
| 2011 | 15.3% |
| 2015 | 13.8% |
| 2020 | 11.9% |
| 2025 | **12.6%** |

## It arrives in a few months, not twelve

![Bar chart: the average share of a year's total special pay that falls in each month, Korea. Tall bars in January, December and September; short bars in May and November.](/charts/korea-bonus-months.svg)

The bigger story is timing. Averaged over full years, the year's special pay is not spread evenly — it clusters:

| Month | Share of the year's bonus |
| --- | ---: |
| January | 14.8% |
| December | 14.4% |
| September | 13.0% |
| February | 10.4% |
| … | … |
| May / November | ~4.1% each |

**December and January together hold 29% of the whole year's bonus.** Add September and February and four months hold about **half** of it. The leanest months, May and November, hold about 4% each. So a worker's December or January pay can tower over their May pay without their salary changing at all.

## Why this matters for reading any monthly wage number

This is the mechanism behind a warning we have made before: [Korea's monthly real-wage figures swing hard](/article/korea-real-wages-flat-since-2021), and not because pay is really lurching around. The fixed-salary part barely moves month to month. Special pay, three to four times larger in its peak months than in its troughs, is what makes a single month jump — so a one-month figure, or a one-month year-on-year change, is mostly telling you whether a bonus happened to land inside it.

The honest reading of a Korean pay number, then, is an annual one: **bonuses are about an eighth of the total, they are shrinking slowly, and they arrive in a handful of months** — which is exactly why the annual average, not the monthly headline, is the figure to trust.
