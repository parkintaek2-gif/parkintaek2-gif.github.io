The Bank of Korea publishes a basic exchange rate for the dollar every trading day. On **7 September 2026** it closed at **1,355.2 won**. Scan back through every one of the 400-plus daily prints since the start of 2025 and only one day was lower: **2 July 2025, at 1,352.6** — 2.6 won below today, and otherwise indistinguishable from it.

In between, the won took a round trip that erased itself.

## The trip

| Date | Rate | What it is |
| --- | ---: | --- |
| 2 July 2025 | 1,352.6 | 20-month low |
| 2 July 2026 | 1,554.4 | 20-month high |
| 7 September 2026 | 1,355.2 | Back to a 14-month low |

From the July 2025 low to the July 2026 high, the dollar cost **14.9% more** won. From that high to today, it cost **12.8% less**. The climb took a year. The round trip back took **67 calendar days**.

## The last nine weeks, day by day

The decline from the peak was not a single move — it built through August and accelerated into September:

| Date | Rate |
| --- | ---: |
| 3 August 2026 | 1,433.6 |
| 10 August | 1,420.1 |
| 19 August | 1,411.0 |
| 24 August | 1,383.6 |
| 31 August | 1,376.5 |
| 4 September | 1,359.5 |
| 7 September | 1,355.2 |

From 3 August to 7 September — 35 days — the rate fell **78.4 won**, or **5.5%**. The pace has not slowed on the way down; the last four trading days alone (2–7 September) covered 15.1 won of the move.

## Why this matters to exporters, not travelers

A won that buys more dollars is good news for anyone converting dollars to spend at home. It is the opposite for a company that earns dollars and reports in won: the same $100 sale now converts to roughly 12.8% fewer won than it did in early July. For an economy where exports are a large share of output, a move of this size over nine weeks changes what a quarter's dollar revenue is worth before a single unit sold or bought changes.

This is a settlement-rate series, not a trading signal, and it says nothing about why the rate moved or where it goes next. What it says, with 410 days of official data behind it, is narrower and harder to dispute: as of 7 September, the won is trading at levels last seen 14 months ago, and it got there by giving back an entire year's weakening in about two months.

Not investment advice.
