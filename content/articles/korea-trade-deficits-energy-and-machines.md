We have shown where Korea's trade surplus comes from: it [more than doubled with the US](/article/korea-us-surplus-doubled) and [flipped to a surplus with China](/article/korea-china-trade-balance-flipped) in the first half of 2026. But a country's trade balance is two-sided. Korea earns from a few partners and pays out to a different set — and the paying-out side has an even simpler shape.

## Nine of the ten are energy or machines

In the first half of 2026, Korea's largest deficits were:

- **Saudi Arabia −$11.6bn** · **Japan −$10.7bn** · **UAE −$6.1bn** · **Germany −$6.0bn** · **Australia −$5.4bn**
- then Chile −$2.6bn, Algeria −$2.4bn, Iraq −$2.4bn, France −$2.3bn.

![Bar chart of Korea's ten largest trade-deficit partners in the first half of 2026, in USD billions. Saudi Arabia (−11.6), UAE, Australia, Algeria and Iraq are marked as energy; Japan (−10.7), Germany, Chile and France are the non-energy deficits.](/charts/korea-trade-deficit-partners.svg)

Group them and the list is almost entirely two things. The **energy exporters** — Saudi Arabia, the UAE, Australia, Algeria, Iraq, Qatar, Oman, Kuwait — are oil, gas and coal. The only large deficits that are *not* energy are **Japan and Germany**, which sell Korea machinery, parts and equipment, and **Chile**, which sells copper. Oil, gas, machines, metal. That is the import bill.

## Why it looks like this

Korea makes very little of its own oil or gas, so energy has to be bought abroad — and it is bought from whoever has it, which is why the deficit league table reads like a map of the world's oil and gas fields. The machinery deficits with Japan and Germany are the other structural piece: some high-end capital equipment is still imported rather than made at home.

Put the two sides together and the shape of Korean trade is clear. It **sells** finished goods — chips, cars, ships — to the [US and China](/article/korea-us-surplus-doubled), and **buys** raw energy and a few machines from everyone else. The surplus is manufacturing; the deficit is the fuel and tools that manufacturing runs on. The [monthly country data](/data/korea-trade-balance.csv) is free to reproduce with credit.

Not investment advice — monthly customs values from official Korean data, every figure dated.
